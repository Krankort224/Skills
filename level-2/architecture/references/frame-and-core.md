# Frame action, component coordination and vertical cores

Select the affected question for a frame layout, coordination grid or vertical
core. Develop gravity/lateral roles, physical dimensions and equipment/route
interfaces together. These are architectural mechanisms and reservations, not
member sizing, a completed stair/lift design, a product specification or code
classification. Use actual project requirements and supplier/discipline evidence.

## Choose frame and stability action with the plan

On aligned plans identify gravity-bearing members, nonbearing enclosure/infill,
and the proposed resisting planes in each horizontal direction. Show their
connections to floors/roof and ground. A material name, photograph, stepped or
broken-axis outline does not establish frame action. A structural frame, a
cladding frame and a visual facade grid are different objects.

| Proposed action | Architectural opportunity | Consequence to retain in plan and section |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Moment-resisting frame in a stated direction | Can keep that direction's bays free of diagonal/wall interruptions. | Actual joint/column/beam/base action and depth must be developed. “Beam on column” is not automatically a rigid joint; the perpendicular direction still needs its own system. Check door/stair/service envelopes at headers and joints. |
| Gravity frame with braced/wall/core resistance | Can separate many room partitions from the gravity grid while concentrating stability at selected edges or service zones. | Preserve the resisting planes, their openings/bases and floor connections. Doorways, a ground lobby or future tenancy cannot silently remove their role. A shaft contributes only through its actual developed enclosure and connections. |
| Mixed directional frame and braced/wall system | Can release one important frontage or route while using less open edges in the other direction. | State which system works in each direction. A diagonal suitable in an opaque end bay may block the only approach when that bay becomes a doorway. Torsion, stiffness compatibility and actual forces remain engineering questions. |
| Bearing-wall/frame or other combined organization | Can align cellular accommodation with bearing walls and use a frame for an open neighboring zone. | Trace the interface and any transfer, settlement/movement or different floor support. A combined-system name does not license an unsupported wall or an arbitrary change of support between floors. |

Compare longitudinal, transverse, crossed and beamless floor arrangements against
the actual room and core openings. Moving beam direction relocates reactions,
downstands, edge support and service crossings; it does not simply remove them.
A nominally flat soffit still has local support/connection zones and opening
restrictions. For a stair/shaft void develop the
[opening's real support](wall-floor-and-opening.md#place-a-floor-opening-without-erasing-its-support).

At an open ground floor, overlay the upper supports/resisting planes. Retain a
continuous recipient, move the entrance beside it, revise the upper arrangement,
or reserve a designed transfer/system change and recount its depth, supports and
ground consequences. Do not draw the upper core on a floor slab as if the word
“core” supplies the missing base. An entrance opening within a developed wall
is different from deleting the whole wall at ground. Compare a continuous-core
concept with directional frames/braced bays when the core doors cannot coexist
with its proposed structural role; neither alternative is capacity-approved.

Keep erection-stage support and connection access distinct from the final
system. An assembly photograph shows an arrangement at that instant, not a
safe temporary-works sequence. When a rigid joint or prefabricated connection
gets larger, recheck the occupied clear field, facade reveal, landing head and
service path before retaining the same room/door claims.

## Separate coordination pitch, component and occupied clearance

Record the convention before using a dimension: coordination axes/nominal
period, actual component outline, installation interval/tolerance, support/bearing
faces, finishes and occupied clear faces. A repeated grid line is not a clear
room boundary, a fabricated member length or evidence of available bearing.
A product's geometry alone also does not identify its material, performance,
connections or current availability.

For an **original trial convention**, take three coordination periods of6.00m.
Within each period reserve0.01m at each end and a5.98m panel between them.
Then `3×6.00=3×5.98+3×0.02=18.00m`: component lengths total17.94 and assigned
intervals0.06. At an internal boundary the neighboring half-intervals meet;
the end convention is explicit. None of these numbers is a prescribed joint,
fabrication tolerance or occupied clear width. A different system's bearings,
overlaps or connection geometry can require a different convention.

If a proposed replacement component is5.99 while each period still needs0.02
interval under this convention, each needs6.01 and three need18.03. A fixed18.00
boundary is incompatible by0.03. Compare a different component, an authorized
grid change or an actually justified joint/tolerance arrangement; do not silently
compress the joint. Check end/corner closures and real assembly, not just the
sum of interior modules.

Separately, a6.00m axis spacing with0.30m-wide supports centered on both axes
gives5.70m between support faces before finishes or projections. It says nothing
about a spanning component's cut/bearing length. Reconcile the resulting finished
clear room, door/landing approach, facade module and actual product dimensions.
Carry a late grid/component change into stair openings, shaft door positions,
panel edges and service penetrations; do not preserve an obsolete room area by
renaming its dimensional basis. Use [area accounting](program/area-ledger.md#how-can-areas-be-counted-once-and-compared)
for the area definition and double-counting rules.

## Fit vertical equipment and maintenance with the core

Reserve separate envelopes for the cabin, shaft/equipment, shaft enclosure and
support, landing-door assembly/rough opening, approach/waiting, pit, overhead,
machines/control equipment and installation/replacement access. A cabin layout
cannot determine the whole core footprint. Shaft outer dimensions cannot
establish cabin capacity or an accessible door/landing. Keep the supplier's
dimensional datum and what each envelope includes explicit.

Read a proposed equipment profile from the lowest served floor through every
landing to the top. Show pit bottom, upper landing datum, lowest overhead
obstruction, structural/weather closure and any machinery space beyond the
shaft. Reconcile these with foundations, floor openings, roof cap and maintenance
routes. If only a car plan is supplied, develop alternatives and reserve the
unresolved envelope rather than importing generic pit/head values.

Compare machinery/support arrangements by the actual product, not a universal
ranking. Overhead machinery can consume a roof volume, need a safe service
approach and require support/handling space that is absent from the passenger
plan. A lower or separate machinery arrangement can consume basement/ground
space, introduce connections and change maintenance/foundation/water interfaces;
it does not automatically eliminate shaft overhead. A machine-room-less label
does not remove all equipment, access or handling obligations. Do not transfer
a historical machine-room section's dimensions, slab recipe, hatch size or old
standard references to a new installation.

Draw a normal passenger journey and a separate maintenance/replacement journey.
Check who can open which threshold, where a technician works and how a component
reaches the machine/support location without using a passenger queue as its
permanent work zone. A hatch/handling beam in a reference is a prompt to reserve
that operation; its capacity and safe access need actual design. Do not place
machinery loads on a slab merely because a room rectangle fits.

For a bounded equipment change, recalculate plan and the whole vertical profile.
A larger shaft can consume waiting or the independent street corridor even if
the car fits. Extra head space may break the roof cap; extra pit may re-open the
[ground/neighbor question](ground-and-basement.md#map-neighboring-ground-and-construction-dependencies).
Choose between relocation, a different actually supported product/envelope,
an authorized building change or a visible incompatibility. A plan-area gain
cannot compensate for a hard section failure.

## Carry core routes and support through every level

Overlay street/ground, repeated floor, any lower floor and roof/top equipment
plans, with a section through landings and the core's most demanding support.
Keep stair, lift, waiting, doors, services and structure as distinguishable
reservations where their boundaries differ. Trace both the ordinary journey
and the separately selected evacuation proposal; an ordinary lift is not made
an evacuation route by its inclusion in a step-free journey.

For independent opening hours, connect the street directly to a controlled
shared core/landing without requiring movement through the closed tenancy.
At upper arrival identify the actual shared zone, doors and private boundary.
Do not claim separate access while a ground commercial queue or machine-room
service door occupies the only clear approach. Keep waiting/rescue and through
movement distinguishable until actual project rules/operations justify their
overlap. Protection, separation, number/capacity of lifts/stairs and accessible
clearances are applicability questions owned by [requirements](requirements.md),
not deductions from an educational floor-count or lift-type diagram.

Where the stair and lift share a lobby, show each door state and the path past
it; changing shaft proportion or door side can alter the whole landing. Carry
stair floors/openings/upper arrival into the
[walking-profile check](stair-section.md#build-and-test-the-walking-profile).
A stair block is not a completed usable stair. At the ground discharge and top
equipment level, preserve both support continuity and the actual route; neither
an external stair nor a roof machine room is resolved by adding its symbol.

This original2D block/section study uses declared trial inputs; it is not a
completed stair, supplier installation or engineered frame. Shell9.60×9.00m,
FF0/+3.20, roof cap+6.50. Keep an activity field x=0.35…5.50,y=3.25…8.65 and
an east core/support band x=5.80…9.60. The front support zone is x=0…5.80,
y=0…3.00. Wall/column/route envelopes below are explicit study reservations;
other room partitions, stairs, finishes and capacity are not fully developed.

**C0 — expose three independent conflicts.** A column at(3.30,6.00) obstructs
the activity field. An upper core drawn above an open ground lobby has no
developed support/stability recipient. With0.15 floor build-up above structure,
0.45 structure and0.25 separate crossing services below it, activity clearance
is `3.20−0.15−0.45−0.25=2.35`, failing its supplied2.55 trial. Calling every
connection rigid or overlapping all depths on paper repairs none of these.

**C1 — change actual support and section.** Compare a gravity frame with a
continuous developed east core against directional frames plus braced/wall bays
on permitted edges. The first is conditionally selected to keep an independent
street-to-core entrance beside retained resisting walls, not through their
deleted bases. Reserve0.30-square posts centered at x=0.20/5.65 and
y=3.05/8.80. Their outlines stay outside the fixed activity rectangle; proposed
center spans are5.45 and5.75m. Floors/headers must frame around the actual core
and stair holes, with the original interior support replaced by a real engineered
proposal. The coordinates establish spatial fit, not span capacity or core
stiffness. If openings cannot coexist with the continuous-core concept, reopen
the directional-frame alternative and its joint/bracing envelopes.

Put the0.25 main service crossing over the front support band, whose supplied
clear trial is2.30; the2.35 result fits there. Keep the activity field free of
that crossing, giving2.60 before additional obstructions. Locate branches with
actual beam direction and permitted routes; this is not permission to cut a

fail2.55, so its structural reservation must be confirmed or the proposal revised.

**C2 — fit the equipment profile and its ground cost.** Trial equipment A needs

Use B conditionally. Its shaft outer box is x=6.00…8.20,y=0.40…2.80, with a
south landing/approach reservation x=6.00…8.20,y=2.80…4.00. Keep a separate
1.20m clear study corridor x=8.20…9.40,y=0.20…8.80, inside a0.20m east-wall
reservation. The shaft's south door faces the landing, which connects sideways
to the corridor without requiring a passenger to cross the shaft. Provide the
independent street threshold into that corridor. Its width is a study input,
not a verified accessible or evacuation minimum.

Reserve a stair block x=5.80…8.20,y=4.20…8.70 with landing doors toward the
corridor on both occupied floors; its actual flights, guards, door states and
walking/headroom profile still require the stair method. This2.40×4.50 block is

or equipment space.

At ground reserve the separate machine envelope x=2.80…4.80,y=0.40…2.20,

still needs public lobby/doors and actual machine operation/removal space;

plant support/handling out of the shaft diagram exposes this real area cost.

**C3 — propagate a changed request.** Increasing both shaft dimensions by0.20
while holding its west/north reference faces gives x=6.00…8.40,y=0.40…3.00.
It intrudes0.20 into the fixed corridor x=8.20…9.40 and0.20 into the former

and consumes its former separation; wall/door thickness or opening operation may
make the arrangement incompatible. Either develop those interfaces with actual
allowances or choose a different shaft/core layout; this is not accepted merely
because the block rectangles touch.

An additional0.20 head requirement raises B's top to6.60, failing the unchanged

The activity field, street threshold and FF levels do not silently move. This
separates an initial block reservation requiring the stated stair, door, equipment
and structural checks from unresolved changed geometry and actual acceptance.

## Source boundary

The ASK01 coordination diagrams, ASK04 directional frame/wall/bracing figures
and ASK06 core/equipment figures are mapped in
[sources](sources.md#ground-frame-and-core-selections). Procedures, alternatives,
numeric trials and worked revisions here are original synthesis. A photograph
does not establish joint rigidity; ASK04's facade-wall support figures are not
core details. Historical upper-machine drawings do not provide current pit/head,
slab, access, protection or product specifications. Source classifications and
floor-count examples do not override the shared jurisdiction, engineering,
modeling or user-deliverable contracts.
