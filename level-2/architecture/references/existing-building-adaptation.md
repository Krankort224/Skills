# Existing buildings and constructive adaptation

Use this reference for a bounded change to an existing building: a different
use, a retained support with a new opening, a repaired enclosure or a replacement
part meeting old work. Begin with the accepted extent of intervention and the
evidence available. An existing plan records an arrangement; it does not certify
present condition, load capacity or permission to alter it. A retained component
also needs a reason to remain suitable for the changed use.

These methods develop architectural choices, interfaces and conditional
reservations. They do not diagnose concealed defects, design strengthening or
temporary works, prescribe hazardous-material removal, or certify occupation.
Apply [requirements](requirements.md) to the actual jurisdiction, work category
and heritage status. Historical foreign classifications, service-life tables and
permission procedures do not establish the project's legal position.

## Turn partial survey into conditional commitments

**Question and inputs.** What can be decided now, and which commitment would
conceal an unverified condition? Gather available measured geometry and datum,
dated photographs, accessible observations, room/component identifiers, historic
drawings and alteration records, current occupation, the proposed intervention
and its precision needs. Identify inaccessible areas and who may authorize access
or investigation. A historic drawing and a recent photograph answer different
questions; neither automatically prevails over a relevant measured finding.

**Mechanism.** Existing buildings can depart from their drawings through
deformation, replacement, undocumented openings and changes of use. Measurement
precision, representation precision, material identification and condition are
separate uncertainties. A precisely drawn wall may have an unknown support role;
one inspected end of a beam does not establish the condition of all its bearings.
Tie the strength of a commitment to the evidence needed to make that commitment,
rather than give the whole building one confidence label.

1. Locate each affected item on a common plan/section and datum. Distinguish
   **observed/measured**, **reported/documented**, **inferred** and **unknown**
   information, with date, accessible extent and source. Preserve discrepancies:
   an archived straight wall and a measured skew are two records until resolved.
   Mark dimensions to visible faces separately from inferred concealed build-ups.
2. Translate the proposed use into its changed demands: occupation and hours,
   loads/vibration, moisture/temperature, separation, access, servicing and
   maintenance. Ask which existing component or interface each demand touches.
   Use the established [program methods](program-and-space.md), rather than
   treating available floor area as evidence of suitability.
3. For each consequential unknown, write a **decision → missing evidence →
   investigation → possible revision** chain. Ask a measured survey for the
   geometry actually needed, an appropriate specialist for condition/capacity,
   and an authorized investigation for concealed layers. State the required
   extent and the question, not simply “survey building.” Begin with appropriate
   nonintrusive information; opening up must have an agreed location, protection
   and reinstatement strategy. A concealed-material or instability concern can
   prevent that intrusive action without preventing unrelated option development.
4. Separate choices that can proceed from commitments that depend on the result.
   Test layouts respecting observed supports, keep an alternative service route,
   reserve adjustable closure zones and defer custom fabrication across an
   unmeasured junction. Keep the dependent opening, fixing or final dimension
   explicitly conditional. A removable partition is only a reversible choice if
   its attachment, substrate, services and required separation can tolerate change.
5. When evidence arrives, revise the affected geometry, support/control paths and
   option comparison. Record why an uncertainty closed or why its extent grew.
   Inspecting one bay may justify that bay's detail while repetition elsewhere
   remains a hypothesis. Give the decision owner the remaining dependency and a
   viable fallback, rather than silently treating a provisional line as surveyed.

| Evidence strategy | Useful choice | Trade-off and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Develop around accessible retained features | Compare uses/layouts with little immediate disturbance. | Preserves options but may retain inefficient routes or unused volume; capacity and concealed continuity remain open. |
| Target investigation at a decision that could reverse | Resolve the bearing beside the proposed opening, or the substrate at a replacement flashing. | Access, localized damage and reinstatement are real work; a favorable result cannot be extrapolated to all similar-looking locations. |
| Broaden investigation before fixing an integrated intervention | Appropriate where repeated concealed junctions or building-wide change make a local sample insufficient. | More disturbance/time and possible occupation changes; define coverage with the responsible specialists, rather than assume a universal sample count. |
| Choose an adjustable or independent intervention | Keep a closure panel adjustable or route new services clear of an uncertain wall. | Consumes space and may add supports/maintenance; independence must be traced through attachments, ground and control layers. |

**Failure → correction.** An original survey scenario has a measured corridor
whose two walls are not parallel. Redrawing both as a regular module makes a
prefabricated replacement door/reveal appear to fit. The error is the lost
as-found geometry, not the number of decimal places. Retain both measured faces,
check the opening at the relevant heights and diagonals, and compare a designed
adjustable closure with a locally remade reveal. Continue the room relationship
study using the measured corridor; defer fabrication until frame, attachment,
tolerance and clear-use geometry are confirmed. If opening up reveals a support
inside the proposed closure, change the attachment or opening choice rather
than absorb it into an unspecified tolerance.

**Checks and limits.** The result identifies surveyed extents, unresolved
contradictions, decisions still conditional and evidence that would change them.
Select survey accuracy for the actual fit/interface decision; do not borrow a
book's scale/accuracy class as a project specification. A visual crack or damp
map can locate an investigation; it cannot establish cause, activity, residual
strength or a repair recipe. Immediate stability, material hazards and safe
intrusive access need their responsible specialists; continue independent design
only where it does not depend on an unsafe or unverified premise.

## Choose retain, repair or replace against the changed use

**Question and inputs.** Which existing functions remain adequate, which defect
or incompatibility needs intervention, and what is lost by replacing the part?
Use the proposed demand changes, accessible condition evidence, known alteration
history, heritage/significance constraints, identified support/control functions,
maintenance access and permissible occupied-work windows. Identify whether the
proposal changes use, component performance, support, enclosure or several at
once. These are design descriptions pending actual regulatory classification.

**Mechanism.** Components have coupled roles and different renewal cycles.
Removing a worn finish can expose a viable backing; renewing an attractive face
can conceal a failing attachment. Replacing a structural part can change load
recipients or stability even when the elevation is reproduced. Conversely an
apparently sound retained floor can be incompatible with new concentrated loads,
vibration, wet use or separation demands. Age alone establishes none of these.

1. Mark the affected element's actual or provisional roles: gravity support,
   lateral resistance/restraint, enclosure/control layers, separation, service
   distribution, use and significant fabric. Overlay floors above/below and
   adjacent parts. An infill panel may restrain or be restrained by a frame;
   do not infer its removability from a thin plan line.
2. Separate a **defect hypothesis** from a **new-use mismatch**. For example,
   staining may come from a roof leak, a service leak or moisture at the old
   wall; a newly required opening width is a geometric mismatch. The first
   needs cause investigation before a durable repair; the second can already
   support a comparison of routes and opening positions.
3. Compare the alternatives below for the same use and affected interfaces.
   Include a use/layout change that reduces the intervention where authorized.
   State which existing layers/parts remain, which must be repaired or removed,
   and which functions are temporarily lost. Do not label the whole building
   “repair” when the proposal also transfers a wall's load or changes occupation.
4. Trace any new reaction, restraint, mass or stiffness change to actual recipients
   and ground, in both horizontal directions where stability is involved. Compare
   preserving the support line with a different route/use before reserving an
   engineered transfer. Use [wall/opening support](wall-floor-and-opening.md#develop-a-wall-opening-as-support-and-use)
   and [frame action](frame-and-core.md#choose-frame-and-stability-action-with-the-plan)
   for those mechanisms; the additional task here is to establish what actually
   exists, its condition and how intervention changes it.
5. Refit the architecture around the selected intervention's real reservation:
   jambs/linings, downstands, floor-level changes, service diversions, junctions
   and maintenance. Make any fallback conditional on its own capacity/condition
   evidence. Return a decision per affected part with its reason and reversal
   trigger, rather than a universal preference for retention or replacement.

| Alternative | What it preserves or improves | Consequence that may reverse the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Retain and maintain; adapt use or layout around it | Existing fabric/support/control continuity and often less disturbance. | May restrict goods routes, activity location or operating hours; continued suitability and access for maintenance still need evidence. |
| Repair a defined part and its cause | Can preserve sound surrounding material while restoring the affected function. | Requires a bounded defect, compatible materials and an accessible sound recipient; patching a symptom leaves the wetting/movement mechanism active. |
| Supplement or separate new work | A new service lining, independent support or distinct conditioned zone can avoid some disturbance to old work. | Takes space, may obscure inspection and introduces attachments, ground/movement and condensation interfaces; “independent” is a claim to trace. |
| Replace a defined component or assembly | Can address widespread incompatibility and reorganize its interfaces. | Removes fabric and temporary functions, may alter mass/stiffness/support and can enlarge the work boundary; a new face alone does not repair a defective retained backing. |

**Bounded opening failure → correction.** This original hypothetical local study
has a measured 0.90 m finished clear doorway in an existing masonry wall. Its
lintel, pier condition, floor bearing and lateral role are unverified. A proposed
routine trolley operation requires a 1.00 m swept width plus a project trial
0.15 m each side: **1.30 m clear**. These are invented study inputs, not legal
minima or engineering dimensions. The retained doorway fails that operation;
it may remain an ordinary people route only if that separate use is verified.

Widening to a drawn 1.50 m rough opening does not repair the failure. Trial side
reservations of 0.12 m support plus 0.02 m finish each give
**1.50 − 2 × (0.12 + 0.02) = 1.22 m**, still below 1.30 m before any additional
frame/operating deductions. These side zones are comparison envelopes, not sized
posts or a proposed strengthening detail. Removing the old jambs also leaves the
load/stability path and temporary state unresolved.

Compare two actual corrections. **A:** retain that opening and use a separately
measured 1.45 m clear service doorway for goods, testing the complete approach,
turns, door operation, ownership and occupation schedule; the straight segment
has **0.15 m** against the trial envelope. It is a conditional geometric advantage,
not an assessment of floor loading or an automatic shared escape route.
**B:** investigate and develop a widened opening with the responsible engineer;
a 1.65 m rough trial with the same provisional side deductions gives 1.37 m,
leaving only 0.07 m before extra frame/operation deductions. Keep a larger
reservation or a different opening position available if real supports exceed
the trial. Do not select B merely because 1.65 exceeds the trolley width.

For B, the targeted questions concern wall construction, actual lintel and its
bearings, masonry/pier condition, floor/roof reactions, lateral role, services,
supports below and the construction/temporary-support proposal. New evidence of
a vulnerable pier or a required deeper transfer can favor A, relocation or a
smaller goods unit whose actual operation is verified. Evidence of a feasible
local intervention can favor B if it also preserves the route, separation,
clearance and occupied stages. Neither alternative grants cutting permission.

**Checks and limits.** Distinguish visible wear from confirmed damage and
functional mismatch. Recheck adjacent bays, both wall leaves, upper/lower support,
diaphragm/restraint connections and any altered ground interface. New storage,
plant or dense occupancy can need structural assessment even where nothing is
removed. Condition, capacity, hazardous materials, fire/acoustic performance and
heritage permissions require actual evidence; do not infer them from appearance,
the former use or a textbook's average service life. Any cost, carbon or duration
comparison requires quantities, boundaries and project evidence, not a generic
“retention is cheaper” score.

## Connect replacement work to the actual retained substrate

**Question and inputs.** How will new work attach, move and maintain continuity
where a retained assembly ends? Identify the actual layers at the interface,
their soundness/moisture and accessibility, structural recipient, existing
movement joints, water entry/exit, climate and changed internal use. Inspect the
relevant opening/base/roof ends as well as the field. Use the established
[control functions and junctions](envelope-and-assemblies.md#develop-four-connected-junctions)
for the general layer logic; develop the old-to-new transition below.

**Mechanism.** A retrofit inherits substrates and terminations that a new-build
section may assume are accessible, dry and continuous. New insulation changes
surface temperatures, reveal/edge heights and drying paths; a new drained face
needs somewhere to discharge above retained openings and at its base. Moving
water outward at one new layer can send it behind an old layer below. An old
finish cannot be presumed a load-bearing fixing base or a durable membrane bond.
Differential movement can break an otherwise continuous control layer.

1. Draw the as-found assembly, proposed retained cut line and new assembly at
   a single affected junction, in plan/section as needed. Label each concealed
   layer unknown until verified. Locate the actual substrate for attachment and
   each air/water transition; a line to “existing wall” leaves the question open.
2. Compare repair in place, local removal back to verified sound material,
   externally supported replacement and a different intervention boundary. Set
   out where investigation and preparation can take place before those surfaces
   are covered. Mechanical attachment still requires a capable recipient; it is
   not an automatic cure for unsound backing or concealed decay.
3. Follow water through the **new field → retained junction → exterior outlet**
   and through the blocked/failed outlet case. Then follow air/thermal continuity
   and the drying strategy across the same line. Check new facade thickness at
   sill projection, jamb/head returns, eave/parapet, finished ground, vents and
   service entries. Preserve viable old functions intentionally; do not cover an
   existing drainage/ventilation exit merely to make a continuous elevation.
4. At an old/new structural or material discontinuity, identify what moves,
   what restrains it and what must remain separate. Continue the movement strategy
   through roof/wall/floor finishes and relevant services; a rigid plaster bridge
   or fixed pipe can defeat a structural separation. Detail the compatible
   flexible weather/air transition and inspectable cover without making it an
   accidental load path. Joint width, movement demand, fixings, sealant selection
   and fire/acoustic closure need actual specialist/system evidence.
   For a selected sealant joint, establish sound, suitably prepared compatible
   bonding faces and control the sealant contact depth with the actual system's
   filler or bond breaker. Prevent unintended adhesion at the back of a joint
   intended to move between two faces; packing an irregular old recess solid
   can restrain that movement. A primer cannot make detached paint or render a
   sound recipient. Compare a developed membrane/covered joint where an exposed
   sealant connection cannot be prepared, maintained or made compatible.
5. Test the intervention edge as well as the finished detail: can the retained
   layer be exposed, prepared, joined, inspected and protected before closure?
   If inaccessible sound backing or a missing old control layer prevents a local
   connection, broaden the repair boundary or change the assembly/route. Use
   [component repair and replacement access](envelope-and-assemblies.md#compatibility-movement-and-replaceability)
   for component insertion/removal; the distinct issue here is whether a viable
   retained recipient and transition exist at all.

| Transition choice | Opportunity | Trade-off and necessary evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Local repair and compatible transition to retained layers | Limits disturbance and preserves viable fabric. | Needs identifiable, accessible sound layers and compatible preparation/bonding; a face bead cannot bridge an unverified concealed break. |
| Added exterior insulation/finish or drained cladding | Can improve the enclosure while retaining backing. | Attachment, drainage exit, opening returns, base/eave geometry and drying change; do not generalize one historical barrier-system failure to every current system. |
| Interior intervention while exterior fabric remains | Can preserve an exterior of significance and avoid some external access. | Loses internal clearance, changes old wall/beam-end conditions and may leave exposed bridges; assess climate/use-specific moisture behavior and protection continuity. |
| New part separated from old support with an intentional joint | Can avoid forcing the old support to accept new actions. | Still needs foundations/ground assessment and continuous weather, air, separation and service transitions; a visible gap does not establish structural independence. |

**Bounded roof interface failure → correction.** In an original local study,
an existing low roof abuts an old wall below a retained window. The measured roof
surface at that wall is datum 0; the sill is +0.22 m. A proposed new roof build-up
raises the finished water plane there to +0.16 m, leaving **0.06 m** to the sill.
The study explicitly reserves **0.15 m** for developing the weather transition;
this is an assumed design allowance, not an accepted regulatory/product upstand.
The proposed overlay is incompatible with that allowance by **0.09 m**. Drawing
a sealant line under the sill cannot restore the missing section or prove the
old painted render suitable for adhesion.

Compare raising/remaking the window sill to at least +0.31 m for that trial,
changing the roof thermal/build-up arrangement, or replacing the affected roof
edge/rooflight arrangement. Raising the sill loses 0.09 m of the former opening
height if its head stays fixed, changing light/view and potentially operation;
an interior insulation alternative consumes internal section and changes moisture
conditions. Do not reduce insulation or a drainage zone arbitrarily to save the
window. Re-evaluate with the [roof-boundary alternatives](roof-and-span.md#choose-where-the-thermal-boundary-follows-the-roof)
and actual performance evidence. Any higher water plane from falls changes this
budget again.

Suppose authorized local exposure then finds detached render at the intended
flashing termination. Move the transition to an assessed sound recipient with
compatible preparation, or widen/change the intervention; avoid bonding the new
system to that detached finish. Distinguish flashing attachment from roof load
support. If the new roof relies on old wall anchors, their recipient and capacity
remain an additional question. Across a deliberately separated old/new roof
joint, provide an actual moving weather transition and prevent rigid insulation,
coping or service bridges from defeating it. No joint-cover shape supplies a
movement capacity. Trace water to an available outlet and retain inspection
before making either alternative a fixed construction detail.

**Checks and limits.** The result names retained substrates, changed control
paths, attachments, movement assumptions and points needing exposure/test or
specialist assessment. Check all ends of the local intervention, occupied clear
space lost to linings, altered ground/outlet levels and future inspection. Do
not add an exterior trench beside old foundations to solve dampness without the
[ground/support assessment](ground-and-basement.md#map-neighboring-ground-and-construction-dependencies).
Underpinning is a separately designed intervention with working-space and stage
consequences, not a detail inferred from an old support sketch. Product bond,
anchor resistance, hygrothermal response and fire/acoustic behavior cannot be
established from this architectural transition study.

## Test occupied work as a succession of usable states

**Question and inputs.** Can the retained use operate during the proposed local
work, and which functions must be provided before each change? Establish actual
occupied zones and hours, available entrances/journeys, work/access rights,
required separations and services, delivery/removal routes, noisy/wet/dusty
activities, exposed-weather periods and the contractor/specialist dependencies.
Use actual requirements and operating agreements for acceptable occupation;
continued presence of people is not evidence that a stage is suitable.

**Mechanism.** A finished design and a sequence of individually small tasks can
both look plausible while an intermediate state loses support, weather closure,
separation, service or the only usable route. Phasing must preserve the functions
that remain necessary in that state. It cannot rely on a later element, an
uninstalled protective layer or a closed tenancy to complete them.

1. Define the states that change a necessary function: investigation/opening up,
   service diversion, removal, new support/enclosure, connection, inspection and
   return to use. Combine unaffected tasks; do not invent a detailed contractor
   schedule. Identify occupied/work/exclusion areas and material/people routes
   for each state on the affected plan/section.
2. For every state, trace permanent and temporary recipients of affected support
   and restraint without designing temporary works. Identify what the responsible
   engineer/contractor must design and confirm before the architectural sequence
   can rely on it. Temporary support may consume the intended occupied room or
   route; its space cannot be assumed zero.
3. Trace the occupied entrance-to-use journey and the separately required
   evacuation/access arrangements, with door states, barriers, deliveries and
   waste movement. Check usable services, relevant separation and weather/control
   closure. A temporary dust screen does not acquire structural, fire, acoustic
   or weather performance from its label. The site safety plan, hazard controls
   and permission to occupy need the actual responsible parties and evidence.
4. Compare localized work with independent access, off-hours work with full
   reinstatement, temporary relocation of the affected use, or a longer decant
   allowing a coherent intervention. Recount storage, holding, alternate route,
   temporary facilities and protection space for each. Less visible demolition
   does not necessarily mean less disruption or fewer return visits.
5. State the predecessor and release evidence at each functional change: the
   alternate route and separation usable before blocking the old route; designed
   support in place before removal; compatible control transition inspected
   before concealment; actual service/closure checks before reoccupation. A missed
   release changes that stage's occupation/work proposal; it need not cancel
   independent design work elsewhere.

**Occupied-phase counterexample.** An original local repair proposal keeps a
small clinic room open while replacing its window and the adjoining internal
reveal. The finished replacement fits. In the removal state the corridor is the
sole supplied patient route, the contractor's protective enclosure projects into
it, and the same corridor is used to carry glazing and rubble. A daytime door
barrier also interrupts the clinic's only supplied access. The proposed phrase
“one window at a time” has not made an operable occupied state.

| State | Required correction and release evidence |
| --- | --- |
| Before opening the reveal | Establish the permitted work zone, actual hazard investigation, protection/separation and handling route; verify whether corridor occupation can remain viable. Do not assume a generic sheet partition resolves it. |
| Removal and exposed opening | Either provide a genuinely usable independently assessed alternate journey/work arrangement, or relocate/close the affected clinic use for this state. Confirm the designed temporary support if required, service isolation and weather protection before removal; do not carry glass through patients to preserve the original timetable. |
| Connection and inspection | Keep retained/new substrates and control transitions accessible until the appropriate checks are complete; protect the room through any overnight exposed state. Finishing the reveal early to reopen it can conceal a failed connection. |
| Return to use | Verify the completed opening/operation, reinstated enclosure/separation/services, clean usable journey and applicable occupation conditions with responsible parties. A surface finish or cleared tool cart alone is insufficient. |

Off-hours work is an alternative only if its actual duration, noise constraints,
protection, curing/drying, inspection and reliable morning reinstatement permit
it. If the window cannot be weather-closed overnight, extend the closure or
provide a designed temporary enclosure; do not transfer that risk to the clinic.
If survey finds deteriorated backing and the repair boundary grows, rerun these
states before keeping the original occupation promise. Conversely an independent
room elsewhere can continue operating if its own support, enclosure, routes and
services are unaffected and the actual occupied-work arrangements permit it.

**Checks and limits.** Return the changed occupation assumptions, state-specific
spatial reservations, dependencies and the evidence needed for release. Check
temporary arrangements against both ordinary operation and required emergency
functions; do not treat a normal lift as a temporary evacuation solution by
default. This is architectural phasing and interface coordination, not a method
statement, a temporary-support design, infection-control plan or safety approval.
Specialist investigation and site responsibilities must be resolved for the
affected activity; a fictional detailed schedule cannot substitute for them.

## Source boundary

Selected Neufert and Ching fragments, exact identities and page extents belong
in [sources](sources.md). The local methods are original synthesis: Neufert
informs differentiated interventions, condition/history/survey evidence and the
structural and building-physics consequences of reuse; Ching informs the
retained-substrate, drainage, old/new movement and separately designed support
interfaces. The decision chains, numeric opening/roof studies and occupied-phase
counterexample are newly authored, bounded illustrations. No source project,
plan, scan, service-life value, joint size, chemical recipe, capacity, temporary
works sequence or historical foreign legal procedure is installed here.
