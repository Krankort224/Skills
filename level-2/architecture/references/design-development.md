# Design development and architectural correction

Use this knowledge when an organizing idea must become a workable spatial
proposal, or a critique must produce a concrete revision. Select the question
needed for the current design. The user's requested form governs the result;
the examples below do not prescribe drawings, schedules or an issue package.
All example dimensions are hypothetical design inputs, not normative minima.

## Carry the idea into spatial decisions

A concept becomes architectural through the relationships it protects. For an
idea of a shared garden room, distinguish the essential shared outlook and
recognizable arrival from optional symmetry, a particular partition position or
a continuous glass wall. Test whether the plan gives the intended users access
to that outlook, whether the section admits it at occupied positions, and
whether the elevation and material depth support the same hierarchy. A label
such as open, calm or flexible does not resolve those questions.

Work from elements with the largest consequences toward those easiest to change:
site access and boundary; established support and floor/roof geometry; cores,
vertical circulation and continuous shafts; envelope bays; partitions; fitout
and loose furniture. This is a hierarchy of change cost and dependency, not an
automatic legal priority. A fixed core may be an accepted project constraint;
a support location may still be provisional. Identify each one's actual status.
Do not move a support merely because a partition fits better in a diagram.

Maintain useful alternatives at the right level. A deep room with inadequate
external exposure may need a different depth or location, not a different paint
color. A blocked door may need a local opening or furniture adjustment, not a
new building concept. Where a preferred idea fails a fixed operational need,
retain its strongest spatial relationship and change its means of expression.

## Establish datums and dimension meanings

A plan needs a shared origin, orientation and measurement boundary before
coordinates can be reconciled. Sections and elevations need a stated vertical
datum: for example, a project finished-floor level of 0.000, explicitly related
to an external survey datum only if that relationship is known. Distinguish
finished floor, structural slab top, underside of structure, ceiling, opening
sill/head, roof surface and parapet. Raising the floor finish can reduce clear
headroom and alter thresholds while leaving the structural level unchanged.

| Dimension | What it describes | Frequent mistaken substitution |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Structural/grid | Axes or structural faces used to organize support and setting out | A room's usable clear width |
| Unfinished opening | Space reserved in its supporting wall | Frame size or clear passage |
| Finished face to face | Room geometry after the stated linings/finishes | Dimension to a wall centerline |
| Clear passage | Unobstructed opening under a defined door position and hardware condition | Door leaf or nominal product width |
| Operational clearance | Space needed for approach, use, maintenance or movement | Empty floor visible with furniture omitted |

Use baseline dimensions from a stable datum for positions whose errors must not
accumulate. Use chains to explain the parts of a whole, with explicit wall and
lining thicknesses. Verify that the chain closes against the overall dimension;
do not distribute a discrepancy invisibly among rooms. Avoid multiple
independently controlled dimensions for the same dependent position.

For example, a hypothetical 8.000 m structural-face span with 0.050 m internal
lining at each end and a 0.150 m finished partition can accommodate clear room
widths of 3.000 m and 4.750 m: 0.050 + 3.000 + 0.150 + 4.750 + 0.050 = 8.000.
If the first room becomes 3.200 m and the span and build-ups are fixed, the second
becomes 4.550 m. Keeping both original room widths would create a contradiction.
If a 1.000 m leaf is specified, obtain its frame/hardware geometry before using
it as evidence for a required clear passage.

For a confirmed Russian project, name the height whose limit is being tested.
A floor-to-ceiling dimension, floor-to-floor spacing and grade-to-roof dimension
answer different questions. Preserve their actual start/end datums in the
section; the applicable Russian definition must come from the selected
[typology record](normative/ru/routes.md#use-and-area) and, for a fire question,
the [fire route](normative/ru/routes.md#safety-and-access). The catalog's housing
height boundary is not permission to insert a convenient geometric roof height
without checking the definition. Architectural height also does not establish
the height used for fire requirements.

For example, with finished floor 0.000, structure underside +3.600 m and a
suspended ceiling +3.000 m, the room is 3.000 m clear beneath that ceiling.
Before: label it “3.600 m high” and place an activity requiring a supplied
3.200 m clear envelope below the ceiling. After: record the 0.200 m conflict;
trial raising the ceiling to +3.200 m leaves 0.400 m below structure for a
coordinated services reservation. If that reservation cannot work, relocate the
activity or revise an authorized datum. These are hypothetical project-fit
dimensions, not Russian minimum heights, and do not resolve building/fire height.

## Fit rooms and operations inside the floor

A room-area total is a demand, not a packing solution. Remove or reserve the
actual envelope build-up, support, core, shafts and shared movement from the
same floor boundary used for comparison. Distinguish whole floor, internal
planning envelope, finished room area and operational furniture envelope;
formal metrics use their governing [area method](program-and-space.md#how-can-areas-be-counted-once-and-compared).

Use the canonical [room-fit method](planning-and-section.md#construct-a-room-that-can-be-used),
including its occupied-state checks and nested clear-area/wall-reservation
example, to construct each affected room. A dwelling uses its additional
[actual household-use test](dwelling-fit.md#test-a-dwelling-through-its-actual-use).
Do not maintain a second general furnishing/door/turning procedure here.

At floor scale, place those checked room envelopes against the same frontage,
support and common-route reservations. Reconcile the dimension chain and area
ledger, test the connections to neighbors, and carry changed edges into the
affected section/elevation or schedule. A room that fits alone may still block
the common route or consume another room's frontage. Change an allowed boundary
or report the fixed incompatibility; a label or area subtotal cannot resolve it.
Keep assumed operating envelopes and unresolved source-dependent clearances
explicit when using the fit result. Preserve unaffected accepted decisions.

## Choose sections that test the solution

Select a cut through the uncertainty, rather than through the most attractive
empty volume. Useful cuts include the deepest occupied room and its opening;
arrival across an actual level change; a stair or vertical connection; a roof
transition; or the junction where a beam, ceiling zone, partition and facade
must coexist. A cut through an unobstructed bay does not test the neighboring
downstand that determines headroom. Mark any offset cut so separated locations
are not mistaken for one continuous plane.

Carry plan coordinates, floor boundaries and openings into the section. Show
the critical finished levels, clear heights and reserved build-up depths. Keep
structure and services reservations visible rather than drawing a ceiling line
through them. An unknown service depth is an unresolved fit, not spare space.
For a tall room beneath stepped upper parts, use the
[floor-footprint/height overlay](planning-and-section.md#reconcile-upper-floors-tall-rooms-and-roofs)
so that every overlap has a physically consistent level/clearance interpretation.
Compare the occupied position, window head/sill, shading, ceiling and outside
obstruction when judging a light/view concept; elevation opening area alone
does not show what the back of a room receives.

Use a local junction explanation when the problem is local. A broad section can
show height and continuity; a closer study of a threshold can show how finish
levels, drainage and the door meet. Neither view requires inventing engineering
sizes or a complete construction detail. State the geometry being established
and the technical property still needing evidence.

## Coordinate elevations without copying partitions

Give the facade its own organizing logic: support/bay spacing, opening cadence,
base and top, entrance hierarchy, horizontal sill/head datums, depth and material
transitions. Check that each actual opening agrees in position and size with
the plan, and in level with the section. Distinguish the structural opening,
frame, visible glazing and opening leaf; they need not share one dimension.

An internal partition change need not move the facade rhythm. It does require a
credible termination: a solid pier, suitable mullion/jamb zone or another
designed interface. A partition ending against the middle of opening glass can
leave privacy, acoustic separation, weather-envelope access and future
replacement unresolved. Move the partition or change the relevant opening when
that interface cannot work; do not silently shift every bay.

Test the elevation from occupied interior positions as well as outside. A desk,
bed or cupboard can conflict with a low sill or an inward opening window. A
raised sill may improve privacy while taking away a seated view. Preserve
accepted external openings during a local revision when possible, and explain
any necessary change through both its interior consequence and facade hierarchy.

## Develop assembly and construction intent

A material name describes neither an assembly nor how it is made. Identify the
layers' roles, supporting substrate, attachment intent, movement/junction zones,
water-shedding and drying paths where relevant, and the accessible face for
installation and maintenance. Reserve real depth for these roles without
pretending that an unverified build-up has achieved a performance rating. Use
[envelope continuity](performance-and-coordination.md#climate-exposure-and-envelope-continuity)
for the environmental paths.

Think through installation order. Can the supporting frame be installed and
checked before the finish hides it? Can the worker reach the fixing after the
adjacent panel is installed? Can a membrane lap be formed continuously around
the opening, and is the drainage path left open by the sill and finish? A neat
line on an elevation can conceal an inaccessible fixing or an impossible layer
overlap. Alter the junction, introduce a removable closure, or revise the order
and access space until the intent is credible.

Test replacement in reverse. Identify which panel, fitting or plant item wears
out first, how it is released, and its route out. A replaceable luminaire above
a permanent timber soffit needs a reachable removal opening and a path past
supports. Labeling the whole ceiling accessible does not establish that an
item fits through it. Avoid fixing short-life fitout across movement joints or
making it the only access to a long-life enclosure junction.

Keep architectural decisions active while technical checks remain open. Choose
the accessible side, reserve the opening, maintain the intended drainage path
and reject contradictory geometry. Then name the specific unresolved evidence,
such as tested junction performance, attachment capacity or manufacturer
removal dimensions. This is more useful than a general instruction to seek
specialist advice. Aesthetic intent, a performance target and a selected
product are separate claims; reconcile them against the actual drawn condition.

## Turn critique into a spatial correction

For an existing Russian object, describe the physical works before assigning
the word “renovation” a legal meaning. Retain the before/after room and premises
boundaries, moved equipment and services, load-bearing interventions, facade
openings and connections to shared land or circulation. Use the
[GrK work-type owner](normative/ru/federal-framework.md#grk-190-design-and-expertise)
for construction/reconstruction/capital-repair classification and, for premises
in an apartment building, the [ЖК owner](normative/ru/federal-framework.md#zhk-188-alterations)
for переустройство, перепланировка and common-property questions. These are
separate checks: an unchanged external envelope or total floor area does not
answer whether an internal premises change falls under the selected procedure.
The current operative legal fields remain open where the owning record lacks
primary text; do not turn a plausible work label into an approval or exemption.

**Changed-entrance correction.** Suppose a consultation-room option gains an
attractive external entrance by absorbing a strip of the building's shared lobby.
Before: the plan calls this unused surplus and counts it within the operator's
room; residents can reach the stair only through the operator's locked boundary.
After: retain the shared lobby and continuous resident route in the base option,
move the waiting pocket inside the operator's established premises, and put its
door on that boundary. Keep an exterior-opening option conditional on the actual
wall/land/property intervention and selected legal procedure. Investigate
common-property effects rather than assuming either that every facade change
needs the same consent or that a door drawing settles the question. Follow
[use and works selection](normative/ru/applicability.md#classify-use-and-works-before-applying-a-rule)
for the affected host, embedded use and local conditions. This preserves a usable
spatial proposal while the distinct authorization question is resolved.

Locate the failure and explain its mechanism. “The plan lacks clarity” becomes
actionable when the entrance faces an opaque store and the destination door is
hidden behind it. “Too little circulation” requires the actual conflict: the
only route crosses occupied chair space, or a door narrows the route when open.
Distinguish a demonstrated failure from missing evidence and from a preference.

Develop a correction that changes the cause: move the opaque store, relocate
the opening, protect a separate route or exchange room positions. Test what the
correction sacrifices as well as repairs: usable room width, privacy, external
exposure, support alignment or assembly access. A more attractive composition
cannot compensate for a failed fixed requirement. Conversely, preserve working
arrival hierarchy, useful daylight edge and accepted geometry rather than
discarding them with the flawed arrangement.

Carry the changed fact through the representations that express it. A moved
partition changes finished room sizes and furniture fit; it may change door
approaches, ceiling boundaries, facade terminations and material quantities.
A shifted door changes the opening location and relevant approach/sightline,
not merely its tag. Retain the unchanged datum and dimensions. Recheck earlier
successful relationships affected by the revision, including behavior at the
limiting geometry; do not equate a revised label with a corrected design.

Distinguish **a completed architectural correction**, **a conditional geometric
trial**, and **a next check**. If the known defect is a bottleneck, missing wall
allowance or unclosed access edge, change an actual permitted dimension, room
boundary or connection and recompute its neighbors. Saying that it should be
widened or checked later does not perform that correction. Where precise
construction or equipment data are missing, use declared study reservations to
show one plausible arrangement and identify its failure threshold; do not
invent verified sizes or claim the trial is a final fit. Leave truly dependent
specialist properties open, while completing the spatial work the available
geometry permits.

## Worked critique and revised plan

**Hypothetical brief and geometry.** A small community studio occupies a single
12.000 × 9.000 m rectangle measured between finished internal perimeter faces.
Coordinates run east in x and north in y, with southwest origin (0,0). There
are no internal columns in the supplied baseline; support design is outside
this example. The fixed south band, y = 0–3.000, contains a 3 × 3 m core at
x = 0–3, an entry zone at x = 3–6, and support/storage reservations at x = 6–12.
These band allocations include their internal walls; they are not clear room
areas. The entry is through the south side. Keep that band, perimeter, north
openings, roof geometry and workshop capacity of twelve fixed. The initial
brief also asks for a six-person quiet meeting room independently reached from
the entry. Furniture envelopes below are project assumptions for this study.

**Attractive failed scheme.** A central meeting pavilion in the north zone,
x = 3–7, y = 4.5–8, makes a strong focal object. A continuous perimeter workbench
and twelve-seat workshop occupy the remainder. Its presentation calls all
remaining floor “flexible circulation.” A work island's west edge at x = 8
leaves a one-metre approach strip against the pavilion's east face at x = 7;
storage closes the strip's north end. The meeting door opens into this strip
at y = 6.0–7.0. An occupied chair at x = 7.2–7.8, y = 4.8–5.4 reduces the
approach from the south to gaps of 0.2 m on either side. Access therefore
depends on the chair being vacated. The pavilion is drawn as four clear metres wide while its
outside faces are four metres apart; with assumed 0.150 m walls it is only
3.700 m clear. Its opaque south wall also hides the meeting door from entry.
The concept's focal quality survives, but independent access, stated dimensions
and legibility fail. An area surplus cannot cure these local conflicts.

**Revised plan R1.** Replace the central pavilion with a north room edge and a
recognizable shared route along its south face. Keep arrival open to that edge,
with meeting and workshop entrances visible from the entry zone. The gathering
idea becomes the shared threshold and outlook rather than an isolated object.

| Region | Coordinates / dimension basis | Area within the planning rectangle |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fixed south band | x = 0–12; y = 0–3, inclusive reservations | 36.000 m² |
| Shared route | x = 0–12; y = 3–4.5, finished clear strip | 18.000 m² |
| North cross-wall reservation | x = 0–12; y = 4.5–4.65 | 1.800 m² |
| Meeting/workshop partition | x = 4.5–4.65; y = 4.65–9 | 0.6525 m² |
| Meeting room | x = 0–4.5; y = 4.65–9, finished clear | 19.575 m² |
| Workshop | x = 4.65–12; y = 4.65–9, finished clear | 31.9725 m² |

The accounting closes to 108.000 m². Wall reservations include door openings
in this simple allocation; no extra door area is added. The two finished rooms
total 51.5475 m², not 108 m² of usable activity space. The route's 1.500 m width
is a chosen study input, with applicable accessibility/escape requirements
still to be checked against the actual use and jurisdiction.

In the workshop a 4.200 × 1.200 m table has a project occupied-chair envelope
5.400 × 2.400 m, with six seats on each long side. Centering that envelope at
(8.325,6.825) gives x = 5.625–11.025 and y = 5.625–8.025. The residual on all four
sides is 0.975 m, exceeding this study's requested 0.900 m movement strip.
This is a check against an assumed operating envelope, not an occupancy or
accessible workstation determination. A nominal one-metre door swing hinged
at (11.900,4.650), opening inward toward the north, misses the chair rectangle:
its closest corner is about 1.31 m from the hinge. But the fully open leaf at
x=11.900 extends to y=5.650, overlapping the chair envelope's y range by 0.025 m
and leaving only 0.875 m beside its east edge. The sweep intrudes further into
the movement strip during opening. No collision with furniture is therefore
insufficient evidence for the study's 0.900 m simultaneous movement objective.

Retain the table position and repair the door strategy. Trial a surface-sliding
door on the workshop side of the south wall, with nominal opening x=10–11 and a
parking length west of it. Assume a total leaf/hardware projection of 0.050 m
into the room for this study; the south movement strip then becomes
0.975−0.050=0.925 m, with no swing through it. The other three strips stay
0.975 m. These are conditional geometric reservations: the actual frame, leaf
overlap, track, handle, removal access and clear opening must fit the selected
system. Check whether a suitable sliding assembly can meet the project's
acoustic, access and any fire requirements; no such performance is inferred.
If its projection exceeds 0.075 m, the stated movement objective fails and the
door/room/furniture arrangement needs another revision. Do not silently narrow
the passage or assume a nominal door width is its clear opening.

For the meeting room, a six-seat table/chair envelope 3.000 × 2.100 m centered
at (2.250,6.825), plus this study's 0.700 m movement allowance around it, requires
4.400 × 3.500 m and fits the 4.500 × 4.350 m room. An inward door near its
southeast corner can keep its leaf sweep clear of the chair envelope; check its
actual approach and operating hardware once selected. Both room doors open from
the shared route, so occupation or locking of one room no longer controls the
other's access. The entry sightline to each door is retained free of tall
storage. These are concrete improvements over R0, with formal requirements and
the assumed furniture dimensions still open to verification.

## Worked section and assembly progression

Use the same R1 coordinates and a finished floor of 0.000. The supplied roof
underside is +3.600 m and a beam over the cross-wall line has underside +3.150 m.
The failed scheme's floating pavilion walls stop at +2.400 m below a common
ceiling void. That can define a visual room, but does not support the promised
speech privacy: sound has a shared path above it. A tall perspective elsewhere
in the studio concealed this interface.

A north–south cut through the meeting room, door and shared route demonstrates
the correction: keep the shared route ceiling provisionally at +2.400 m and
the meeting ceiling at +3.000 m, while the separation continues through the
ceiling zone to a coordinated roof/beam junction. These are selected spatial
levels, not verified acoustic or service performance. Show the beam crossing
and its junction explicitly. The 0.150 m difference between room ceiling and
beam underside cannot be called a usable service zone without actual fit data;
reserve the main distribution south of that line and keep any necessary crossing
as a named fit question. Do not cut or move the beam to protect a ceiling image.

A second local cut at the north opening checks sill +0.900 m and head +2.400 m
against the occupied table position and +3.000 m ceiling. The glazing stays
below that ceiling; a high room alone does not provide high-level daylight.
Opening, shading and outside obstructions still govern the daylight result.

For a timber-faced shared threshold, distinguish demountable finish from the
separating wall behind it. Install and inspect the continuing separation and
penetration interfaces before covering them; leave the necessary service
access on the route side. Arrange removable facing panels so adjacent fixed
joinery does not trap their release edge. Check that the intended removable
item and a worker's access fit the proposed opening. Retain the warm material
intent while changing concealed construction that defeats privacy or access.
No tested isolation rating, structural attachment or service capacity is
established by these section decisions.

## Propagate a changed client constraint

**Change R2.** The client replaces the six-person meeting room with two
independent consultation rooms. Each needs a supplied, accepted clear activity
envelope of 2.100 × 4.000 m. Keep the workshop, fixed band, shared route, north
openings, structural/roof levels and perimeter unchanged. This is a changed
room requirement, not permission to reduce workshop capacity or move the core.

If this hypothetical revision is applied to premises in a Russian apartment
building, record the new fixed partition and two-room arrangement as actual
works for classification, even though the enclosing 108 m² rectangle stays
fixed. Confirm the consultation activity: a public service, private household
use and a clinical service are different source-selection hypotheses. The
geometry below establishes none of those uses or their legal work category.

Divide the former meeting width with a 0.150 m partition occupying
x = 2.175–2.325, y = 4.650–9.000. The two resulting finished rooms are each
2.175 × 4.350 m = 9.46125 m². Their 18.9225 m² plus the new wall reservation
0.6525 m² equals the former 19.575 m². Each accepted activity rectangle fits;
the small residual is not permission to add further furniture. Provide a door
from the shared route to each room and test the actual furniture, door sweep
and approach together. Do not retain the former six-seat furniture or use one
consultation room as access to the other.

The existing north openings in the west zone occupy x = 0.500–1.700 and
2.800–4.000 m. The new partition meets the solid interval between them, so each
room retains one opening and the facade opening pattern stays fixed. Keep the
sill/head datums at +0.900/+2.400 m. Recheck privacy from outside and the new
desk positions; two windows do not by themselves establish two adequate daylight
conditions. Elsewhere the facade bay/opening alignment stays as accepted.

Continue the new separation through the ceiling zone as in R1. Check its roof
termination and service penetrations, and revise the local ceiling/access
arrangement where the added wall interrupts it. Update the two room dimensions,
area allocation, room/door identity, furniture, separation/material intent and
the section that explains the junction wherever those appear in the supplied
outputs. Retain the unchanged workshop geometry and external levels.

This revision is a plausible geometric response to the changed brief. If the
client instead requires each room to be 2.300 m clear, the fixed former meeting
width cannot contain them: 2.300 + 0.150 + 2.300 = 4.750 m > 4.500 m. State that
specific incompatibility. A different room arrangement or an authorized change
to another fixed boundary is needed; relabeling the rooms or borrowing the
workshop's movement strip would not satisfy the stated constraint.
