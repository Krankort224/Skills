# Roof, span and support decisions

Use this reference to give a clear room a plausible spanning/enclosure strategy,
or to repair a roof that conflicts with supports, height, services, drainage or
openings. Select the affected question below; a roof-shape request does not
require reading every structural family. These are architectural mechanisms and
space reservations, not member/foundation sizing, a structural analysis or a
tested roof specification. Retain actual project limits and use
[requirements](../../requirements.md) for any applicable numerical prescription.

## Choose a spanning mechanism and its boundaries

Start with the clear activity field, allowed support positions, desired openings,
usable height, roof-height limit and service/replacement routes. Distinguish the
main span from secondary decking/purlin spans: rotating the main structure
changes which edge receives concentrated reactions, not just the ceiling rhythm.
Trace roof surface → secondary members → principal system → supports → ground.
An architectural preference for a clear facade does not remove a reaction.

| Mechanism | Architectural opportunity | Spatial obligation and failure test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Beam, slab or bending frame | A flat or pitched upper boundary can align with room bands; a designed rigid frame may keep an opening free of diagonal bracing. | Reserve actual depth and joint/support zones. A beam simply placed on two columns is not automatically a lateral frame. A rigid frame can develop horizontal reactions; its connection and base cannot be assumed equivalent to simple bearings. |
| Triangulated truss | Places a deeper, often expressive structure over a column-free field; roof shape and bottom-chord shape can differ. | Lower chords, web nodes, gussets and bracing occupy volume. A triangular diagram is an idealized force arrangement, not a right to hang plant anywhere or remove an apparently unused member. Non-nodal loads and eccentric connections can add bending; coordinate load introduction. A rectangular Vierendeel opening needs bending-resisting members/joints, not a silently deleted diagonal. |
| Arch or tied arch | A curved compression-dominant span gives a continuous vault-like room. | Name the horizontal-thrust route: abutment/foundation, an appropriate adjacent system, or an engineered tie. Glass infill is not an abutment. For comparable loading, flattening an arch can increase the horizontal-reaction problem; it does not provide a universally shallower equivalent of a beam. A tie may solve a boundary question but cross the required room volume. Changed/asymmetric loads, settlement and out-of-plane action still matter. |
| Cable or tensile field | A light-looking suspended edge or canopy can span above a broad field. | Sag, mast/backstay, anchor and prestress/stabilization geometry are part of the architecture. Changing load changes an unstabilized cable shape and can alter drainage/headroom. Compare the ground/edge space claimed by anchors with the apparent gain of removing columns. Do not assign a cable compression resistance or infer wind stability from a hanging curve. |
| Plate, folded surface or shell | A continuous surface can combine enclosure and support, with a directional fold or curved room character. | Trace forces to the actual edges, ribs/rings, ties and concentrated supports. A thin field does not imply a thin edge or unrestricted opening. A rooflight, free edge or hung point load can change the mechanism; compare a supported opening or separate rooflight band before cutting the surface. An arched vault, a barrel shell and a lattice dome are not interchangeable construction systems. |

Compare at the same required clear field and height, including the consequences
at the ends. A ridge beam may move supports into end glazing; an arch may put
abutments into circulation; a truss may reserve a larger ceiling zone. Material
names alone cannot select the mechanism. Keep system depth as a supplied or
explicit trial reservation until structurally checked; no span/depth ratio or
capacity follows from this table.

## Distinguish a ridge board from a supported ridge

A ridge board locates opposing rafters; naming that board does not create a
beam carrying their upper ends. With paired rafters whose feet tend to spread,
retain the designed lower tie/joist and its connections or select a different
complete mechanism. Removing a tie to gain a vaulted ceiling changes the force
path. A high collar connection and a low tie do not have automatically equivalent
roles; moving a tie upward requires a revised structural proposal, not a label.

A structural ridge beam carries rafter reactions to defined end/intermediate
supports. Show where each support reaches the ground or an actually permitted
support below. This can clear the room's center, but a post in the garden door,
an unsupported beam end or a transfer hidden in a partition defeats that intent.
Distinguish this from rafters/purlins supported on internal posts, walls or
struts: those elements consume the attic or room and must align with allowed
support lines below. Do not adopt the course illustration's post/rafter spacing
as a project prescription.

For a vaulted-room revision compare a retained low tie; an engineered raised-
tie/scissor/truss arrangement with its actual clear envelope; and a supported
ridge/frame strategy. Draw the lowest member and any crossing service at the
most constrained position, not only the attractive ridge section. Keep wall
plates, connections and uplift restraint coordinated; roof boarding is not
automatically a verified diaphragm or a replacement for a removed tie.

## Keep the support system connected in three dimensions

A repeated set of stable transverse frames/trusses can still need longitudinal
and roof-plane restraint. Locate the gravity supports and the lateral paths in
both plan directions; connect the collecting roof/floor plane to the actual
braced/wall/frame lines and their foundations. A rooflight strip, expansion joint
or open corner can interrupt that collection path.

Give a brace or shear wall a real bay and height before placing a door, view or
future removable partition there. Compare moving the opening within its allowed
edge, reserving another bay, using a designed moment frame, or changing the
support organization. Hiding a diagonal on a drawing or calling ordinary infill
structural is not a repair. A tension-only diagonal does not resist the reversed
direction merely because the same line is drawn; reserve the paired/other
resisting mechanism specified for the system.

For a highly glazed pavilion, compare braced opaque service bays with an open
moment-resisting edge and connect them through a developed roof-plane strategy.
Do not concentrate all restraint in one convenient corner and assume the rest
is unaffected: distribution, torsion and diaphragm deformation need structural
assessment. This is not a command for symmetrical architecture. Keep the
movement/joint strategy consistent with facade seals and partitions. Temporary
erection stability and the order in which permanent restraint becomes effective
also need an explicit construction proposal.

## Choose where the thermal boundary follows the roof

Roof profile, supporting mechanism, water-shedding system and conditioned-volume
boundary are separate choices. A pitched outline can enclose a cold attic or an
occupied insulated slope. Keep the control functions in
[envelope and assemblies](../envelope/assemblies.md#assign-control-functions-before-drawing-layer-order);
use the following alternatives to place those functions physically.

| Arrangement | Section logic | What must fit before retaining it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Unconditioned attic above an insulated ceiling | The air/thermal boundary follows the top occupied ceiling and returns to wall insulation; the pitched roof lies outside that conditioned volume. | Continue the boundary at hatch, wall top and penetrations. Reserve access and the chosen attic ventilation/weather strategy without blocking eave routes with insulation. Do not count the cold attic as conditioned usable accommodation. A communal warm attic receiving ventilation air is a different building/services system, not a generic substitute. |
| Insulated occupied roof slope | The conditioned boundary follows the slope and relevant walls; rafters, any above-rafter insulation, lining and chosen ventilation/drainage zones reduce usable volume. | Test the low edge, dormer/rooflight, ridge and eave together. A ventilated cold-side path needs a real inlet, continuous route and outlet; an unvented assembly needs its own developed moisture strategy. Removing a gap to gain headroom does not prove the resulting roof works. |
| Compact roof with insulation above the structural deck | Locate insulation/control layers and falls so the structural deck and internal volume have a coherent enclosure; waterproofing is generally above the insulation in this family. | Reconcile the highest build-up with parapet/opening heights, outlets, fixings and attachment/uplift strategy. A flat soffit does not imply a flat water plane. Protect the control-layer continuity at the perimeter and structural penetrations. |
| Protected membrane / inverted arrangement | The waterproofing lies below suitable moisture-resistant insulation and its protection/retention system. | Allow water to reach and drain at the waterproofing level as the actual system requires; coordinate covers, outlet access and relevant drainage levels. Ballast/pavers/protection add load and height. Covering a membrane can protect it while making inspection and leak access harder; do not infer suitability from a terrace photograph or put any insulation above a membrane. |

Choose for the actual indoor humidity/use, climate, construction moisture,
maintenance and compatible system. No universal vapor-layer side, ventilation
gap, insulation product or thickness is prescribed. A change from exposed roof
to occupied terrace needs a new load, threshold, guarding, drainage and access
study, not just pavers placed on the same section.

## Resolve roof water in plan and at the junction

Mark water-plane high/low points, catchment boundaries, valleys and interruptions
before selecting an outlet location. Follow one water path around a rooflight,
plant curb or high/low roof junction to discharge. Insulation falls, structural
falls and a local cricket can create different depths under the same silhouette.
Adding a curb across a valley without a route around it creates a new low point.

Give the roof membrane, underlay where present, flashing/counterflashing and
wall drainage plane a compatible sequence with end conditions. Keep the support
and movement joint distinct from the visible rain seal. A bead at the face of a
moving glazed head cannot replace the roof-to-wall water/air transition. Use the
[roof-edge and opening methods](../envelope/assemblies.md#develop-four-connected-junctions)
for layer continuity and replacement.

Trace primary discharge and a blocked-outlet scenario against entrances,
inclusive routes, glazing, occupied terraces and neighboring property. Reserve
cleaning and replacement without burying the only access under fixed furniture
or ballast. Outlet count, slope, upstand, drainage capacity and overflow height
require the actual project/system and applicable sources; a course figure or
foreign-book detail supplies none of those values by default.

## Worked revision of a glazed activity pavilion

This is an original hypothetical comparison, not a source detail or a coursework
proposal. Fix an internal plan x=0…12 m, y=0…18 m, finished floor 0, central
activity field x=1…11 (10 m clear), required activity height 4.00 m and highest
finished roof +6.00 m. Reserve the two 1 m side strips for support/enclosure/
interfaces, without claiming every part is usable services space. Keep south
entry and north garden exit centered at x=6. The east edge is intended as glazing;
external buttresses/anchors are not allowed by this **brief**, not by a claimed law.
All following depths are trial vertical reservations, not engineered sizes.

**R0: a shallow arch with an erased reaction.** A proposed arch intrados rises
from +4.10 at its springing to +5.10 at the crown. The sketch labels ordinary
glazed-edge members as supports but gives neither abutment nor tie a place.
Its apparent column-free room has an unresolved horizontal-thrust boundary.
Adding a tie at underside +3.75, depth 0.20, would cross the central field below
the required 4.00 m. Deleting it or renaming it a collar is not a solution.

**R1: retain the arch with an explicit tie envelope.** Compare a tie reservation
with underside +4.05 and top +4.25, connected to a developed springing detail.
The nominal room margin is only 0.05 m; a proposed non-overlapping 0.30 m service
zone below it would leave 3.75 m and fail. Move distribution out of the central
activity field into coordinated side zones, or reopen the support/height scheme.
An arch-depth reservation of 0.45 above the stated crown intrados plus 0.35 of
roof build-up gives **5.10+0.45+0.35=5.90 m**, nominally 0.10 below the roof cap.
The tie/arch connection, deflection, asymmetric loading, out-of-plane restraint
and actual side-zone fit remain unresolved, so R1 is conditional, not approved.

**R2: change mechanism, retain the room and doors.** Compare a truss with lower
member underside +4.10, full structural envelope 1.20 and maximum roof build-up
0.35: highest roof **4.10+1.20+0.35=5.65 m**. These trial dimensions are supplied
for this comparison, not derived from the 12 m span. Nominal activity margin is
0.10 m and roof-cap margin 0.35 m. It keeps an uninterrupted lower chord above
the field, but changes the visible ceiling from an open vault to a deeper band.
Service routes cannot simply cut that chord or a web; locate a coordinated path
and load introduction before accepting plant or distribution within the truss.

Reserve longitudinal restraint in suitable side bays and a connected roof-plane
path without crossing the fixed central entry/exit. If a west opaque/braced edge
and a glazed east frame are compared, retain the east edge's actual structural
role and the need to check torsion/connection to the roof. Glass panes do not
provide the missing restraint. Supports and joints must fit inside the side
reservations; if they do not, the promised 10 m clear width must be redesigned.

**Plan/section/junction repair.** R2 originally sends the whole roof's water to
an outlet immediately above the fixed north garden doorway. Keep the doors and
footprint, but compare a central longitudinal water-plane ridge at x=6 and falls
toward the side edges, with gutters/downpipes at coordinated side locations.
For an explicitly **hypothetical 1.5% study fall** across each 6 m half-width,
rise is `6×0.015=0.09 m`. With the high water/finished plane at +5.65, its low
edge is +5.56; the assumed package above structural top +5.30 varies 0.26…0.35.
That reservation still needs real insulation/deck/drainage design and is not a
selected minimum slope. Give each gutter its own fall, accessible discharge and
blocked-route response; no gutter capacity is inferred from these elevations.

At the glazed east head, support the roof on the structural line, allow the
actual roof/frame movement, return the wall air/thermal functions to the roof,
and carry waterproofing/flashing to the side gutter independently of the glazing
face seal. Show how an outlet can be cleaned and a head closure removed without
loading the glass. The garden approach at x≈6 is no longer the intended discharge
location, but final downpipe/ground discharge and overflow must still be checked
against actual routes and ground levels. A transferred stain/water problem at a
side entrance would invalidate the repair.

The plan ledger is 10×18=180 m² of clear activity plus 2×1×18=36 m² of side
reservations, within the 216 m² shell. Those side reservations are not two extra
rooms or a promise of complete MEP capacity. Choose between R1 and R2 using the
client's room character, height margins, support/joint feasibility, services,
drainage and actual discipline evidence; neither is a certified span solution.

## Source boundary

For timber support topology, the depth of stacked versus face-hung secondaries
and actual fixing/removal access, use the affected [timber joint question](../materials/timber-and-joints.md#choose-support-topology-and-visible-expression-together).
Its bearing/rotation examples do not replace the complete restraint mechanism
above.

The selected P05, Engel and ASK material and exact file/printed-page mappings
are recorded in [sources](../../../sources/library.md#architecture-library-selected-content).
Drawings, force diagrams, code values, product details and span tables were not
copied into this reference. ASK's erroneous shear-based explanation of arch
thrust is not adopted; compression/force-path reasoning was checked against the
two books. Source classifications aid judgment but cannot prove engineering
capacity or override the user's deliverable choice.

- Affects — [Natural-exhaust roof interface](../../performance/comfort/natural-exhaust-topology.md#reserve-shafts-top-floor-and-roof-interfaces): when roof height/shape/outlets change, revisit top-floor column height, terminal exposure, intake/window relationships, penetrations and maintenance; a cowl symbol does not establish upward flow.
- Affects — [Building-volume boundary](../../program/building-volume.md): when the outer roof or conditioned/occupied boundary changes, reconcile its actual solid and metric separately from floor area; equal geometric volume does not establish headroom, thermal performance or support.
