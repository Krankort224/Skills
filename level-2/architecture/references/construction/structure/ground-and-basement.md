# Ground, basement and neighboring support

Use the affected question when a floor, basement, foundation reservation or
neighboring building changes the ground section. Coordinate the architecture
with actual survey, ground/water and structural evidence. These methods do not
select bearing capacity, excavation support, underpinning, pumping or foundation
sizes. A diagrammed influence region is not a measured safe boundary.

## Decide what actually needs to be below ground

Start with the program and its access, not the outline of a foundation. Separate
occupied basement rooms, low crawl/inspection space, lift pit, service trench,
foundation/ground beam and retaining construction. They have different volume,
access, water and maintenance obligations; a dark strip under a section is not
automatically available storage. Establish finished floors, structural undersides,
ground levels, excavation extents and relevant neighboring levels on one datum.
Use actual or explicitly supplied trial build-ups and equipment envelopes.

Compare the underground program with a ground-level support band, a smaller
buried part, a permitted separate service volume or a revised floor/ground
relationship. Recount the space taken from another use, the journey to storage
or equipment and its loading/maintenance access. A smaller basement can reduce
the affected excavation, but a pit or remaining deep corner can still govern
water and neighboring-ground consequences. Do not equate “no basement” with
“no excavation” or “no ground risk.”

| Architectural ground/support arrangement | What it can make possible | Reservation and failure test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Continuous support under bearing-wall lines | Repeated wall/room organization can continue toward the ground. | Openings, offsets and intersecting supports need real continuity. A strip drawn below a wall is not evidence of depth, bearing width or suitability of the ground. |
| Individual column supports linked as required | Can follow a frame with fewer continuous bearing walls. | Bases/ground beams, overlapping support zones, boundary eccentricity and pits can consume the apparently free service space. Do not route a drain through a foundation reservation by default. |
| Continuous raft or basement box concept | Can coordinate a broad support/underground footprint. | Actual actions, ground deformation, water pressure, joints and excavation sequence govern it. A raft is not a generic remedy for weak soil or an automatic waterproof floor. |
| Deep supports with a suspended floor/ground beams | Can separate the occupied floor from an unsuitable near-surface layer when justified by ground/engineering evidence. | Reserve heads, caps, beams, construction access and interfaces to existing buildings. Piles do not make adjacent excavation or differential movement harmless. |
| Raised floor, platform or stepped parts | Can retain a public datum or follow different level bands with less occupied basement. | Raised floors require real support and exposed-underside protection/access; platforms need actual cut/fill/retention; steps need complete ordinary and inclusive journeys. Test these costs rather than hiding them below the floor. |

These are concepts to coordinate with the building system, not a selection table
by soil label. A wall/frame choice changes concentrated versus distributed load
recipients and their positions; ground evidence can in turn change the plan.
Keep the [frame/core action](frame-and-core.md#choose-frame-and-stability-action-with-the-plan)
and upper support lines connected to actual ground reservations.

Write the critical underground section as a physical budget. For a basement,
the underside of the ground floor, services/ceilings, required clear use, basement
floor build-up and excavation/support reservation must all fit. Include the
stair/lift access and service replacement route. A pit below the lowest landing
and an overhead zone above the highest landing belong to the selected equipment,
not to a universal lift outline; use the [equipment envelope](frame-and-core.md#fit-vertical-equipment-and-maintenance-with-the-core).
Do not infer groundwater level or drainage feasibility from the elevation of
the court, basement floor or a nearby ditch.

## Map neighboring ground and construction dependencies

Draw existing and proposed walls, actual or unknown support footprints/depths,
excavation edges, retained soil, utilities, access boundaries and construction
working space in plan and section. Record which information is surveyed, supplied
or still unknown. Ground can transmit changes beyond the new footprint even when
new concrete does not touch old concrete. A neighboring deep-supported building
and a shallow-supported extension can move differently; one pile symbol cannot
establish compatibility of the pair.

Treat a deeper nearby excavation, different support levels, changed ground/water
conditions or an uncertain old foundation as a coupled design question. Compare:

1. Omit or reduce the underground program, placing its uses and access elsewhere.
2. Reposition the buried volume, pit or support line within authorized land and
   recount the resulting program, routes and ground section. Greater separation
   is a geometry change, not proof that an influence region has been escaped.
3. Retain the location conditionally with a designed ground/support intervention,
   real working/access space, a feasible construction sequence and appropriate
   neighbor investigation/agreement. Reserve their architectural consequences;
   do not invent a shoring or underpinning scheme.
4. Separate parts with an actual movement strategy where required, coordinating
   structure, envelope, floors and services. A drawn joint does not stop soil
   movement or compensate for an unstable excavation.

Reject fixed-angle or fixed-distance “safe excavation” rules from schematic
teaching figures. The relevant region and acceptable deformation depend on the
actual ground, water, support and construction. Groundwater control can affect
neighboring ground and needs its own evidence and permissions; a sump symbol
does not provide a discharge right or remove those consequences.

Read the intended sequence without prescribing temporary works: what must be
investigated/protected before the cut; where equipment/materials can stand; which
permanent supports/retaining parts exist at each stage; how access is maintained;
and when below-ground surfaces become inaccessible. If the only working strip
lies on another property or the proposed excavation removes the only entrance,
revise the footprint/sequence or identify the unresolved right before claiming
the concept buildable. The final building's stability is not evidence for an
unsupported intermediate stage.

## Keep support, soil retention and water control distinct

At a buried wall distinguish gravity support, lateral soil/water actions,
waterproofing/water-control continuity, insulation where applicable, protection
and drainage. One part may perform several roles only with actual design
evidence. Waterproofing does not retain soil; an ordinary wall label does not
prove retaining capacity; a drain does not remove all water pressure or replace
a suitable water-control strategy. Foundations and retaining elements remain
connected to the structural proposal, not just to a finish detail.

Trace separately surface runoff, groundwater and water from use/services. Show
the intended receptor, access for cleaning/repair, overflow/failure consequence
and whether active operation is required. Do not assume a gravity outlet below
the basement floor, a permanent groundwater level, reliable pumping or legal
discharge. If water-control feasibility changes the occupied basement, change
the architectural proposal rather than append “provide drainage.”

Develop wall-to-floor, wall-to-exterior-grade, construction/movement joints and
penetrations as connected interfaces, using the
[envelope control-function owner](../envelope/assemblies.md#develop-four-connected-junctions).
Protect the chosen water-control system during works and show where it can be
installed, inspected and repaired. An exterior system beside a property boundary
may lack the working space assumed by an open-site detail. An interior finish
can conceal a leak without resolving the route by which water enters.

Coordinate a low court, lightwell or service recess with the real threshold,
upstand, collection and overflow geometry. A window symbol does not establish
light, escape or an acceptable occupied basement. Keep entrances, lift pits and
inspection openings outside the intended roof/paving discharge path; if a low
point cannot drain by the assumed means, revise its level, exposure or use.
Return changed public levels to [site and arrival](../../context/site/site-and-context.md#let-topography-and-servicing-shape-the-section).
