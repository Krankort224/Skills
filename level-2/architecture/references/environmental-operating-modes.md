# Environmental operating states and inclusive control

Use this method when a room appears satisfactory in separate light, air, noise
or access sketches but may not work in its **actual occupied state**. Its unit is
one room and its opening/control/route interface, not a whole-building control
strategy. It develops the simultaneous condition, a physical correction and a
credible fallback. A state diagram does not establish environmental performance.

The causal mechanisms remain with [daylight, view and occupied
comfort](performance-and-coordination.md#daylight-view-and-occupied-comfort),
[acoustic paths and ventilation
choices](performance-and-coordination.md#acoustic-paths-and-ventilation-choices)
and [inclusive/fire spatial
coordination](performance-and-coordination.md#inclusive-use-and-fire-spatial-coordination).
Use those methods for the relevant light or sound path; use this one to determine
whether the resulting provisions can be used together. Opening geometry and
support remain with [walls, floors and
openings](wall-floor-and-opening.md#develop-a-wall-opening-as-support-and-use);
weather and control-layer continuity remain with the [opening
junction](envelope-and-assemblies.md#opening-head-jamb-and-sill).

## Define the occupied condition and its dependencies

Start with the activity, people and occupied periods. Distinguish scheduled
capacity from people actually present, including a visitor, interpreter,
companion or assistant. Record whether a person can leave or change position
independently and whether privacy, face recognition, listening, sustained visual
work or an outward view is essential to that activity. One person's preferred
shade position can prevent another person's task; room-wide control is a shared
decision, even when the button is reachable.

Use supplied project facts and their existing owners. If exposure is unknown,
write a condition to investigate, such as “traffic prevents the intended quiet
conversation with this opening open”; do not invent a measured level or design
temperature. Collect only the inputs that can change this interface:

| Input | Required distinction and architectural consequence |
| --- | --- |
| Outdoor condition during occupation | Sun direction/height and cloud, temperature/humidity, wind/rain, noise, pollution and security can prohibit different devices at the same time. A hot, noisy period can require both solar control and closed-window outdoor air; “summer open” is insufficient |
| Task and people | Position, eye/task height, furniture in use, communication and privacy requirement; mobility/assistive equipment, reach, dexterity, strength, vision, hearing, comprehension and preferences. Do not replace actual users with one standard wheelchair silhouette |
| Plan and section | Finished dimensions, neighboring windows/paths, sill/head/reveal, frame and clear opening, leaf/shade sweeps, furniture/occupant envelopes, support/piers, ceiling/services and route to the door. A seated sightline and an overhead leaf need different sections |
| Available services | Actual outdoor-air supply and extract/return routes, heating/cooling provision, power, lighting and verified controls; who determines readiness and acceptable occupation. A fan symbol or recirculating cooling unit does not establish outdoor air |
| Devices and dependencies | Independently movable bands or one coupled device, parked/deployed/partly deployed geometry, power and manual operation, locking/restriction, feedback, wind/rain response, cleaning and repair access. Product behavior remains unknown until evidenced |
| Operating responsibility | Who may request/change a state, whether staff are actually available, response delay, locked hours and who may take a failed device out of use. A remote app or a key held elsewhere can make a physically reachable device unavailable |
| Safety boundaries | Door/route and assistance space, required separation and opening restrictions, smoke/fire strategy and permitted maintenance closures. Establish actual requirements through [requirements](requirements.md), not through this state method |

Keep four things distinct: the desired condition, the commanded device position,
the observed position and the resulting environment. “Blind down” can be observed;
“comfortable” or “private” requires different evidence. Record a mismatch rather
than allowing a command indicator to stand for successful movement or a healthy
ventilation path.

## Develop states that can actually coexist

1. **Select decisive combinations.** Include ordinary occupied use, simultaneous
   adverse conditions, partial deployment/closure, changeover, unavailable or
   failed equipment, and maintenance. Add emergency operation only through the
   project's actual fire strategy. Choose combinations from the activity/exposure
   pattern; a huge list of theoretical permutations can hide the decisive case.
2. **Fix people before devices.** Put the same occupied task positions and
   movement/assistance envelopes on each compared plan and section. If a repair
   removes a seat or requires somebody to move, identify the changed demand and
   obtain its project decision through the existing brief owner. A vacant room
   that can be purged between sessions does not demonstrate occupied air supply.
3. **Describe the whole state.** For each selected combination state opening and
   shade positions by band, air path and its availability, heating/cooling and
   lighting dependencies, privacy/view consequence, control/approach position,
   permitted occupation and residual uncertainty. Include the internal door's
   position; do not silently use it open for air and closed for speech privacy.
4. **Overlay operation in plan and section.** Draw the deployed shade, partly
   open leaf, handles, operator, companion, occupied furniture, air route,
   maintenance envelope and protected movement/support zones. Trace rays through
   the opening that remains after closure. Do not credit an upper daylight band
   that the selected sun-control state covers. A partial opening can change
   weather, acoustic and air behavior without providing any of their required
   performance; area fraction is not an airflow or sound-isolation result.
5. **Expose incompatible claims.** Write the conflict as a concrete dependency:
   “privacy closes the only seated-view band”; “wind stows the sole external
   solar control”; “the quieter air route ends when the separating door closes”;
   “the operator occupies the only clear passage.” Separate a geometric collision
   from a performance unknown and from a genuine brief contradiction.
6. **Change a physical choice and retest the same condition.** Compare separate
   control bands, a local screened activity, rotated/relocated furniture,
   another facade/room allocation, a differently operating leaf, a separate air
   path or a larger control/service recess. Retain support and continuity zones.
   Time-sharing is an alternative only if the activity permits it and the
   changeover itself is workable. Added automation changes responsibilities,
   feedback and failure behavior; it does not remove the spatial test.
7. **State the retained and unavailable modes.** Distinguish a complete reserved
   path awaiting specialist verification from a verified usable mode. If every
   permitted air path or necessary control is unavailable, the result is a
   relocation/closure condition, not an invented safe occupancy or duration.

The comparison should make a choice possible. For example, split blinds can
retain some sky while sacrificing the lower view; a fixed screen avoids motor
failure but may impose that sacrifice permanently; an offset bay can separate
view and overlooking directions but consumes depth/frontage and changes support,
cleaning and weather interfaces. A separate quiet air route costs service space
and depends on equipment/acoustic/fire design. None is universally best.

## Make changeover usable and failure explicit

Follow one actual person from noticing a problem through reaching a control,
understanding it, operating it, confirming the response and recovering from a
mistake. Then repeat with a seated mobility-device user, a person with limited
reach or hand function, a person who relies on nonvisual feedback, and an
assistant where relevant. Select actual user participation and specialist advice
to suit the project. A mounting-height check alone cannot establish usability.

- **Approach and act:** reserve a stable approach/working position and any needed
  companion position outside the passage. Check furniture occupied, doors open,
  foot/knee and lateral reach, grip/force, hold time, glare on the panel and the
  possibility of using it without stretching across glass or hot equipment.
  Prefer a local control point connected to an otherwise unreachable device over
  a standing-on-furniture routine. Connection, actuation and safe restriction
  still require a feasible selected mechanism.
- **Understand and confirm:** relate each control to the actual window/band;
  use clear wording, consistent direction and distinguishable controls. Do not
  rely on color, a beep or a screen alone. Provide suitable visual/tactile and
  other feedback, without claiming a chosen cue serves every sensory need.
  Distinguish “opening closed,” “outside air available” and “service required.”
  Use mock-up/user trials to resolve comprehension, error and response questions.
- **Share authority:** agree how a private-session request and another person's
  view/air request are resolved. Local zones or a second suitable position can
  reduce conflict; repeatedly asking an assistant to override another user's
  control is an operating burden. Staff assistance is an explicitly staffed
  alternative with delay and privacy consequences, not assumed independence.
- **Change state:** identify the trigger, request/authority, ordered actions,
  observable confirmation and fallback. A proposed closed-window changeover
  needs an available alternative air path before continued occupation is credited.
  Physical control interlocks, sensor locations and acceptance criteria belong
  to the relevant specialists. Do not specify generic thresholds or assume
  simultaneous open windows and cooling are harmless or always prohibited.
- **Lose a function:** distinguish power loss, jammed shade, failed actuator,
  ambiguous feedback, missing key, disabled app and plant out of service. A
  manual override must itself be reachable, operable and safe in the failed
  condition. State which actual functions remain; do not call equipment
  “fail-safe” without the project-specific safety requirement and evidence.
- **Maintain and return:** reserve inspection/cleaning and component removal
  positions, isolate the affected operation as required by the actual system,
  and identify whether occupation must stop. A maintenance user, ladder or
  component cannot be assumed to share a route with passing users. Confirm
  restored movement, settings and feedback before reinstating the mode.

Trace daily and emergency controls separately where their functions differ.
Comfort opening, door hold-open, shading and security devices must not bypass a
required separating door, obstruct a safety route or substitute for an agreed
smoke-control system. Fire cause-and-effect, release/locking, power resilience,
evacuation assistance and permitted overrides need the actual fire/accessibility
strategy. Leave its decisions open rather than programming a universal “all
windows open” or “all doors unlock” response.

## Worked revision of a shared advisory room

### Fixed study inputs and first failure

This is an original, hypothetical room fragment for two people doing visual work
or a private seated conversation; an interpreter/assistant may also be present.
It is not a healthcare, confidential-speech or ventilation-capacity design.
During a private session the study brief prioritizes preventing the specified
external sightline over a seated outward view; whether that temporary view loss
is acceptable remains a project decision. Another use could require both and
reject this room. The external court can be noisy during events; a supplied
scenario also combines sun, warmth, wind and that noise. No climate, site,
jurisdiction, exposure level, safe duration or product rating is assumed.

All following dimensions and angles are **supplied original study assumptions**,
not source dimensions, climatic design conditions or norms. Coordinates are in
metres relative to finished floor: `x` across a 4.80 m room, `y` from the court
glazing at 0 to the rear wall at 4.20, and `z` upward. The room/door fragment is
bounded; the continuation beyond the rear entry needs its own journey check.

- Preserve the facade rough opening at `x = 0.60–4.20`, sill `z = 0.75`, head
  `z = 2.70`, with the existing assumed support/piers outside it. Frame dimensions
  and capacity are not established. Do not widen it to repair an operating issue.
- Reserve an east-side movement strip `x = 3.60–4.80` from front to rear entry.
  Its 1.20 m width is a study reservation, not an accessibility/egress minimum.
  Rear door/approach, upstream route and evacuation capacity are unverified.
- The original full-height inward leaf has a proposed operating envelope
  `x = 3.40–4.15, y = 0–0.75`, including partial opening and its sweep. This
  envelope overlaps the reserved strip by 0.55 m across `x`; that is a reserved
  envelope conflict, not a constant residual passage width. A continuous 0.65 m
  deep facade bench obstructs the
  approach to the original high handle. The original single blind covers the
  entire opening when either privacy or glare control is requested.
- The revised worktable is `x = 0.80–3.20, y = 1.55–2.35`; the reference seated
  eye is at `y = 2.50, z = 1.15`, and the reference task at `y = 1.80, z = 0.75`.
  The two section tests below use `x = 2.00` throughout.
  Occupied chairs and assistance positions need their own envelopes; table
  dimensions do not prove a three-person accessible fit. No third fixed seat is
  credited merely because an assistant can enter.

The original “open for air, blind down for privacy, court view retained, clear
route” description contains three conflicts: the open leaf invades the strip;
the blind removes the view; and an open court aperture contradicts the intended
quiet session during the assumed noisy event. An unreachable handle adds an
independent control failure. These are identifiable without predicting sound,
temperature, illumination or airflow.

### Compare physical alternatives

| Alternative | Physical revision and gain | Cost or unresolved condition |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A: fixed opening and independent closed-window air path | Remove leaf sweep; provide separate upper/lower shades, relocate controls and reserve supply/return through the rear service edge | Gives up user opening completely; every occupied period depends on the engineered air provision. Fixed glazing still needs an actual cleaning/replacement method |
| B: fixed lower view glass plus independently operated high vent | Retain optional suitable-condition opening, remove the original low leaf/bench collision, split shading and relocate controls; reserve the same complete closed-window air path | Adds actuator/restriction, seals, frame divisions and maintenance. Upper vent can compete with shade/light space; noise or weather can make it unavailable. This is the conditional choice developed below |
| C: relocate private activity behind a local screen or to another room | Can retain an unshaded view position for another activity; changes the occupied sightline rather than covering all glass | Screen/turning/assistant envelopes consume this small room; a displaced person must have an equivalent usable position and route. It changes room allocation and still needs light/air/quiet evidence. If simultaneous private outward view is essential, develop an offset opening/other room rather than claiming split blinds solve it |

### Revise plan, section and control together

For B remove the facade bench, fix the lower glass and reserve a high inward vent
within `x = 0.60–3.40, z = 2.10–2.70`, with the remaining width fixed and an
assumed maximum 0.20 m inward projection. Its operation stays outside the east
strip in plan; height alone is
not the reason it avoids that strip. Verify the actual leaf, hinges, limiter,
actuator, head-impact/entrapment risk and maintenance operation with selected
system data. No opening area, frame fit, guarding or safe opening claim follows
from this reservation. Keep the lower/upper shading control break at `z = 1.60`.
Separate the lower privacy screen from external upper solar control, including
their storage, guides, frame attachment and replacement space. Preserve support,
head drainage and frame seals; a concealed shade box must not erase the lintel
or required service/insulation zone.

Move the control point to the rear wall at `x = 3.00`, nominal `z = 1.10`, with a
study approach bay `x = 2.40–3.60, y = 2.70–4.20`, entered from the east strip.
Remove shelving formerly occupying that bay rather than placing a panel behind
it. The bay is separate from the through strip and table; it is not a verified
turning, reach or assistance envelope. Confirm actual users can approach, operate
and leave it while another person passes, including any companion and rear door
approach. The short control wall/rear wall must carry the panel/connections; do
not route them by cutting an unexamined pier or separating construction.

Use separately identified lower-screen, upper-shade and vent requests with
observable position/status feedback and a distinct service indication. Reserve
the connection to the high device and local actuation/override mechanism; a
button at 1.10 m does not prove the upper mechanism is feasible. Test wording,
force, precision, hold time, visibility/tactile identification and erroneous
selection with actual users. If an override requires climbing, the independent
opening mode remains unavailable to that user until repaired. A phone-only
interface does not replace this local use test.

Reserve room supply and return/extract connections at the rear service edge to
an actual services route. Keep the door closed during the private/noisy state;
neither the east movement strip nor its rear door is credited as an air-transfer
path. Specialists must establish outdoor-air source, routes, terminal and plant
space, acoustic/separation treatment, controls and capacity for the full actual
occupied demand. Heating/cooling and task lighting are separate dependencies.
Do not call this complete merely because the rear edge is marked “services.”

### Test the partial closures rather than the nominal glass

For the specified observer at `y = −1.50, z = 1.65`, the line to the seated eye
at `y = 2.50, z = 1.15` crosses the glazing at
`1.15 + (1.65 − 1.15) × 2.50 / 4.00 = 1.4625 m`.
A lower screen covering `z = 0.75–1.50` intercepts that one line. It also removes
the seated horizontal view at 1.15 m. It does not prove privacy from a taller,
nearer, lateral or elevated observer, nor speech confidentiality. A standing eye
at 1.65 m facing an observer at that height still has a line through the upper
band; standing private activity would require a different state or arrangement.

Under a second original assumption, a facade-normal sun ray at 30° altitude
reaches the reference task through the glazing at
`0.75 + 1.80 × tan(30°) ≈ 1.789 m`. Closing only the lower privacy screen to
1.50 m does not intercept that ray. Deploying an opaque upper shade across
`z = 1.60–2.70` intercepts it in this ideal section, leaving only the unshaded
0.10 m band from 1.50 to 1.60. Do not label that residual strip useful daylight
or a satisfactory outward view. Finite width, side gaps, oblique sun, frame,
reflections and the actual device must also be tested in plan; neither line test
predicts glare, heat gain or annual light. The masks in this test are idealized
at the glazing plane; actual shade stand-off/reveal geometry also changes them.
A raised/lowered partial shade changes
the answer, so give the tested edge position rather than saying “half closed.”

### Retain only credible occupied states

| Actual condition | Concurrent state of the revised room | Consequence and next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Quiet, suitable outside conditions; no private session | Lower screen open, upper shade as required, optional restricted high vent, suitable lighting and verified air strategy | View opportunity returns; verify leaf/shade clearances and actual air/weather suitability. An open high vent alone is not demonstrated ventilation |
| Noisy court, seated private session and the assumed sun ray | High vent closed; lower screen to 1.50, upper shade deployed, complete closed-window outdoor-air provision available, suitable task lighting/thermal provision | The tested external seated sightline and ray are intercepted. View is largely lost and light/thermal/acoustic outcomes remain unverified. Retain this state only if temporary view loss is accepted and specialist checks support occupied use |
| Cold/wet or dark occupied period, possibly also noisy | Vent closed where weather/noise requires; outdoor-air and heating provision available; shades set for actual sun/privacy, with task lighting | Do not force a window open to conceal missing outdoor air. Inspect drafts, surface temperatures/moisture and night-time internal reflections/visibility with the actual system and observers. A daytime sightline test does not establish night privacy or cold-weather comfort |
| Same demand, wind requires upper external shade to stow | High vent remains closed for noise; lower screen remains private; upper solar control is unavailable | Do not keep the earlier sun/thermal claim. Compare an independently supported interior glare-control layer, relocating the task or suspending that use. An interior layer can cost more sky/view and cannot inherit the external device's thermal performance |
| Shade jams down after privacy is no longer needed | Air path and lights may remain available; view and daylight remain obstructed; local feedback identifies the fault | Continued use depends on the activity/actual light and thermal evidence. Provide another equivalent position if required; have maintenance restore the shade. A working air system does not repair the missing view |
| Alternative air system fails while event noise/weather prevents opening | Closed-window air mode unavailable; opening mode also unavailable for the stated demand | Relocate or suspend occupied use through the actual operating plan. No safe duration or reduced headcount is derived here. Restart only after the required air provision is restored/verified, or after the conditions and verified permitted mode change |
| Cleaning, actuator or shade repair | Front working zone and transport path reserved, affected device isolated as required by actual system, normally room unavailable to visitors | Front floor space is not proof of safe high-level access. Confirm work platform/support, component handling, route and return-to-service tests; a maintenance closure must not obstruct the building's continuing safety route |

An ordinary-to-noisy changeover therefore requests/establishes the available
closed-window air provision, closes and confirms the vent, sets the two shade
bands and confirms the task/view compromise with the users. The environmental
specialist sets actual system sequence and readiness evidence; this order only
exposes the dependency. If readiness is absent, the state cannot be credited as
occupied. A conflict between two users over privacy/view can require separate
activity times or another suitable room; priority is not inferred from who can
press the control first.

The revision preserves the facade support hypothesis and removes the original
low sweep/bench obstruction, at the cost of storage, service space, view during
private sun-exposed use, and more devices. It does not establish that the high
vent, three-person occupation, control bay or rear entry is adequate. If devices,
services or user envelopes do not fit, compare A or C and change the room/opening
allocation explicitly; do not shrink their required space to retain B.

## Check the retained interface and its limits

Check the smallest evidence that can overturn the decision, using the same
occupied positions and demand across alternatives:

| Check | Evidence needed / repair when it fails |
| --- | --- |
| Geometry across states | Dimensioned plan and section with actual frame, leaf, shades, table/chairs, people/assistive equipment, bay, rear door and maintenance/transport envelopes. If an operator/companion blocks the route, move/enlarge the bay or refit the room, not just the control height |
| Light, view and privacy together | Test front/rear/seated/standing and relevant outside observers with actual band positions; obtain appropriate daylight/glare assessment and lighting provision. If view is required during privacy and cannot survive, change geometry/activity allocation or report the brief contradiction |
| Thermal and air behavior | Actual climate, exposure, occupation, heat gains, solar-control states and air/thermal-system availability; specialist capacity, comfort, ventilation and moisture checks. If wind or failure removes the only adequate path/control, revise the fallback or make the affected use unavailable |
| Sound and communication | Actual source/time, closed/partly open opening, facade/duct/flanking paths and occupied listening/communication needs; acoustic and user evaluation. Absorption, a screen and closed windows are not a verified confidentiality or hearing-support solution |
| Inclusive control and use | Mock-up/user trial of approach, comprehension, effort, response, feedback, recovery and assistance for actual users. If feedback or authorization prevents independent use, change the interface/authority or explicitly provide a staffed alternative with an acceptable response |
| Support and continuity | Selected opening/shade/actuator support, movement, air/water/thermal transitions and repair access. Route connections around protected support/separation zones; engineered changes require their own assessment |
| Daily versus safety operation | Actual fire/accessibility strategy, separation penetrations, door/route/assistance continuity, security and failure priorities. Do not treat comfort controls, the high vent or a normal lift as safety equipment without the applicable evidence |

Record the retained option, the decisive simultaneous state, demonstrated
geometric corrections, accepted functional sacrifices and exact unresolved
checks in the project's existing design decision/output. Give specialist
questions a position, demand and state: for example, “confirm closed-window air
and thermal provision for two users plus an interpreter during noisy sun-exposed
sessions with both screens deployed,” rather than “check comfort.” No new
project register, simulation suite or engineering calculator is required.

## Source and applicability boundary

Selected Neufert mechanisms inform distinguishing light/view, changing sun and
obstruction, adjustable shade/ventilation interaction, and occupied approach,
reach and maneuvering. Exact identities, individual file/printed locators,
reading extents, adoption and exclusions belong in [sources](sources.md).
The integrated state procedure, room alternatives, coordinate geometry,
changeover/failure reasoning and control trial are original bounded synthesis.
No source room, image, schedule or dimensional table is reproduced.

The selected 2011 Russian translation of a German edition contains dated DIN,
energy and building-rule references, example climate/sun positions, numerical
access dimensions and material/assembly values. None becomes a current local
obligation, climatic default, product rating, comfort target or performance
result here. Source accessibility diagrams are spatial prompts, not proof of
coverage of all disabilities. The work remains partial architectural operating
coordination; actual climate/environmental engineering, accessibility,
confidential speech, controls commissioning and fire strategy require their
project-specific sources, users and specialists. Apply [requirements](requirements.md)
before adopting any normative conclusion.
