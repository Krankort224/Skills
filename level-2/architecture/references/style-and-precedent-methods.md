# Style, precedent and adaptation

Use this reference when a named movement, architect or building must inform a
new decision. The unit is an architectural relationship and its consequences,
not a copied project or an image resemblance. For a requested character without
a named reference, start with [style into decisions](composition/styles/character-to-decisions.md#how-can-a-requested-style-become-design-decisions).
For the selected knowledge perimeter and individual cases, use the
[direction selector](style-directions.md). Read only the relevant direction and
case; the whole corpus is not an induction exercise.

## Read an object before classifying it

**Decision:** What can this particular reference teach about organization,
experience and making, and what does the evidence actually establish?

**Inputs:** Identified building and intervention or version; architect and
collaborators where verified; actual source/page/view; the current architectural
question. Original work, later alteration, restoration, reconstruction and
present operation can have different authors and dates. Retain that distinction
where it changes the interpretation. A famous name does not fill an unknown.

1. Establish identity before inference. Record the source's building name,
   location, authorship and date meaning: design, construction, completion or
   later intervention. Preserve a meaningful disagreement rather than choosing
   the date that makes a movement boundary look tidy. A heritage inscription or
   visitor opening is not necessarily the construction date.
2. Name exactly what was inspected. A plan can support adjacency or enclosure;
   a section can support vertical relationships; a photograph can show a view,
   surface or visible joint. Text may *report* a plan arrangement without the
   reader having seen its geometry. A linked uninspected drawing is a future
   source, not an observation. Check whether the view represents the original
   work or a changed condition before treating it as evidence of authorship.
3. Follow a person through the actual evidence: approach, threshold, first
   decision, principal space, subsidiary space and outward relationship. Identify
   what is shown, what the text reports and which connecting portion is missing.
   Do not turn a selected ceremonial view into proof of all public, accessible,
   service or escape journeys.
4. Read plan, section and enclosure together where they are available. Ask what
   makes a space central or peripheral; what repeats and what changes; where
   movement enters, crosses, turns or bypasses a room; and whether the apparent
   facade order corresponds to occupied bays or overlays different functions.
   For actual mechanisms use [spatial relationships](spatial-composition.md),
   [section and form](composition/massing/section-form.md#how-can-section-generate-form)
   and [occupied elevation bays](composition/facade/occupied-bays.md#derive-an-elevation-from-occupied-bays).
5. Distinguish visible expression from physical action. A heavy-looking wall
   need not carry the floor; an apparently continuous surface may hide joints;
   exposed concrete does not establish a frame's complete stability system.
   Use verified construction evidence for a structural statement. Otherwise
   describe the visible support/material *expression* and leave action open.
6. Relate the significant context to the mechanism: patronage/use and public
   address, available craft and production, urban grain, changed social program,
   or the architect's stated intention. History should explain an observed
   choice or its conditions. Do not substitute a biography, invented intention
   or present-day judgment for evidence of the original project.
7. Only then test a classification. Separate a source's explicit designation,
   the analyst's qualified comparison and an uncertain label. A vault, curve,
   colonnade, white wall or rough surface alone does not identify a movement.
   Several readings may illuminate a building without becoming equivalent labels.

The useful result is a short causal statement, for example: a distinct public
threshold makes entry legible while a lateral route preserves the central room.
It must say what evidence supports the relationship and what remains unknown.
“Organic,” “monumental” or “transparent” alone does not describe a mechanism.

**Alternatives:** Read an individual object in detail; compare two objects with
the same question; or compare a source's intended state with a documented later
condition. The first exposes dependencies, the second variation, and the third
change over time. None permits inventing the missing object or phase.

**Check:** Each consequential claim is identifiable as observed, source-reported,
interpretive or a new project assumption. If a plan is unavailable, keep claims
that need its geometry unresolved while using the supported formal/material
evidence. Source absence limits that claim; it need not halt unrelated design.

## Separate a family from its variations

**Decision:** Which relationships form a useful direction, and which belong to
one building, region, date or architect?

Start from the relevant [direction profile](style-directions.md), not from an
imagined universal style recipe. Use its causal features as propositions to test.
A movement name, a period and a building type classify different things. A
contemporary library can use an ordered threshold without becoming an ancient
temple; a building completed later can continue an earlier design language.

Identify the discriminating relationships, not every visible feature. Compare
the direction's organization of principal and subsidiary space; continuity or
articulation of the route; repetition and hierarchy; depth of the enclosing edge;
and the relationship of making to appearance. Then identify variation within the
direction: centralized or longitudinal organization, regular or exceptional bay,
abstract or expressive detail, urban front or isolated object. Use only the
variants actually supported by the selected profile and case evidence.

Ask which observed features depend on the original conditions. A ceremonial axis
may depend on a single dominant destination; a continuous gallery may depend on
shared public access; an integrated craft surface may depend on bespoke making
and future repair. Those conditions explain both the value and the transfer
limit. An unsupported second example is not evidence that all members vary that
way. Name the actual corpus limit rather than expanding its classification.

**Counterexample:** Symmetry and a central entrance do not establish Renaissance
authorship. Nor does a curved tiled roof alone establish Catalan Modernisme.
An object may share one device with a direction and differ in spatial ordering,
construction, social setting or documented attribution. Describe the selected
device without assigning the whole new design an unsupported historical identity.

**Check:** A direction can generate more than one coherent scheme, and a scheme
can reject a historically common device when it conflicts with its brief. If the
direction dictates every plan regardless of use, the interpretation is too rigid.

## Transfer a mechanism into a changed brief

**Decision:** What relationship should survive the transfer, what must change,
and what concrete architectural correction follows?

**Inputs:** One supported reference mechanism; the new brief's fixed activities,
site/approach, climate/jurisdiction if known, actual dimensional basis, budget and
maintenance conditions; the accepted design baseline and allowed revision scope.
Do not infer those inputs from the style name or the source building's location.

First state the mechanism without its iconic shape: “a sheltered intermediate
edge gives a gradual public-to-private transition” is transferable; “repeat this
roof and its dimensions” is a reproduction proposal. Then identify the mechanism's
dependencies: depth and approach, repeated room access, support or fixing space,
daylight/view, control of water and air, local craft and replacement access.
Use [local revision](methods/bounded-revision.md#how-should-a-bounded-revision-or-comparison-be-judged)
to preserve unaffected decisions and bound the changes.

Work through the changed condition that matters:

| Change | Preserve if it serves the brief | Rework and verify |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Program or operator | A hierarchy, sequence or relationship of common and quiet space | Actual adjacencies, supervision, independent hours, goods/service and private access; a shared route must not cross a fixed private activity. |
| Site or approach | A legible threshold or an intentionally framed outside relationship | Entry location, slope and continuous user journeys. Do not place a ceremonial centre where the actual approach cannot reach it. |
| Climate or exposure | An intermediate edge, layered enclosure or a controlled contrast of light | Deployed shade, weather/drainage and thermal boundaries, view/privacy and simultaneous air/noise states. A source image proves no performance in the new place. |
| Scale or capacity | A meaningful part-to-whole hierarchy and readable rhythm | Occupied room/bay dimensions, walking and service distance, frontage length and changes of level; enlarging every dimension preserves neither human scale nor operation. |
| Budget or making | The intended depth, rhythm, hierarchy or material relationship | Feasible repeatable component families, actual joints/stock/cuts and access, supplier capability, construction stages and repair. A printed texture does not replicate the source's spatial or structural work. |
| Retained fabric | The evidence-supported relationship that can coexist with it | Actual substrate, support, openings and occupied intervention states; stylistic continuity does not establish permission or capacity to alter existing work. |

Develop at least two real translations where a choice matters. One may retain
the sequence while changing the enclosure; another may retain a layered edge
while changing the route or rhythm. Explain what each gives up. Place the change
in the affected plan, section, opening, bay or occupied state; an adjectives-only
proposal is not an adaptation. Link to [environmental states](environmental-operating-modes.md#develop-states-that-can-actually-coexist),
[envelope interfaces](envelope-and-assemblies.md) or [operation over time](operation-and-lifecycle.md)
only when that dependency is active.

Test the causal claim against a counterexample. A screen may enrich facade depth
but obstruct the only usable control; a central gathering space may strengthen
hierarchy but become the sole passage to a quiet room; an apparently continuous
surface may conceal inaccessible replacement joints. Change the conflicting
relationship, not only its color. If no permitted revision resolves a fixed
conflict, report the incompatible conditions and continue independent work.

**Check:** The proposal explains what was learned, why it serves the new use,
what physically changed, what was deliberately not transferred, and which
project/product/specialist inputs are still required. A new route or joint that
looks plausible remains conditional until its actual geometry and operation are
established. Do not claim compliance, tested comfort or historical authenticity.

## Compare directions through the same brief

**Decision:** Which interpretation best serves the current request, and which
differences actually change the architecture?

Keep the same activities, site facts, area meaning, fixed constraints and
evaluation horizon. Select only relevant directions/cases through the
[selector](style-directions.md). Compare their different choices about hierarchy,
route, section, occupied edge and making. The objective is not a beauty contest
between periods. Use [consistent comparison](computational-design.md#compare-options-consistently)
for common feasibility and uncertainty, not an invented universal style score.

**Original bounded threshold study.** A supplied rectangular entrance fragment
is 4.00 m wide. The client's minimum uninterrupted transverse route is 1.20 m;
this is a hypothetical project requirement, not a legal minimum. A proposed
symmetrical pair of display zones each projects 1.50 m from opposite sides.
At that section the remaining width is 4.00−1.50−1.50 = 1.00 m, failing by
0.20 m. The symmetry's promise of a clear centre is contradicted by its use.

- **Retain bilateral order:** reduce each display projection to 1.40 m;
  4.00−2×1.40 = 1.20 m nominally meets the supplied transverse width. Check actual
  plinths, handles, people viewing, door motion, construction tolerance and the
  rest of the journey before calling the route usable. The nominal equality
  leaves no unallocated width at that section; displays lose depth.
- **Retain hierarchy with unequal parts:** place one 1.50 m display against one
  side and use the opposite wall as a shallow visual datum without a projecting
  display. With zero opposite projection in this study, the section leaves
  2.50 m. A repeated datum and a clearly emphasized destination can give order
  without mirrored occupation. Actual shallow elements must be deducted if added;
  this arrangement reduces display provision and changes the balance.

Neither choice is intrinsically a named historical style. A verified case can
explain why an axis, rhythm, layered edge or asymmetric balance matters; it does
not supply the new width. Compare the lost display function and desired entry
experience as well as geometry. The second option's larger transverse space does
not establish that turns, waiting, wheelchair maneuvering or emergency access
work. Those interfaces need their own project evidence.

The useful comparison reports the physical difference, affected users and tradeoff,
not “classical is rigid” versus “modern is free.” A direction can accommodate
several translations; an unsupported stereotype can reject a viable solution.

**Failure tests:** Can two alternatives be distinguished in plan/section or actual
material/interface operation without their labels? Does either silently change a
fixed demand, borrow an unknown source dimension, assume a source climate or
omit a price/maintenance consequence? Resolve those failures before a preference.

## Sources and reproduction boundary

This procedure is original synthesis extending the existing composition method.
Selected P15 preface/introduction distinguishes learning architectural
relationships from reproducing projects and connects spatial order with movement,
technology, program and context. Exact identity, file/printed-page reading extent
and exclusions are in [sources](sources.md). It does not establish historical
classifications or verify every case in this corpus. Direction profiles and
individual cases own their actual case-specific evidence.

Each case records the source locator, what was actually inspected, who authored
the work, observation versus interpretation, and any uncertainty that limits
use. Its rights disposition distinguishes permission to view from permission to
redistribute. The installed corpus contains authored analysis and links; do not
assume the skill's upstream software licenses license a book, photograph, plan
or website. If a requested later presentation needs such an image, check that
specific asset's rights and attribution before including it. A public URL or an
architect's name is not permission to republish. Preserve the existing
[third-party boundary](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md).
