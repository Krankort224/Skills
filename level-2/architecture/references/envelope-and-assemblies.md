# Envelope and assemblies

Use this reference to choose an envelope family and develop a coherent conceptual
section, including support, openings and replaceable parts. It supplies local
architectural reasoning; it does not specify a tested assembly, engineer its
fixings, predict moisture/thermal performance or establish fire compliance.
Return to [performance and coordination](performance-and-coordination.md) for
room comfort, acoustic operation, structure/services and inclusive-use conflicts.

## Choose a system family

“Mass wall,” “rainscreen” and “frame” describe different aspects and can coexist:
a mass backing wall may carry external insulation and a rainscreen; a rainscreen
may instead cover framed infill. Name **support + backing + insulation/control
strategy + exterior protection**, rather than choosing a finish alone.

| Family / conceptual outside-to-inside arrangement | Why choose it architecturally | Space, junction and failure consequences |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mass backing wall with external insulation and rendered system: compatible render/reinforcement → insulation/fixings → masonry or concrete backing → internal finish/air-control strategy | Continuous external insulation can wrap a substantial backing; punched openings and robust internal surfaces suit cellular rooms | Allow insulation returns at openings and continuous protection at roof/base. Render is not a drained rainscreen: cracks, impact and failed flashings can wet a system that may be difficult to repair locally. Use a complete compatible system and deliberate movement joints |
| Drained rainscreen over mass backing: replaceable cladding → drained/ventilated cavity as designed → water-control backing/insulation arrangement → mass wall → internal finish | Separates the weathering face from a secondary drainage plane; panel scale, texture and replacement can be deliberate parts of expression | Cavity, brackets, rails and thermal bridges consume thickness. Do not obstruct drainage with fixings or assume open joints are watertight. Coordinate cavity fire barriers and water exits without inventing their arrangement |
| Framed infill with exterior insulation/rainscreen: cladding → drainage cavity → weather-control layer/sheathing and continuous insulation in chosen order → insulated studs → air/vapor-control strategy → service cavity/lining as designed | Lightweight infill can follow a frame/module and accept layered interior services; external insulation can reduce, rather than erase, framing bridges | Protect moisture-sensitive sheathing/frame during construction and service. Floor-edge movement, stud/rail bridging, air-seal transitions and cavity fire/acoustic continuity are critical. A service cavity can reduce puncturing of a chosen air-control layer; lining thickness and fixings still matter |
| Masonry cavity wall: outer weathering leaf → drained cavity/insulation arrangement → inner backing leaf → internal control/finish | Robust exterior, depth/reveal character and familiar repetitive openings may suit the project | Support the outer leaf, accommodate its movement relative to the inner leaf, drain above interruptions and at base, and avoid mortar bridges. A cavity full of debris is not a drainage route; a lintel or support shelf may interrupt insulation and water control |
| Factory sandwich/panel wall: external facing → insulation/control strategy → inner support/backing, linked by designed connectors | Repeated panels can reduce site assembly and integrate the desired surface with the wall | Panel joints, corners, transport/erection, connection pockets and tolerances drive the section. The field panel does not prove joint performance; connectors and edges bridge insulation and may need accessible repair |
| Curtain wall with vision and insulated opaque zones: glass/panel → framed drainage/seal system → internal closure, attached to primary structure | Continuous facade grid, broad views and factory panelization can suit an open frame | Reserve mullion/transom depth, anchors, stack/movement joints, spandrel, floor-edge separation and blind pockets. Broad vision competes with solar control, noise and services; a slender external face conceals real depth. Glass-only thermal/acoustic values do not describe the whole facade |

A stick curtain wall assembles frame/glazing largely on site and can accommodate
irregular conditions, but depends on site access and execution of joints. A
unitized wall brings factory-assembled units with interlocking joints, but needs
module discipline, lifting/transport, installation sequence and future replacement
access. Neither name guarantees weather performance. Coordinate the actual
system's internal drainage chambers and air seals; structural silicone, pressure
plates, gaskets and sealants have different roles and compatibility requirements.
A double-skin facade adds a second enclosure, cavity access, ventilation and fire/
acoustic paths; choose it only if those spatial and operational demands can be
resolved, not as an automatic answer to street noise.

Choose with the actual construction sequence, supplier capability, exposure,
interior humidity, site access, desired opening behavior and repair resources.
Do not rank families by generic cost/carbon/rating numbers. Compare initial
complexity with likely failure concentration: many small joints, a concealed
single critical outlet, or an inaccessible fixing can outweigh a robust field.

## Assign control functions before drawing layer order

| Function | Conceptual job | Architectural check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rain-shedding face and flashings | Deflect most bulk water; shed it clear of vulnerable surfaces | Give ledges/recesses an intentional fall/drip and outlet. Do not send upper-wall runoff behind the lower protection |
| Secondary water/drainage plane | Receive incidental water behind the outer face and return it outside | Follow it down to an exit at bases, heads and interrupted cavities. Lap/transition in a water-shedding direction, with end conditions; drains need accessible paths and cannot discharge into the wall below |
| Air-control layer | Limit unintended air movement through the enclosure | Select a sufficiently supported continuous layer and connect it at frames, structure, roof, base and penetrations. Do not confuse a loosely jointed outer screen or fibrous insulation with an air barrier |
| Thermal-control layer | Limit heat flow across conditioned boundary | Wrap openings, slab edges, parapets and support transitions while identifying unavoidable bridges. Extra field insulation does not remove a conductive bracket or exposed concrete connection |
| Vapor-control / drying strategy | Limit damaging moisture accumulation while allowing appropriate drying | Use climate, indoor conditions, material permeability and construction moisture. Vapor diffusion and air leakage are different mechanisms; a vapor-retarding sheet does not cure an unsealed air path |

One layer may perform several functions if the selected system supports it.
Conversely the air-control layer and vapor-control layer need not be the same.
Do not prescribe an impermeable sheet on a universal side: heating/cooling,
humidity and solar-driven inward moisture can change risks. Two low-permeability
layers can trap construction water or leakage; removing one without analysis can
also create a problem. Reserve a buildable, inspectable assembly and request
climate/use-specific moisture assessment when the consequences warrant it.

Distinguish drainage from drying. A cavity can drain bulk water without providing
the drying capacity assumed for a wetted substrate. Ventilation depends on actual
openings and geometry, not the label “ventilated.” Protect stored materials and
close in only at suitable moisture conditions; an otherwise coherent assembly
can trap rain or wet-trade moisture during construction. Consider temporary roof
and opening protection and the sequence in which membranes become inaccessible.

## Adapt the family to climate, use and works

Make an exposure/use-specific section from the selected family. Record outdoor
inputs separately from indoor temperature/humidity, occupancy and operating
mode, including shutdown and construction drying. Heating and cooling seasons
can both matter at one site. Keep unknown design temperatures, rain/wind/snow
actions, ground water and material properties conditional; descriptive weather
averages do not supply engineering design inputs. For confirmed Russia use
[climate/site/assembly selection](normative/ru/applicability.md#select-climate-site-and-assembly-inputs)
to select the affected records and provisions, rather than treat every listed
SP as a universal specification.

| Change in inputs or works | Change the architectural section/reservation | Evidence that can reverse the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Frequent cold-season arrival is an explicit brief priority | Compare a protected lobby with a directly opening reading room; maintain the air/thermal boundary at both doors and reserve floor depth for threshold/drainage. Refit circulation after adding the lobby. | Actual operating schedule, accessible door/queue geometry, envelope and HVAC assessment; the lobby is no quantified energy claim. |
| Sun-exposed occupied facade and warm-season outdoor reading are explicit priorities | Compare movable exterior shade, a fixed shaded porch and revised openings. Reserve brackets, controls, cleaning and glass-removal access independently of the water/air layers. | Orientation, occupied hours, obstructions, glare/solar assessment, wind/support and operation; an overhang is not effective at every sun direction. |
| A cooler conditioned interior and a rain-wetted exterior can occur together | Compare drying paths and the location of impermeable finishes before fixing vapor control. Keep sill/head water exits visible and request a moisture assessment using the actual climate/use. | Material permeability, inward/outward drives, air leakage, indoor humidity and construction moisture; no universal membrane side is selected. |
| Roof shape, plant, terrace or shade changes | Recheck high/low roofs, outlets/overflow, local collection areas, penetrations, maintenance and support zones. Compare aligned supports and a simpler roof before retaining a transfer or hidden gutter. | Structural/geotechnical and drainage input; no snow/wind value, outlet capacity, foundation or fixing size follows from the architectural sketch. |
| Existing facade repair rather than a new envelope | Establish which layers/openings actually change and what survey reveals; preserve viable interfaces and develop the replacement sequence. | Existing construction, intervention classification, product/system evidence and any local/heritage conditions; do not assume new-build scope or force an all-new family onto the work. |

A use change can alter source selection even when the elevation is unchanged.
For example, suppose a **standalone hypothetical public pavilion** is heated only two
days per week. The checked periodic-heating exclusion in the
[SP 50 scope record](normative/ru/environment-and-interfaces.md#sp-50-13330-2024)
excludes it from that document's thermal-protection scope; record **not applicable
for that selection**, with the stated operating fact. This does not exempt the
pavilion from other requirements or establish its alternative thermal basis. If it
becomes heated every day, reopen selection and the assembly assessment. In an
existing-room study explicitly limited to finish replacement, with no ground,
basement, foundation or load changes, do not select foundation design merely
because [SP 22](normative/ru/environment-and-interfaces.md#sp-22-13330-2016) is in
the climate/interface route; reopen that branch if the works expand.

Resolve these reservations with the four junctions below. Architecture owns
the changed geometry and access; specialists confirm support/actions, system
capacity, thermal bridges, moisture behavior and the applicable technical basis.
If a required service zone blocks the intended clerestory or removes the usable
shade-maintenance path, revise the facade/roof arrangement and recount space.

## Develop four connected junctions

At every junction draw a path for water, air and insulation; identify support,
movement, moisture/drying and the part that will need replacement. Test the same
junction in plan as well as section when corners or end dams matter.

### Roof edge and parapet

For a conventional warm-roof concept, reserve deck/support, the chosen air/vapor
strategy, insulation, falls and waterproofing; actual order depends on the roof
family. Connect wall and roof airtight layers at a supported accessible interface,
and continue insulation around the edge/upstand rather than leaving a bare
parapet bridge. Turn waterproofing up a compatible upstand and protect its
termination below coping/counterflashing; a coping joint is not the sole roof
waterproofing strategy. Provide coping falls/drips so runoff is controlled rather
than staining or wetting the wall.

Locate low points, primary outlets and an intentional overflow route that does
not send water into openings or onto an unsafe route. A parapet can conceal a
blocked outlet and retain water; leaving out the overflow because it disturbs
the elevation changes the failure consequence. Make outlet inspection and roof
repair accessible and coordinate guard/plant fixings without casually puncturing
the membrane. Roof movement and wall/roof differential movement need compatible
transitions. Select the actual [roof thermal-boundary arrangement](roof-and-span.md#choose-where-the-thermal-boundary-follows-the-roof)
for an unconditioned attic, occupied slope, compact or protected-membrane roof;
do not transplant the warm-roof sequence unchanged. Coordinate
[water-plane geometry and interruptions](roof-and-span.md#resolve-roof-water-in-plan-and-at-the-junction)
with the same plan, support and opening arrangement.

For a rooflight/monitor, continue insulation at the curb, weatherproof the raised
edge and drain the surrounding roof away from vulnerable joints. Reserve safe
cleaning/replacement, shading and structural support. A curb height, slope or
overhead-glass specification is a project/product and jurisdiction question,
not supplied by this concept.

### Opening head, jamb and sill

Before developing the perimeter, distinguish the rough/frame/light/clear opening
and its [support and operation](wall-floor-and-opening.md#develop-a-wall-opening-as-support-and-use).

Choose the frame position relative to insulation and support deliberately.
Position near the insulation plane can simplify thermal continuity, but the frame
must transfer loads to a designed support, not rest on soft insulation. Return
insulation around the reveal where appropriate; allow room for frame attachment,
seals, tolerance and blinds. Moving the frame changes reveal shade, visible sky,
view, internal sill depth and cleaning reach.

At the head, intercept water from above and direct it to the exterior with ends
that prevent flow into the jamb. At jambs, connect the wall water-control layer
to the opening system in a compatible sequence. At the sill, provide a supported
outward-draining surface/pan with back/end containment as appropriate and an
unobstructed exterior exit. A neat front bead is not a substitute for this path.
Connect the frame continuously to the selected air-control layer independently
of exterior rain shedding; accommodate movement in the transition, rather than
relying on a brittle plaster return.

Coordinate threshold level and approach with access, wind-driven rain, snow,
interior floor build-up and external drainage. A flush internal/external line
requires a developed protected threshold/drainage strategy; do not remove an
upstand on the drawing and assume the weather resistance survives. External
shades, rails and balcony supports need independent support/sealing decisions;
they must not block sill/head drains or prevent glass replacement.

### Floor edge, balcony and facade attachment

Trace slab-edge insulation through the facade support zone. Rainscreen brackets,
shelf angles and curtain-wall anchors transmit loads and allow movement but
can bridge insulation. Reserve enough depth for a developed support/thermal
solution; do not label every bracket “thermal break” without an actual system.
A curtain-wall floor edge also needs coordinated air, acoustic and fire/smoke
separation between floors while allowing the facade to move. Concealing the
floor with a glass spandrel does not provide those functions by itself.

Compare a balcony slab continuing through the wall with independently supported
external structure or a specialist connection. The first interrupts thermal
continuity; the alternatives consume support/connection space and introduce
movement questions. In every option establish deck falls, a drained threshold,
waterproofing termination, edge discharge and access for replacement. A balcony
that drains toward a level doorway is an unresolved concept even if its support
can be engineered.

### Base and ground contact

Distinguish above-ground rain/splash from ground moisture and hydrostatic water.
Continue the chosen wall air and thermal layers to slab/foundation strategy,
while relating waterproofing/damp-control to actual ground and drainage
conditions. Insulation below or near ground needs suitable exposure protection;
cladding cavities need a termination that sheds water outside and resists
blockage/entry as the project requires. Keep moisture-sensitive framing/sheathing
away from persistent splash or wet paving and accommodate finished ground levels
without burying intended outlets.

A subgrade drain needs a viable, maintainable discharge and cannot automatically
replace waterproofing. Check paving falls, entrances and overflow/flood paths
against the internal finished floor and accessible approach. A robust plinth
material must still transition correctly to the upper drainage plane; changing
the finish at the base is not itself moisture protection.

## Compatibility, movement and replaceability

Define which substrate each membrane, tape, sealant, fixing or adhesive actually
touches. Confirm adhesion and chemical compatibility, substrate moisture and
preparation, corrosion/galvanic contacts, coating and gasket compatibility,
expected movement and installation sequence. “Silicone,” “breather membrane” or
“mineral wool” is not a complete product/system specification. Avoid drawing
one sealant as rain seal, air seal, structural fixing and firestop without an
actual tested compatible system.

Accommodate thermal/moisture movement of cladding separately from primary-frame
deflection and differential settlement where relevant. Put joints where movement
can occur without tearing control layers or forcing rigid returns to crack.
Allow tolerance at panel edges and frame anchors; nominal repetition does not
remove the need for installation adjustment. Fixings and joint depths remain
engineering/supplier decisions.

Choose a repair sequence alongside the section. Identify how to inspect a head
flashing, clear a sill outlet, replace a gasket/insulating-glass unit, unclip a
panel, renew roof membrane and replace a concealed fixing. Keep those operations
possible without destroying the primary air/water layer or blocking the sole
access route. Where access depends on lifting equipment or exterior staging,
reserve a real future route/standing location rather than assuming the original
construction crane remains available.

Prefer separating components with different repair cycles where practicable.
A screwed panel may be replaceable only after adjacent panels are removed; draw
the actual sequence. A long-life stone panel can still be stranded by inaccessible
shorter-life fixings or sealant. Provide movement at joints instead of assuming
rigid continuity between unlike materials. Supplier confirmation must address
substrate preparation, sealant/adhesive/membrane compatibility, corrosion,
installation tolerances and repair access.

## Test an envelope choice before retaining it

When new work meets uncertain retained fabric, use the [actual retained-substrate
method](existing-building-adaptation.md#connect-replacement-work-to-the-actual-retained-substrate)
for evidence, intervention boundary and old/new transition. The shared control
functions and generic compatibility/removal procedure remain above.

For example, a hypothetical library considers a rendered insulated mass wall and
a panel rainscreen on the same backing. The rendered option gives a continuous
surface and less panel-joint articulation, but an exposed entrance base is prone
to impact and runoff is concentrated above it. The rainscreen can provide
replaceable base panels, but adds brackets, cavity depth, fire/drainage interfaces
and removal access. Do not conclude that either is intrinsically durable.

Develop the same head/sill/base section for both. If the panel option drains its
cavity behind the entrance sill, revise the sill/cavity transition to discharge
outward without wetting the threshold; if that cannot fit the protected level
entrance, change the opening position/base detail or compare a different family.
If the rendered option relies on an inaccessible seal at the canopy penetration,
move the support or make a properly flashed accessible penetration before
retaining its smooth appearance. Compare the resulting reveal depth against
visible sky and the repair sequence against the public route. These are design
revisions, not proof of moisture, durability or fire performance.

The handoff should identify selected family and alternatives, provisional total
thickness/support space, four junction sketches or descriptions, control-layer
continuity, operating openings/shading and repair access. Request specific checks:
wind/support/movement, thermal bridges and condensation, wetting/drying, air/water
tests, compatibility and assembly fire/acoustic behavior as relevant. Architecture
retains the geometry and reservations and revises them when evidence changes.
