# Scale, hierarchy and rhythm

Start from the brief, [actual demand](../../program/demand.md) and accepted decisions. Choose and compare architectural consequences rather than inferring a style or regulation from a name; select [requirements](../../requirements.md) where applicability matters.

## How can scale, hierarchy and rhythm support the idea?

**Decision:** Make the important spaces and routes legible while relating the
building's parts to its whole, its neighbors and the people using it.

**Inputs:** Parti, entry sequence, principal rooms, neighboring datums, proposed
room and facade organization, and any known structural or service interfaces.

1. Identify what should be prominent and why: the entrance, a common room, an
   outdoor space or a route. Compare prominence through position, size, contrast,
   depth or light; do not assume that the largest element is always most public.
2. Examine relationships at site, building, room, opening and person scale.
   Compare a long undivided frontage with one broken into legible parts; assess
   whether entrances and ground-level details can be understood on approach.
   Proportional ratios are optional exploratory devices, not requirements.
3. Establish a repeatable order from actual room needs and plausible support:
   regular bays, alternating openings or a gradual change. Place deliberate
   exceptions where an entrance, corner or special room needs one. Symmetry can
   clarify an axis; asymmetry can balance different uses or site edges.
4. Check the order against both sides of the facade and against the plan. A
   repeated opening that crosses a partition, exposes a private room or conflicts
   with support is a conflict to resolve, not a reason to conceal the problem.
   Adjust room grouping, the rhythm or the exception and compare consequences.
5. Review the sequence from arrival to destination. If every element is equally
   emphatic, reduce competing accents; if the main entry disappears, strengthen
   a meaningful cue. Check that the cue remains usable and accessible.

**Check:** Describe the hierarchy and its exceptions in terms of real uses. A
rhythm that looks consistent in elevation still requires room-fit, structural
and service coordination; do not treat it as proof of constructability.

## Provenance and use limits

The operative reasoning is local; external reading is not a runtime prerequisite. [Pinned readings, adaptations and exclusions](../../sources.md#composition-and-style) retain the source identity, inspected extent and limitations. These sources are not project requirements. Foreign codes, certification credits, thresholds, numeric benchmarks, modeling workflows and prescribed scoring weights are not adopted.
