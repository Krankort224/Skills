# Section as a form generator

Start from the brief, [actual demand](../../program/demand.md) and accepted decisions. Choose and compare architectural consequences rather than inferring a style or regulation from a name; select [requirements](../../requirements.md) where applicability matters.

## How can section generate form?

A section is an organizing proposition about ground, occupied height, level
changes, light openings, support and roof. Establish a representative transverse
section through room/route/edge and a longitudinal section through arrival and
the principal destination. These can be described textually when no drawing is
requested. Work with program clearances and provisional architectural zones;
do not derive engineering sizes from visual proportions.

| Section strategy | How it works; when useful | Conflicts and tests |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Common level, varied roof | Put frequent adjacencies on one accessible floor; raise the main hall or selected roof bays above lower support rooms. Useful for shared public use and different room heights. | A tall roof everywhere wastes volume and can obscure hierarchy; a low service band can block the high room's side openings. Test the actual occupied height, roof/support zone and light path across both parts. |
| Room beside a tall void | Stack small rooms beside a double-height destination or atrium; galleries allow visual orientation across levels. Useful when shared identity and oversight are desired. | Visible connection is not acoustic separation. Check privacy, floor-edge safety, routes and the floor area displaced by the void. Return to stacking or adjacency if a quiet use cannot tolerate the shared volume. |
| Stepped floors | Follow ground or give neighboring rooms different volumes and outlooks. Useful where a few program groups genuinely benefit from different levels. | Test a continuous inclusive journey and goods movement. Frequent level changes can make closely related rooms operationally remote. Compare fewer consolidated levels before accepting complex links. |
| Terraced upper parts | Recede floors to make external rooms at intermediate levels and reduce the apparent upper bulk from selected viewpoints. | A terrace needs a destination, access, privacy edge and enclosure/drainage coordination. Do not count a lower roof as usable terrace until those conditions are reserved. |
| Roof monitor / clerestory / repeated roof bays | Open a raised roof edge or place openings between repeated high and low roof parts; brings a potential light source to rooms away from external walls. Useful for a large hall with occupied perimeter rooms. | Roof opening location determines what receives light and what may receive glare. Structure, maintenance, water paths and service zones can obstruct it. Trace the opening-to-task line in section, then refer performance claims for assessment. |

Section and plan must generate each other. A tall room changes roof silhouette,
external scale and the elevation's upper openings. A sloping roof can orient the
interior toward a view or a high light opening, but its low edge must still fit
the activity and service zones. A compressed threshold followed by a taller hall
can clarify arrival through contrast without a monumental exterior. The change
must occur along a usable route, not rely on steps or a hidden alternative entry.

At each level trace plausible aligned supports and vertical service reservations
against the room and opening arrangement. If a desired void or offset interrupts
those lines, reserve the interface and identify who must confirm it. Compare an
aligned option; do not claim that expressive form supplies its own structural
solution. Coordinate through [structure and services reservations](../../space/vertical/section-and-services.md#budget-the-section-and-routes).

## Provenance and use limits

The operative reasoning is local; external reading is not a runtime prerequisite. [Pinned readings, adaptations and exclusions](../../../sources/upstream-packs.md#composition-and-style) retain the source identity, inspected extent and limitations. These sources are not project requirements. Foreign codes, certification credits, thresholds, numeric benchmarks, modeling workflows and prescribed scoring weights are not adopted.
