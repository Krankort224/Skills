## Baroque

### Classification and useful distinctions

Baroque developed in Italy and spread widely during the seventeenth and first
half of the eighteenth century. The V&A emphasizes coordinated architecture,
painting and sculpture, drama, and the persuasive purposes of religious and
political patrons. Its international spread also involved colonial networks;
local materials and contexts changed its expression. A contemporary brief
therefore needs to decide whose identity and experience its composition serves,
rather than treating historical authority as an automatic design benefit.
See [V&A, The Baroque style](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/the-baroque-style).

Keep at least these readings distinct:

| Reading and verified reference | Consequential distinction |
| --- | --- |
| Roman, continuous sculptural enclosure: Francesco Borromini's [San Carlo](../../../assets/styles/baroque/san-carlo-alle-quattro-fontane/analysis.md) | A restricted complex can contain a strongly differentiated interior. Its perimeter, upper transition and street front require separate readings; an ellipse is insufficient as a whole-building diagram. |
| Roman, integrated polychrome interior: Gian Lorenzo Bernini's Sant'Andrea al Quirinale | The major axis of its oval crosses the entrance axis; recessed chapels preserve the interior rhythm. This is a counterexample to assuming that a Baroque oval always elongates the route ahead. Marble, stucco and gilding also counter a universal white-interior recipe. [Rome tourism authority](https://turismoroma.it/en/places/church-santandrea-al-quirinale). |
| French palace context and mixed historical layers: Versailles | The operator describes a combination of Baroque, Classical, Neoclassical and Gothic architecture. Treating the entire present palace as a pure style specimen would erase different campaigns. Its political history also differs from a small religious community's commission. [Château de Versailles](https://en.chateauversailles.fr/node/35077). |

These are bounded comparisons, not a survey of every region or period. Curvature,
symmetry, a dome, gold or monumental scale alone cannot establish a Baroque
classification. San Carlo supplies the main inspected plan in this profile;
Sant'Andrea and Versailles are textual checks on generalization, not additional
fully analyzed cases. Historical ratios and a universal geometrical construction
are not established here.

### Compose space before specifying ornament

The following are design interpretations informed by San Carlo. Select them
only when they solve a brief's actual relationships.

| Decision | Mechanism and alternative | Check and reason to change direction |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival and sequence | A restrained approach followed by a more open, taller shared destination can distinguish arrival from occupation. Alternatively, reveal the destination from the street if uncertainty would inconvenience first-time users. | Trace entry, waiting, accessible arrival and the onward route. Reject a dramatic compression that leaves people waiting in the door or makes the everyday entrance subordinate. |
| Spatial center and secondary uses | Keep one occupied field legible while shaping its edge into recesses, thresholds or distinct secondary territories. Compare a regular hall with adjacent rooms when the secondary uses require closure. | Furnish the main field and each edge. A curving recess that cannot hold its intended activity is lost capacity; a visually continuous boundary does not provide acoustic separation. |
| Proportion and rhythm | Repeated vertical elements can gather varied openings into a larger order; a continuous horizontal datum can connect changes in direction. Compare one consistent bay with deliberate emphasis at the main destination. | Read the composition from the approach and seated positions. Check whether the dominant order obscures doors, conflicts with floor levels or overwhelms a small room. Derive proportions from the new section and occupied clearances. |
| Facade depth | A projecting central threshold and recessed side fields can distinguish entrance and edges through actual depth. A planar front with a deep entrance recess may achieve enough hierarchy on a narrow pavement. | Draw the occupied threshold and a weathering section. Projection cannot consume a required route, create an unmaintainable ledge or conceal the entrance from an oblique approach. |
| Light and upper enclosure | A higher or brighter destination can reinforce orientation; a calmer perimeter can allow the room to read as one field. Compare side light, a raised roof strip and ordinary ceiling lighting against the same occupied views. | Inspect glare, view, shading and maintenance access. A bright photograph does not establish useful light at work surfaces or comfort over a day. |
| Structure and material expression | Distinguish the load-bearing enclosure, its visible ordering elements and applied relief. Repeated surface work can give a modest material a strong spatial role. Compare a simpler support/enclosure with localized craft. | Identify what is load-bearing, what is lining and how each is replaced. A sculptural appearance cannot establish load paths or justify a false structural explanation. |

### Adapt the direction to another commission

**Program:** A worship space can focus many occupants on one destination. A
library or clinic may require several equal destinations and independent quiet
rooms. Transfer the legibility of the main field and its thresholds; reconsider
the single focus where it would create a queue or force circulation through
another activity. Use the [San Carlo changed brief](../../../assets/styles/baroque/san-carlo-alle-quattro-fontane/analysis.md#a-small-changed-brief)
for an original example.

**Site and scale:** On an oblique, close street approach, depth and a recognizable
threshold may matter more than a symmetrical distant view. On an open campus,
test more than one approach and provide a clear side arrival. Enlarging a small
curved interior multiplies the roof, environmental and circulation problem;
repeat a bounded spatial unit or introduce independently served rooms before
assuming the original enclosure can simply grow.

**Climate and budget:** A contemporary curved front still needs a continuous
weather enclosure, drainage and cleaning access. In a cold or wet setting,
compare a sheltered entrance and simple primary enclosure with sculptural work
inside; in a hot setting, test solar exposure before increasing roof glazing.
For limited funds, prioritize one well-resolved sequence, a consistent datum
and a small number of craft locations. Compare replacement and cleaning access
as well as initial construction complexity. These are conditional choices, not
claims that historic masonry, roof forms or finishes meet current performance.

**Counterexample:** Adding scrolls, giant columns and a curved screen to a
double-loaded corridor does not resolve its long routes, indistinguishable
doors or undersized waiting space. If the brief still fails after those motifs
are removed, the spatial problem remains. A straightforward room-and-route
solution can be preferable even when a Baroque visual reference was requested;
explain which desired experience the simpler scheme retains.

## Art Nouveau

### Classification and variants

The V&A dates Art Nouveau from the early 1890s to the First World War. Its
international forms include fluid and geometric lines, asymmetric compositions
and relationships between structure and decoration. Belgian work by Victor
Horta and Henry van de Velde coordinates building and fittings; Austrian
Secession work includes a more geometric vocabulary. These distinctions prevent
the sinuous line from becoming a universal rule.
[V&A collection overview](https://www.vam.ac.uk/collections/art-nouveau),
[international variants](https://www.vam.ac.uk/articles/art-nouveau-an-international-style).

For Antoni Gaudí's [Casa Batlló](../../../assets/styles/art-nouveau/casa-batllo/analysis.md),
use **Catalan Modernisme** as the specific context. The Gaudí Council identifies
it with that movement; Spain's official tourism service also places it within
Art Nouveau. The broader umbrella is useful for comparison, but does not make
the house an example of the French branch or its forms a template for all
Modernisme. **Modernisme** here is also distinct from the later, much broader
term **Modernism**.
[Gaudí Council](https://consellantonigaudi.cat/en/buildings/casa-batllo/),
[Spain tourism authority](https://www.spain.info/en/places-of-interest/casa-batllo/).

This profile develops an integrated craft-and-space direction through one
Gaudí case. It does not establish a complete Belgian, French, Austrian or
Catalan historical taxonomy. A contemporary organic form, floral grille or
colored tile alone is insufficient for historical attribution.

### Develop a continuous family without making every element unique

| Decision | Mechanism and alternative | Check and counterexample |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Spatial sequence | Give changes in territory tangible thresholds: enclosure, stair, handrail, door and light can reinforce one another. Compare a direct level route with a prominent stair-led sequence where independent access matters. | Follow a first visit and ordinary daily use. A sculptural stair that displaces the inclusive route cannot be treated as the whole arrival strategy. |
| Plan and section | Begin with retained structure, occupied frontage and internal depth. Give a shared stair, court or lightwell an explicit spatial purpose. Compare internal light access with shallower rooms or an additional external edge. | Check the rooms left after the void, their access and privacy in section. A tiled shaft cannot compensate automatically for excessive room depth. |
| Proportion and rhythm | Keep recognizable families of openings, surfaces and fittings while varying them where room use or exposure changes. Compare a repeated module with a limited special family at the communal room. | Check furniture, opening operation and actual fabrication families. A different curve at every opening can destroy coordination without improving use. |
| Facade depth | Let a viewing bay, balcony or sheltered threshold occupy a real relationship with the room. Compare a shallow reveal and shading element where a projecting bay is unnecessary. | Test outlook from occupied positions, overlooking, solar exposure and cleaning. A shallow applied wave has different spatial consequences from an inhabitable bay. |
| Structure and material | Decide which element supports, spans, screens or merely finishes. Relate the visible pattern to those roles; use compatible materials and a repairable joint strategy. | Compare the exposed element with a lining over a separate support. A plant-like metal member is not proven efficient, and an arch-like lining is not evidence of arch action. |
| Craft and touch | Concentrate the family at door edges, rails, seats and selected light filters where people encounter it. A clear repeated fitting can carry the idea across otherwise simple rooms. | Make a full-size sample when grip, cleaning or replacement is uncertain. Repeating a complicated motif at inaccessible locations may consume funds without strengthening the spatial sequence. |

These are authored design choices, not measurements or performance findings
about Casa Batlló. Its remodelling is particularly useful because a new visual
language and inherited fabric need to be considered together; do not attach
the facade language to an arbitrary untested floor plan.

### Adaptation by use, site and means

**Program:** For a school, clinic or workplace, begin with robust repeated rooms
and legible shared space. Use craft to distinguish the shared threshold or a
place of rest; keep closures, changing furniture and servicing workable. An
integrated appearance does not require every door, partition and fitting to
be immovable. The [Casa Batlló changed brief](../../../assets/styles/art-nouveau/casa-batllo/analysis.md#a-small-changed-brief)
tests a modest existing-building adaptation.

**Site and climate:** A deep attached urban building raises different light,
privacy and air-path questions from a freestanding shallow pavilion. On a
corner, compare two occupied frontages before introducing an internal void.
In a cold climate, an internal court or rooflight requires a new enclosure and
maintenance proposition; in a hot climate, compare shading and night/security
constraints before relying on operable openings. The case's tile color gradient
is an optical design intention, not a portable daylight calculation.

**Scale and budget:** A small room can sustain a close relationship between a
handrail, threshold and wall. Across a large building, use recognizable families
and a few distinct public destinations to avoid an unmanageable number of
special parts. For a limited budget, compare standard openings with one crafted
shared-room assembly against unique windows everywhere. Include access to
replace glass, finishes and hardware; avoid claiming savings without quantities
and actual supplier evidence.

**Counterexample:** A wavy glass facade over deep, identical rooms may offer no
useful light distribution, privacy transition or meaningful hierarchy. A plain
external wall with an appropriately placed shared light space and a coherent
family of doors can better express the intended integration. Conversely,
adding a lightwell to an already shallow building may sacrifice useful rooms
without solving a problem. Retain the decision's cause, not its visible device.

## Choose between the directions

Use the shared [comparison through one brief](precedent-transfer.md#compare-directions-through-the-same-brief)
when options are requested. For a small public room, a Baroque-informed direction
might emphasize arrival, a common destination and a continuous larger order;
an Art-Nouveau-informed direction might emphasize how the threshold, openings
and fittings form one usable family. Neither choice predetermines a central
plan, a curved perimeter or a particular material.

Compare the actual entry sequence, occupied room shapes, plan/section interfaces,
facade depth and repair obligations. A choice between cream plaster and colored
tiles alone does not distinguish architectural organizations. If the same
spatial scheme supports both directions, state that explicitly and compare the
specific material and threshold decisions that remain different.
