# Composition and style

Translate a requested style into observable design decisions. Use a named style as
a design vocabulary, not a claim that every building in a category is identical.
Work from the brief and site in [program and space](program-and-space.md); check
performance consequences in [performance and coordination](performance-and-coordination.md).

## Read the context

Record the street edge, orientation, topography, views, neighboring scale and
setbacks where supplied or verified. Consider arrival, public and private edges,
landscape, climate exposure and existing fabric. Mark missing site information.
Choose what to continue, contrast or reinterpret and explain why.

## Build a composition

- State a concise organizing idea: for example, rooms around a sheltered court,
  a public spine with quieter side rooms, or a sequence from compressed entrance
  to an open common space. Relate it to the stated activities and context.
- Compare overall silhouette and massing, parts and whole, solid and void,
  symmetry or deliberate asymmetry, repetition, rhythm and hierarchy.
- Test proportions at building, room, opening and human scales. Use a chosen
  ratio as an exploratory device, not as an inherited rule or performance proof.
- Coordinate the grid and facade rhythm with room uses and the plausible
  structural and service strategy. Note where a visual decision creates a
  harder structural span, deep room, awkward furnishing or expensive junction.
- Place entrances, thresholds, focal points and exterior space so a visitor can
  understand the sequence. Indicate orientation and main views in a 2D diagram.

## Describe style through choices

| Design lever | Questions to answer |
| --- | --- |
| Proportion | Tall or low, compact or extended, regular or varied? |
| Composition | Axial or informal, ordered or layered, open or enclosed? |
| Facade | Depth, opening rhythm, shading, solid-to-glazed balance? |
| Materials | Texture, color, pattern, weathering, local availability? |
| Detail | Flush or expressed joints, restrained or articulated edges? |
| Setting | Continuity or contrast with streetscape and landscape? |

Make descriptions specific enough to draw and compare: “deep reveals and a
regular vertical window rhythm” conveys a decision more clearly than “modern”.
A request for a style can lead to multiple coherent options; avoid assuming a
particular period, person, material, or region is mandatory.

## Evaluate the proposal

Assess daylight, glare, privacy, weather protection, access and maintenance
alongside visual coherence. A highly glazed facade, for example, may support
views yet increase glare and solar exposure; its result depends on orientation,
shading, glazing and climate. Avoid prescribing a fixed glass percentage as a
universal answer. Explain conceptual judgments separately from sourced facts
and from calculations, and identify the studies or specialist review needed.

Use plans, simple elevations, annotated diagrams or 2D SVG/raster visuals to
communicate intent. These are conceptual drawings unless measured and verified.
If a building model is later requested, hand over the brief, decisions, dimensions,
assumptions and unresolved issues for a separate explicitly requested workflow.
