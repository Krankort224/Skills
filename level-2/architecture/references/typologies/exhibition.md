## Derive the exhibit and audience commitments

For each object/group establish narrative role, dimensions in display and packed
states, mass/support, fragility, materials/finishes, permitted touch and mounting,
viewed faces/detail, acceptable distance, light/environmental sensitivity and
display duration. Record who supplies or approves each constraint: curator,
lender, conservator, operator, handler or project. Unidentified paint, adhesive
or textile can control a mixed object's sensitivity; an apparently robust metal
body does not settle it. Missing object evidence leaves that decision open.

Identify visitors' seated/standing positions, assistance, close examination,
audio/tactile/large-print interpretation, queues and rest. Separate fixed object,
barrier, mount and display case from each viewer's approach/use field. A nominal
room area cannot establish that a seated viewer sees over a case rim or that
waiting leaves a route open. Use audience consultation/prototypes for actual
usability rather than treating one eye height or angle as a population standard.

Develop the public story and collection-service story together: receive, inspect,
unpack, stage, install, display, clean, remove, repack and dispatch. Establish
where each transition happens and what stays in the room when it closes.
Separate a routine visitor state from a supervised replacement state; an empty
arrow through a case or occupied viewer field is not a handling route.

## Construct viewing and sequence

Choose a route from the interpretation: comparison may need simultaneous views;
chronology may need an ordered sequence; a large object may need an initial
distant overview followed by side/detail access. Offer a bypass/return where
the brief allows it. Locate label reading and waiting beside the moving route,
and make each exit/transition legible. A compulsory dark room must not sever
access to exhibits that remain open. Trace closure at all actual portals.

For a plane object width w and a viewer centred at normal distance d, the
horizontal angular span is2×atan(w/(2d)); off-centre viewers need the two actual
edge directions. For top height z_t and eye z_e, upward angle is
atan((z_t−z_e)/d), with the actual distance to that face. Set the requested
angle/distance from the specific interpretive task, not a universal optimum.
Trace lines to top/bottom/side and relevant detail from actual eye positions;
test mounts, barriers, glazing, other objects and people as occluders.
Whole-object overview and close detail can need different stopping places.

Evaluate glare/reflection from the seated and standing positions and the actual
luminaire/window directions. A clear geometric view does not establish readable
detail, contrast, acoustic description or glare-free glazing. Use mockups,
photometry and material evidence as those claims require. A permitted replica,
tactile model or description should convey the same interpretive relationship,
with its identity disclosed, rather than quietly substitute for the original.

## Choose an object-specific light and enclosure response

[CCI's selected reading](../../sources/volume-and-exhibition.md#collection-light)
supports distinguishing visible-light exposure from UV and heating, and choosing
controls with the collection's sensitivity. UV filtration alone does not stop
visible-light fading. A light limit is not a promise of zero damage. Establish
object-specific illuminance, exposure and other environmental conditions with
the responsible collection specialist, rather than copying a material table.

Use E at the object plane, not at a remote floor sensor. For supplied piecewise
constant states, exposure D=sum(E_i×hours_i) in lux-hours. Include daylight,
electric/label spill, installation, closed hours and maintenance on the same
accounting horizon. Variable E requires its actual time integral or an evidenced
upper bound; an annual average can hide a short intense patch. Dose accounting
is not a fading prediction, spectral assessment or conservator approval.

Compare spatial responses before choosing a glazed gallery:

| Collection/interpretive condition | Response to develop | Consequence to retain |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Sensitive originals with variable external light | Enclosed controllable display room, independently lit interpretation and managed exposure schedule | Opaque walls/roof or evidenced blackout; transition/adaptation, safe routes, inspection/controls and replacement access remain necessary |
| Object permits daylight but needs clear form/detail reading | Test a controlled high/side opening, indirect wash or electric alternative at actual viewing faces | Seasonal sun, glare, case reflection, heat and maintenance can defeat the intention; orientation alone supplies no result |
| Bright overview and low-exposure detail coexist | Separate spaces or independently controlled cases and viewing states | Keep their visitor/support connections and gradients usable; test spill at the sensitive surface |
| Outdoor object proposed | Confirm object-specific weather/light/security and mounting acceptance; compare shelter, seasonal removal or a declared replica | Grounds, drainage, support, wind, touch and replacement route; moving outdoors does not establish conservation suitability |

The Smithsonian reading supports seated/standing access, alternative
interpretation, gradual light transitions and route visibility alongside
conservation. Its institutional standards and numbers are not local code.
Do not darken an entire visitor path merely to reduce an object's dose.

## Test light exposure and a changed exhibit

The original drawing's hypothetical lender supplies an instantaneous40lux cap
and12,000lux-hour remaining allowance for this installation/display episode.
These are test inputs, not material defaults. After this episode the original
must be removed or a new authorized budget established; do not reset a cumulative
history annually. The provisional object-plane bounds are40lux viewing and1lux
closed, including all spills. Fifty24-hour display days give1200h. Separately
account8h installation at100lux and2h handling at80lux:960lux-hours. If the
40lux cap applied to handling too, those states would fail and need correction;
here the lender expressly grants those two separate handling states.

Opening the original for all8h/day gives40×400+1×800+960=17,760: fails dose.
With4.5h/day original viewing,225h view+975h closed, dose is
40×225+1×975+960=**10,935**, leaving1065. This correction needs a real timed
light/cover and limited-viewing operation; an opaque-room proposal alone proves
neither40 nor1lux. Measure/map the object surface and verify all states and
faults. Maintain safe independent circulation and label/alternative access;
if their spill defeats the bounds, refit the lighting/case/position or schedule.

If a changed original instead requires45lux for the agreed detail task and its
lender explicitly permits that new instantaneous level while retaining the
12,000 dose budget,4.5h/day gives12,060: fails. A permitted4h/day gives
45×200+1×1000+960=10,960. If the original40lux cap remains fixed,45lux fails
instantaneously regardless of shorter duration: seek another viewing/detail
response or a permitted reproduction, not a dose-only excuse.
