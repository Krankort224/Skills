## Combine families at an explicit interface

Resolve each use on its own operating basis, then decide what it exchanges with
its neighbor. Sharing a room, arrival space, staff position, toilet, store, lift
or court are different decisions. For each proposed shared resource, record:
the users and hours; who holds keys/stock and resets it; where displaced objects
go; the boundary when one use closes; and the consequence of a missed reset or
delivery. Preserve a viable ordinary day for each operator, including residents.

Compare an independent street address and support chain with a common arrival
and controlled branches. The independent option can simplify hours and identity
but duplicates occupied/support space and takes frontage. The common option can
support shared help and amenities, but creates queues, control and staffing
dependencies. If it requires a staffed desk whenever either use operates, put
that commitment in the comparison rather than declaring the shared lobby cheaper.

Develop a section through the consequential interface: noisy evening group room
under homes, book stock above public space, short-stay rooms beside an event
volume, or a teaching store replenished from another level. Mark the actual
occupied floors, route/handling connection, service depth and boundary that must
continue. Use [levels and cores](../space/planning/room-floor-and-section.md#organize-levels-cores-and-service-depth)
for the general construction and [performance](../performance/comfort/acoustics-and-air.md)
for physical paths. Neither a different floor nor a commercial label proves
compatibility. The combination may need a changed room assignment, a genuine
buffer/separation, reduced simultaneous use or independent support.

## Worked mixed-use correction

This original hypothetical study concerns only two public rooms and their
interface with homes above. It is not a complete building or an area standard.
The accepted brief requires a small library open until 18:00, a classroom for
evening groups, and uninterrupted residential entry. Books are returned during
the evening; one worker processes them the following morning. A side lane can
receive deliveries. Keep the existing residential core, public frontage and
classroom capacity fixed. Available rooms and their dimensions remain project
inputs to be fitted with the linked room method.

**Faulty proposal.** The shared street foyer opens into the reading room, which
is the sole path to the classroom and public toilet. The book-return trolley
stands beside that path. The residential core also opens from the reading room.
At 18:00 the library is shown closed by locking the foyer-to-reading door. This
blocks the class, toilet and home journeys; leaving it unlocked exposes the
collection and makes quiet seats the evening passage. Calling the reading room
an adaptable foyer deletes its promised reading activity without permission.

**A — shared foyer, independent branches.** Take an edge strip from the reading
room reservation to form a public passage from foyer to classroom and toilet.
Give the reading room a continuous closable edge to that passage, and connect
the existing residential core directly to the foyer through its own controlled
threshold. Move return holding and morning sorting behind a staff-side interface
connected to the side-lane receiving route; an evening return opening, if used,
feeds that holding without opening a public path into it. Check item sizes,
cart approach and the operator's collection procedure before choosing equipment.
This costs reading-room depth/frontage and requires refitting shelves and seats.
If the required reading capacity no longer fits, A fails despite its better routes.

**B — independent evening entrance.** Keep the reading-room boundary and give
the classroom and toilet an independent external approach. Retain a separately
controlled residential branch from the street foyer and the staff-side return
holding. B preserves more reading space but consumes external edge, can lengthen
the evening approach and duplicates arrival/control functions. It remains a
candidate only if the fixed frontage, inclusive journey and actual door locations
support it; an entrance arrow is not enough.

**Decision and verification.** Prefer A only if the room refit retains required
reading and teaching capacity and the shared arrival works without an unprovided
evening attendant. At 17:55 trace readers leaving, class users arriving, a
resident entering and a returned item reaching holding. At 18:05 repeat with
the collection closed, then follow next morning's trolley from holding through
sorting to shelves without crossing an occupied class. Inspect the section for
evening activity below bedrooms and the carrying route through the existing
core; retain acoustic and other performance questions explicitly. If either
candidate needs changed capacity or frontage beyond the boundary, report the
specific conflict and decision required. Route repair alone is not acceptance.
