# Dwellings, access and the court

Use this reference when a housing layout must reconcile household mix, apartment
frontage, shared access and outdoor life. General
[plan families](../../space/planning/room-floor-and-section.md#choose-a-plan-family-from-the-relationships)
and [urban edges](../../context/site/site-and-context.md#choose-how-the-building-meets-its-edges)
retain their existing owners. Here, test the dwelling, common route, section and
ground interface together. A building silhouette or number of stairs does not
establish how the homes are actually reached.

## Choose a dwelling and access family together

Start with whole households and their required mix, essential single-level
activities, room relationships and frontage needs. Mark the real street, court,
gallery and passage conditions. Two building facades do not give every dwelling
two aspects; a court facade beside a delivery road is not automatically quiet.

Develop a representative street dwelling, court dwelling and corner/termination
before multiplying the typical plan. Mark each dwelling boundary, occupied
frontage, wet/support band, entrance and route from its door to the shared core.
Distinguish windows belonging to living/sleeping rooms from those serving a
bathroom, access lobby or common corridor.

| Dwelling/access choice | What it can achieve | Coupled cost and failure test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Double-loaded internal corridor | Concentrates access and can serve many compact units on two sides of a deep wing. Corners or special end units can differ. | Repeated ordinary units may be single-aspect. A dwelling behind another dwelling cannot claim the far facade. The long common route and wet/core organization must work; repeated stairs alone do not turn it into a short-landing scheme. |
| Short landing/core serving through or corner dwellings | Can give local addresses and dwellings with different aspects on one level, with less shared route directly beside their windows. | Finite frontage and landing area limit dwelling count/mix. More cores, larger dwellings or fewer doors per core change the area and ground entrances. Show how a central wet/support zone and internal route actually connect both fronts. |
| External or enclosed gallery with single-level dwellings | Makes a shared access edge and can serve a through-dwelling arrangement without internal stairs. | Rooms beside the gallery face passers-by, noise and possible weather exposure. Setback, screens, a partial buffer or different room placement can help but consume depth or reduce light/view. A fully opaque support strip at that edge can leave occupied rooms effectively single-aspect. An enclosed gallery needs a developed environmental/separation strategy. |
| Gallery/corridor with maisonettes or interlocking levels | Access on selected levels can release facade at another level and create different room/volume relationships. | Carry it through at least two plans and a section. Internal stairs, landings, service alignment and level-dependent access consume area and can defeat essential single-level use. Fewer common access levels do not remove vertical travel or automatically prove better efficiency. |

Compare at the same boundary and household demand. Count dwellings by required
type, actual occupied aspects, common/core area, private planning zones and
internal steps. Include the corner, end, ground and top cases; a repeated typical
floor does not establish the ground entry, roof dwelling or complete accessible
journey. Do not copy a foreign core/lift threshold, density, orientation rule or
student project's count. Select applicable requirements separately.

## Give the dwelling an internal life

For a furnished family or narrow compact plan, develop the
[actual use, doors and deductions](dwelling-fit.md#test-a-dwelling-through-its-actual-use)
as well as the family/access choice below.

Place furniture and real paths before celebrating a corridor-free diagram.
Separate arrival, shared daily activity and sleeping/private rooms deliberately.
An internal corridor can give independent bedroom access; a grouped day/night
arrangement can reduce circulation but needs an intelligible threshold. A central
support zone can leave both facades for occupied rooms, provided its services
and route do not sever the dwelling. An enlarged central living space must have
an actual use, light relationship and furniture fit; residual depth renamed
“family room” is not a solution.

Test an ordinary evening, a visitor arrival and simultaneous sleeping/work use.
Must someone cross a bedroom or a noisy living setting to reach a bathroom?
Does an open entrance expose a private room to the gallery? Can a household use
the required essential activities without an internal stair? Preserve at least
one credible furniture/door arrangement for each repeated type. Gross planning
zone area includes its assigned wall/shaft reservations; it is not net room area.

Overlay two floors to locate actual wet rooms, riser reservations and maintenance
access. Repeated bathrooms make alignment plausible, not proven. Moving a flat
boundary, gallery or landing can move its kitchen/shaft, door, facade and support
line together. Compare the changed wall/floor organization through
[walls and floors](../../construction/structure/walls-floors-openings.md#make-wall-roles-and-floor-direction-agree),
and acoustic relationships through
[performance](../../performance/comfort/acoustics-and-air.md#acoustic-paths-and-ventilation-choices).
Do not require every project to have the same room sequence or services layout.

## Connect home, court and servicing

Follow one child's/visitor's route from an actual dwelling door through the
landing/core, ground entrance and threshold to the chosen court activity. Mark
every vehicle crossing and controlled door. A pedestrian line or service-radius
circle on a masterplan does not demonstrate that journey. A view of play space
does not establish access to it.

Give the court an ownership and operating arrangement: public passage,
resident-shared space, private edge or a deliberate combination. Locate the
occupied edges and entrances that support it, visitors' stopping points,
deliveries, waste, maintenance and any required emergency access. Compare an
outer service edge, a controlled crossing at the court opening and a shared
internal route. Removing ordinary inner traffic can improve the intended
door-to-court relationship, but is not feasible until displaced operations have
somewhere to go. A gate or planting label does not resolve that conflict.

If parking lies below, draw a section through the occupied court and its real
garage boundary. Include access/ramp, plant/exhaust, structure, soil/planting
reservations, waterproofing/drainage and maintainable transitions as applicable.
The surface cannot be counted simultaneously as unrestricted deep ground and
an uncoordinated garage roof. A lower/open wing can admit sky and access while
also admitting street exposure and public view. Test the actual orientation,
heights and receivers; enclosure is not a sunlight, wind or noise result.

## Worked revision of a housing wing and court edge

This original hypothetical comparison fixes a 60×14 m **internal planning
envelope**, x0…60/y0…14. Street is at y0, desired resident court at y14. An upper
floor must contain three family homes with two occupied aspects and essential
rooms on one level, plus six compact homes. For this study only, minimum gross
planning reservations are96 m²/family and48 m²/compact; they include internal
walls/support/shaft allowances and are not legal area or room standards. The
lower floor is communal/support use, so its entrances need not preserve upper
apartment boundaries. Core envelopes below are trial reservations, not verified
stair/lift assemblies or proof of adequate escape/access provision.

**Baseline.** An internal double-loaded corridor with compact cells on each side
has enough doors and facade in aggregate, but ordinary cells have only one
occupied aspect. Renaming three cells “family through-flats” without changing
their boundaries fails the brief. A vehicle ring between entrances and the
play space also contradicts the proposed direct resident court journey.

**A — three independent short-landing modules.** Repeat the following 20×14 m
module three times, with local coordinates u0…20:

| Ownership in one module | Coordinates | Gross planning area |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Family dwelling | u0…10, y0…14 | 140 m² |
| Core/landing reservation | u10…14, y3…11 | 32 m² |
| Street compact | u14…20, y0…6, plus u10…14, y0…3 | 48 m² |
| Court compact | u14…20, y8…14, plus u10…14, y11…14 | 48 m² |
| Common branch | u14…20, y6…8 | 12 m² |

The family enters at(u10,7); the compact doors meet the branch at(u17,6) and
(u17,8), whose connection to the core is at(u14,7). These are non-overlapping
zones; actual doors, wall/shaft deductions and fitted rooms remain within them.
The family has its own street and court frontage, rather than reaching across
another dwelling. One possible family organization reserves a central y5…9
support/arrival band with a route to both fronts, then fits living and sleeping
settings at the facades. Which receives the noisier street is an explicit
household/acoustic choice; a through-plan does not make street bedrooms quiet.

Over three modules: private zones **3×140+6×48=708 m²**, cores96 and common
branches36, total **840 m²**. Family homes exceed the96 study minimum; that
generosity is part of A's result, not hidden equivalence with a smaller dwelling.
At ground level reserve each core's street approach through local u10…14/y0…3
and court approach through u10…14/y11…14. Both replace the upper compact's
projected zone within the communal lower plan. Do not claim those approaches
fit if the same ground area has already been assigned to a private tenancy.

**B — single-level gallery alternatives on the same envelope.** Reserve the
street gallery y0…2 across60 m, end core zones x0…6 and54…60/y2…10, and common
end pockets above them at y10…14. Fit three family zones x6…14,14…22,22…30 and
six compact zones of width4 from x30…54; each extends y2…14. Private zones are
**3×(8×12)+6×(4×12)=576 m²**; gallery120, end cores96 and end pockets48 give
the same **840 m²**. The count and study minima are met, but private/common
allocation differs substantially from A.

Each B dwelling can have openings at the gallery edge and court edge on the
same level. Fit an offset entry and privacy treatment beside any street-facing
living/sleeping room; test the screen/setback against actual light, furniture,
handle reach and passing views. If the whole gallery edge is instead made an
opaque wet/support strip, the desired two **occupied** aspects may be lost.
The [furnished family and4×12 compact studies](dwelling-fit.md#worked-family-fit-and-the-cost-of-a-study-place)
develop walls, deductions, furniture and operating routes within these gross
reservations, with explicit limitations; area alone proves no quality.
Gallery width, core fit and actual routes require their own project
checks. If they need more area, rerun this ledger rather than consume a flat.

A maisonette/interlocking version of B might release frontage on another level,
but it is rejected under this brief if sleeping or another essential activity
is reachable only by an internal stair. It is not a reason to reject the
single-level gallery alternative. A is provisionally favored for doorstep
privacy and generous family settings; B can become preferable if its smaller
family zones meet the real household brief and well-developed gallery privacy
and environmental conditions justify the common route. Requiring all gallery-
side principal rooms to avoid passers-by's view without any acceptable screen,
setback or room change would instead undermine B. A ground-floor tenancy that
cannot release A's three core-to-court passages would undermine A.

**Carry the change to ground and section.** Put direct resident core-to-court
paths at the assigned exits, then compare moving routine delivery/waste access
to the street edge with a controlled service crossing at a court end. Neither
option may simply delete required maintenance/emergency operations. If garage
space remains below the court, reserve its actual access and roof/landscape
section before assigning play and planting over it. Keep each upper wet/support
zone at the same position on the next typical floor, with explicit riser and
maintenance reservations; ground service distribution and structural supports
must be refitted to the three entrances or two end cores. This progression
establishes a spatial comparison, not a complete housing or garage design.

## Read project examples as evidence

Extract the observed mechanism before generalizing: trace real doors and common
routes on the plan, check levels in section and compare ground access to the
site plan. Separate observed labels, a reasonable hypothesis and unresolved
interfaces. A repeated typical wet-room arrangement is not a verified stack;
an exterior stepped section is not a complete step-free journey. Preserve title
or indicator conflicts and do not infer requirements from another student's work.
Reconstruct a relevant count/area boundary when reported indicators conflict
with the drawings; do not silently repair their figures or calculate a new
density from incompatible values. Exact example observations belong in
[sources](../../../sources/library.md#architecture-library-selected-content), not as runtime
instructions to imitate a named project.
