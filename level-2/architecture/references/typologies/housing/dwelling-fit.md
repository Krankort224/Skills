# Furnished dwelling fit

Use this reference to develop a dwelling reservation into rooms that can be
used. Select the relevant case after the method. Keep the general
[room-fit and wall-area procedure](../../space/planning/room-floor-and-section.md#construct-a-room-that-can-be-used)
and [dwelling/access family](access-and-court.md#choose-a-dwelling-and-access-family-together)
as their owners; a furniture fit does not re-establish the building's core,
jurisdiction or structure.

## Test a dwelling through its actual use

Declare the household and inventory before fitting the plan: sleeping places,
study/work, storage, guests, cooking/eating and sanitary/laundry activities.
A smaller kitchenette, absent study place or nightly furniture conversion is a
program change, not a free geometrical repair. Use actual project/product data
or explicit study objects and clearances; a book's illustration is not a legal
minimum or a furniture product specification.

1. Establish the gross assignment and finished faces, including each enclosing
   wall share, internal partition, threshold and shaft. Close one area ledger
   without overlaps. Keep furniture inside clear room area: do not subtract its
   footprint again and call the remainder the room's legal/net floor area.
2. Fit each requested object and the relevant use/door/standing envelope. A
   wardrobe's sliding front removes a door sweep, not the standing person;
   dining installation size may omit chair pull-out. Locate appliance fronts,
   room hinges, leaf thickness/parked position and handles deliberately.
3. Trace independent entrance-to-daily-space, bed-to-bathroom, child-to-study
   and guest-to-WC paths. A bathroom door behind cooking or another bedroom is
   a real daily/night privacy choice. A public gallery must not become an
   unacknowledged route through the family's occupied room.
4. Test the decisive simultaneous-use modes with those envelopes. Distinguish
   a permanent solid collision, a blocked route in the selected mode and a
   declared compatible time-sharing condition. If a dining chair blocks the
   only bedroom route, move the table/seating, add a bypass or change the room
   boundary; do not merely redraw the arrow. A folding bed requires both day
   and night plans, stored bedding/furniture and a workable changeover.
5. Give wet rooms and kitchens their actual fixture inventory, service chase
   and maintenance approach. Overlay the same shaft/fixture organization on
   the next floor; do not treat pipework as a zero-width line. Carry changed
   equipment or doors into the ventilation, moisture, acoustic and structural
   interfaces without claiming those disciplines are resolved by a plan.
6. State exactly what passed, which condition limits the result and how a
   counterexample reverses it. Preserve furniture dimensions across a compared
   repair unless the changed inventory is explicitly accepted. Clearances in
   an original study are assumptions to verify, not source-backed code values.

For a family evening, test occupied dining while someone reaches the private
hall and bathroom; for a narrow compact home, test the permanent/deployed bed,
desk chair and storage against the night route. A visually tidy unoccupied plan
is insufficient evidence for either mode.

## Operating conditions and source boundary

Case data include hinges,0.90 trial leaves, quarter-disc sweeps,0.04 parked-leaf
thickness, physical threshold openings and protected route rectangles. Those
leaves are not a product's verified framed clear width. The declared normal
mode checks permanent bodies, pulled dining/desk chairs, wardrobe standing,
kitchen and sofa use against private routes. A person does not occupy a door
sweep while the door moves; sequence entry and operation explicitly.

Both bathrooms retain WC, basin, shower and washer plus a nonzero shaft.
Their use zones are single-user/time-shared: WC and basin operation overlap;
the family washer requires the bathroom door closed, and compact shower use
requires entry/door closure first. Shower screens are non-swinging study
assumptions. These are disclosed operating conditions, not simultaneous fixture
capacity. Actual appliances, handles, moisture/electrical/extraction/service
details and closed-door emergency access can change the choice. Repeat the
shaft coordinates upstairs, then develop real branches and maintenance access.

31/171 and33/173 informed entry/door, kitchen, dining operation, sleeping/work,
storage and wet-service questions. In particular, the dining table footnote
excludes chair pull-out from one installation-area measure. No furniture,
corridor, fixture or foreign-code number was adopted. All case dimensions and
furniture are original study inputs; source illustrations were not copied.
Geometric passage of a declared scenario does not establish environmental,
structural, legal or household acceptance of the whole dwelling.
