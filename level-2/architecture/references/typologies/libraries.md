## Libraries as reader and collection systems

**Decision.** Choose how readers find and use material and how that material
returns to availability. Confirm open-access versus staff-fetched stock,
collection formats and growth/discard policy, individual versus social use,
staffing and independent event hours. Seat count alone does not define a library.

1. Follow a first-time visitor from entry/help to browsing, reading or a group
   room and departure. Locate information and issue/return functions at a
   meaningful choice or controlled interface. A desk must support actual staff
   work and views; a centrally drawn symbol does not establish supervision of
   tall shelves, remote rooms or another floor. State any staffing assumption.
2. Follow a returned or new item through receipt, temporary holding, checking or
   processing, sorting and reshelving. Reserve the carts, work surfaces and
   waiting stock while those stages occur. For staff-fetched material, also trace
   request → retrieval → handover → return. Keep public queues and quiet seats
   out of that working circuit. Test where deliveries and discarded/repair items
   go; a return slot without accessible holding is an incomplete operation.
3. Distinguish browsing stock from quiet work and conversational/group activity.
   Compare a legible open field with quiet enclosed rooms against separated
   departments around a common route. The open field can simplify orientation
   and flexible browsing, but carries speech and interruptions. Departmental
   separation can protect quieter use while increasing thresholds, duplicated
   help points or staff travel. Do not make the quiet reading zone the passage
   to a group/event room or another department.
4. Fit collection capacity from the actual shelf system, usable shelf lengths,
   formats, accessible reach and a stated occupation/growth assumption. Keep
   shelf access, browsing, reader seating and cart movement distinct. More books
   cannot be added by filling a main cross-route or removing all sorting space.
   Mobile/high-density stock changes retrieval, loading and environmental needs;
   it is a separately coordinated option, not spare capacity in an ordinary room.
5. Compare one main public level with stacked departments. A broad level may
   reduce reader/staff vertical trips and simplify oversight, but takes site
   frontage and may leave a deep interior. Stacking can separate noisy and quiet
   uses or fit a constrained site, but needs an intelligible reader route and
   a workable stock/cart connection through every level. A tall atrium joins
   views and can also join sound; a quiet upper gallery is an unproven hypothesis.
   Place sustained reading with deliberate exterior/light relationships and
   protect collections from unsuitable exposure; establish performance separately.
6. Make an independently used event/group room and its support reachable when
   collection access closes. Decide which desk, lobby, toilet, cloak/storage and
   staff facilities remain available. Decide whether returned items can still be
   received and who processes them next; independent events do not imply an
   unattended open collection.

**Critical condition.** Collection movement consumes real floor and staff time.
Changing shelving, stack height or stock density changes structural and access
questions; no load value is adopted here. Rare collections, preservation archives,
security systems and specialist environmental control exceed this public-library
method. A spatially controlled boundary is not a verified theft/fire strategy.

**Small stock/support choice.** In an original trial, six double-faced shelf bays
each have four usable shelves per face and 0.90 m usable length per shelf:
6 × 2 × 4 × 0.90 = **43.20 linear m**. These are hypothetical inventory inputs,
not a product or recommended spacing. A stated collection occupies 38 linear m;
returned items from that same collection are already included in the 38, rather
than new stock. Removing one bay to make a sorting pocket leaves **36 linear m**,
so the apparently helpful support repair loses 2 linear m of required storage
even before any growth/working reserve. Compare keeping six bays and reducing
an optional seating setting, or securing another staff-side sorting location.
Choose only after fitting the pocket, carts and retained seating, and agreeing
any change to seat capacity. This arithmetic checks shelf length; it does not
prove accessible reach, shelf loading, plan fit or a sufficient collection reserve.
