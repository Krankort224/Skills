# Style directions and selected cases

Use this selector for a requested direction or a relationship that needs a
verified precedent. It is not a compulsory history survey. Read the selected
profile's method through its next peer heading, including conditions and checks;
open the linked object analysis only when that example helps the decision.
For a named object not in this corpus, use [read before classifying](style-and-precedent-methods.md#read-an-object-before-classifying-it)
and establish its own evidence rather than pretending it is covered.

## Declared perimeter

The installed corpus develops selected Roman classical, Gothic, Renaissance,
Baroque, Catalan Modernisme/Art Nouveau, modernist, organic and Brutalist
relationships through identified objects. These are useful lenses with overlaps,
not mutually exclusive bins or a genealogy of all world architecture. A Roman
building, a regional movement and an architect's approach are not equivalent
kinds of classification. Use the profile's source-qualified meaning.

The particular object evidence concerns Rome, Chartres, Palladio's Veneto,
Borromini's Rome, Gaudí's Barcelona, a French modernist villa, Wright's
Pennsylvania house and London's Barbican. It does not establish other regions,
periods or every variation within these directions. This declared perimeter
contains methods and examples; it does not certify exhaustive stylistic
competence. A request about an unrepresented tradition needs its own qualified
sources and substantive method, with the coverage gap stated.

Profiles own general characteristics, variations and adaptation tensions. Cases
under `assets/styles/<style>/<building>/` own their identified building, actual
inspected evidence, analysis and conditional transfer. A case folder is an
address for an authored analysis, not a claim that its photographs or plans are
licensed assets. General composition, spatial organization, envelope and
environmental performance remain with their existing references.

## Select by the design question

| Question or intended relationship | Read selectively | Keep visible |
| --- | --- | --- |
| A legible principal space, ordered threshold, repeated edge or centre | Classical/Renaissance methods in [historical directions](style-classical-gothic-renaissance.md#contents), then the relevant Pantheon or Rotonda case | A ceremonial centre does not resolve independent room access, changed use or the present approach. |
| Vertical emphasis, a repeated longitudinal sequence or articulated support/light edge | [Gothic method](style-classical-gothic-renaissance.md#gothic-repeated-bays-sectional-hierarchy-and-filtered-light), then Chartres | Visible articulation does not establish structural action or daylight performance; the church's program is not a generic public-building brief. |
| Curved enclosure, changing views and hierarchy in a constrained setting | [Baroque method](style-baroque-and-art-nouveau.md#baroque), then San Carlo | Distinguish facade, church, dome and adjoining complex; one curve is not the whole spatial system. |
| Integrated craft, material transitions, inhabited facade depth and characteristic detail | [Art Nouveau/Modernisme method](style-baroque-and-art-nouveau.md#art-nouveau), then Casa Batlló | Regional/individual specificity and remodelled fabric matter; visible surface alone does not prove the plan or construction. |
| A route that generates section, distinct enclosure/support expression or an abstract repeated framework | [Modernist method](style-modern-organic-and-brutalist.md#modernism-frame-planes-and-a-composed-route), then Villa Savoye | A promenade or apparent free plan must still accommodate the actual program, route, privacy and engineering interfaces. |
| Site-specific horizontal/vertical relationships and inside/outside thresholds | [Organic method](style-modern-organic-and-brutalist.md#organic-architecture-site-occupation-and-a-coherent-whole), then Fallingwater | Site attachment is a relationship to test; copying a cantilever, water setting or historic envelope proves no present performance. |
| Collective public/private levels, articulated concrete mass and linked outdoor/inside realms | [Brutalist method](style-modern-organic-and-brutalist.md#brutalism-articulated-mass-construction-and-collective-space), then Barbican | A visible material is not a complete definition. Distinguish urban organization, continued operation and individual building evidence. |

These are choices of method, not compulsory stylistic assignments. One brief can
test a centralized relationship, a layered threshold or articulated sequence
without claiming a new building belongs to the historical movement.

## Use a case without importing its project

Identify the actual source observation, the mechanism inferred from it and the
new brief's changed condition. Use [mechanism transfer](style-and-precedent-methods.md#transfer-a-mechanism-into-a-changed-brief)
to develop physical alternatives and a counterexample; use
[same-brief comparison](style-and-precedent-methods.md#compare-directions-through-the-same-brief)
when choosing between them. Do not treat the case's date, name or successful
image as evidence of a new project's capacity, engineering, climate response,
accessibility, cost or permissions.

Each case identifies its inspected text/views and absent evidence. A precise
pointer to a drawing is useful provenance but is not a claim that its geometry
was read. Missing or later-altered material limits the corresponding analysis.
Uncertain attribution or disagreement remains visible, not repaired by a style
label. See [sources](sources.md) for identity, reading scope and adaptation, and
the [rights boundary](../THIRD_PARTY_NOTICES.md) before including an external
image in a later requested deliverable.
