# Spatial relationships and movement

Use this knowledge when an adjacency diagram or a named plan family does not yet
explain how people experience, occupy and close the spaces. A whole-building
organization and the relationship between two rooms are different decisions:
a linear building can contain an enclosed room, overlapping activity fields and
a shared intermediate place. Use the applicable sections below with actual room
geometry; these are design mechanisms, not prescribed dimensions or performance.

## Choose how spaces meet

Start with the required contact: shared activity, visual connection, direct
access, acoustic separation or independent closure. Then construct a spatial
relationship rather than letting touching rectangles decide it.

| Relationship | How to construct it and when it helps | What changes the choice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| One space inside another | Put a smaller, recognizable setting within a larger occupied field: a consultation enclosure in a public hall, or a reading alcove within a library. Make the surrounding space wide and continuous enough to have its own use and identity. Contrast in orientation, height or enclosure can distinguish the inner setting without a new building wing. | The inner room gains external light, outlook and access through its host unless independently connected. Enlarging it can reduce the host to leftover passages; rotation can produce unusable wedges. Test the ring after walls, doors, furniture and host activity are placed. An open-topped enclosure does not provide independent acoustic/environmental separation. |
| Two fields overlap | Let compatible activities share an actual zone: an exhibition edge and welcome area may meet in a place for demonstration. Decide whether that shared zone belongs jointly to both, primarily to one, or becomes a third named place. | The overlap cannot simultaneously be a protected quiet setting and a busy crossing. Count its physical area once, test concurrent use and retain a bypass where closure or occupation would break access. A boundary between enclosed rooms is not an overlap merely because their diagram outlines cross. |
| Spaces are adjacent | Give each room a complete usable envelope and deliberately choose the intervening boundary: enclosing wall with a controlled opening; glazed separation; freestanding screen; columns; or a continuous floor with a material/ceiling change. The latter choices can distinguish settings while keeping a larger visual field. | Visibility, passage and acoustic separation are independent. A screen or finish change may organize perception but cannot perform the job of a closed wall. A floor step changes access as well as emphasis; prefer another cue when the brief needs a common level. Refit the opening and furniture at the shared boundary. |
| Spaces share an intermediate place | Connect otherwise separate groups through a foyer, common room, gallery, court or covered link. Choose its shape and scale for its own activity, orientation and arrival, not just the residual gap between rooms. It can become the principal destination while the linked rooms remain independently controlled. | The mediator may become a bottleneck, noisy dependency or locked gate. A long link trades a shared place for travel; an enlarged center consumes area and couples groups operationally. Test each connected room with another closed, and test the shared place occupied rather than empty. |

Change one decisive relationship when generating an alternative. For example,
compare a quiet room *inside* an open learning hall with a quiet room *beside*
it, reached through a common threshold. The first gives a strong shared setting
but depends on the host for outlook and access; the second can gain a direct
external edge and independent closure at the cost of another boundary and route.
Do not claim the first is acoustically private because its outline is enclosed.

Combine relationships only at identified interfaces. An intermediate foyer can
contain a small reception enclosure; it need not become a separate room at every
change of activity. When two groups need incompatible sound or opening hours,
change their boundary or insert a real buffer before decorating the shared field.
Return to [room fit](planning-and-section.md#construct-a-room-that-can-be-used)
for action envelopes and wall/door reservations.

## Give the linking space a program and a boundary

Decide whether the link is primarily a route, a place to stay, or both. A route
can widen into a meeting place; a common room can contain a clear route. In both
cases locate the occupied setting and movement separately before calling the
same rectangle available to each.

Place the link's own destination, pause/queue and usable edges. Orient it by an
external view, recognizable room entry or section/light change where that helps
the brief. A leftover polygon can be a useful common place, but only after its
corners, furniture and approach actually fit. If the form of adjoining rooms
leaves thin tails, give them a service/threshold purpose or simplify the junction.

For shared floor accounting use the union of the physical fields. If an activity
field A and a route B genuinely share O, the combined footprint is A+B−O; this
does not establish that their uses can coincide. If a clear route must remain
available, remove its occupied envelope from the concurrent activity capacity.
An event layout and everyday layout may share floor area at different times;
identify who changes the arrangement and whether independent access survives.
Use the existing [area ledger](program/area-ledger.md#how-can-areas-be-counted-once-and-compared)
for walls, definitions and allowances rather than creating a second area basis.

In section, test the mediator as a real volume. A lower linking ceiling can
distinguish two taller rooms; a high common room can orient several lower ones.
It also changes where light, sound and services travel. Keep any enclosing
separation continuous where the program requires it; a decorative ceiling edge
cannot close a sound path. A shared outdoor court needs the exposure, levels
and operation checks in [site and context](site-and-context.md#compose-entrance-threshold-and-outdoor-room).

## Place movement beside through or into a space

Choose the relationship separately for each consequential journey, including
service and after-hours use. The same room can terminate one visitor journey
and be passed by another. Record where the path meets the occupied setting.

| Path relationship | Architectural move | Discriminating test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pass beside | Keep a continuous path at an edge; use short thresholds to enter rooms. Room activity can stay intact and close independently. An opening or glazed edge can still reveal the destination. | Fit the path, door approaches and pause points simultaneously. A display, bench or door swing must not turn the supposed bypass back into travel through the activity. Avoid placing every private work position directly against the passing audience. |
| Pass through | Let the route cross an activity field, centrally, obliquely or at its edge. This can make browsing, exhibition or shared exchange part of arrival; the line divides the field into actual places to pause. | Furnish both sides and test a peak or closed mode. If passage interrupts a seminar or the locked exhibition severs access to a later room, provide an independent route or change the operating proposition. Crossing an empty room proves little. |
| Arrive and stop | Make the room a destination: its entry, view or brighter/taller volume resolves the approach. This can give a quiet reading place or gathering room a clear address. | Do not put an unrelated necessary destination beyond it. A focal view and a public door need not coincide: a view can terminate at glazing while circulation turns beside it. Show the turn and cue so the apparent destination does not conceal the usable route. |

In a sequence, choose what is revealed at approach, threshold and first decision
inside. A direct aligned view can orient immediately; a turned sequence can
protect privacy and reveal a shared place gradually. At each turn supply a
visible cue or destination from the real walking position. Compression can be
made with ceiling, light, width or enclosure without changing the floor level.
Check usable height and service depth before describing that contrast as built.

Use two overlays: the desired visual sequence and the required open/closed route.
Keep an intentional view through a room if useful, but relocate actual movement
when that room must be private or closed. The design has failed if the attractive
sequence works only while every door is open. Ordinary access here is distinct
from evacuation and applicable inclusive-use requirements selected through
[requirements](requirements.md).

## Compose for the actual approach and sequence

A composition is encountered from positions and along routes. Establish which
edges can be seen, from how far away, and in what order. A broad frontal view,
movement around a volume, a vertical landmark and a view directed into depth
are useful design lenses; a project can combine them. They are neither building
classifications nor a rule that all four views must be produced.

- For a predominantly frontal arrival, organize the entrance, occupied bays and
  depth of the threshold so they read from the available approach. If a narrow
  sidewalk prevents seeing the whole elevation, work with successive bays, an
  oblique view and the near entrance rather than relying on an imaginary plaza.
- Where movement around the object matters, resolve corners and changing solid/
  opening relationships across the visible faces. A coherent perimeter does not
  require identical facades; use, exposure and servicing may need differences.
- For a sequence into depth, place a real destination or view at the end and
  use intervening edges, gaps and thresholds to sustain orientation. Repetition
  can link unequal parts; it need not force identical street fronts or rooms.
- Where a vertical element marks arrival, check whether its base actually leads
  to the entrance and how it is seen nearby. An elevated sign in a distant view
  cannot repair an obscure door at ground level.

Draw or describe the view at the approach, at the threshold and after the first
turn alongside the same plan/section. If the next destination disappears, change
an opening, recess, partition termination or orienting cue before adding signage
as the only repair. If a proposed height or opening conflicts with fixed structure,
change the cue or sequence within that geometry. Shape, color and symmetry do
not carry guaranteed emotions; evaluate the intended character in this context.

## Worked revision of a common room

This is an original hypothetical study, not a library drawing or coursework
decision. Fix an **internal clear shell** x=0…18 m, y=0…12 m, common finished
floor 0.000 and roof underside +4.200 m. Keep entry (7.2,0) and garden door
(7.2,12). The brief asks for independent evening access to the reading room while
workshop and seminar are closed, plus a place for temporary shared displays.
Assume 0.20 m partition reservations; opening sizes, furniture and performance
are still project selections.

| Clear field | Coordinates, m | Area, m² |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Workshop | x=0…6, y=0…8 | 48.00 |
| Reading | x=0…6, y=8.2…12 | 22.80 |
| Support | x=12…18, y=0…5.8 | 34.80 |
| Seminar | x=12…18, y=6…12 | 36.00 |
| Common linking space | x=6.2…11.8, y=0…12 | 67.20 |

Vertical partition reservations x=6…6.2 and 11.8…12 consume 4.80 m².
Horizontal reservations within the two room bands consume 2.40 m².
`48+22.8+34.8+36+67.2+4.8+2.4 = 216 m² = 18×12`.
This is a nominal partition-reservation ledger, including door zones, not a
formal room-area measurement. The clear room dimensions do not establish a
regulatory capacity or a furnished fit for an unspecified use.

**R0: an attractive overlap fails its operation.** An initial display occupies
x=6.2…11.8, y=4…8 across the whole common space. It promises both 22.40 m² of
uninterrupted display and passage to reading/garden. An open browsing arrangement
may share this field, but a booked demonstration or closed display cuts off the
only north–south route. Relabeling the field a foyer does not fix the conflict.

**R1: retain the common room, change the path relationship.** Reserve a straight
2.00 m **brief-selected trial** path at x=6.2…8.2, y=0…12: 24.00 m². Move the
display to x=8.4…11.6, y=2…7.6: 17.92 m², with its own occupied envelope entirely
off that path. The 67.20 m² common space contains both; its other 25.28 m² are
remaining pause/connector/buffer space, not another complete activity allocation.
Place the reading opening in its east boundary around y=10.5, connecting directly
to the retained path. Workshop access around y=4 can close independently; the
door opens into its room under this trial assumption. Put the seminar opening
around y=10.5 and preserve a connector across the north part of the commons,
outside the display rectangle. Verify actual door, approach and furniture
envelopes before fixing any of these coordinates as construction dimensions.

The north garden view now orients arrival along the west side of a shared place,
rather than requiring travel through the displayed work. Use a lower ceiling
over the path only if it fits services beneath the fixed roof; the remainder can
retain greater height. Reading stays an adjacent enclosed room, not an open nook
in the noisy common field. Its separation must be developed through the ceiling
interface; neither the view nor a change of ceiling finish provides sound privacy.

**Counterexample and bounded revision.** If a later brief needs an immersive
installation occupying the *entire* 5.6 m width, R1 cannot honestly retain both
that occupation and independent reading access. Keep the fixed entry/garden
doors and compare an enclosed passage carved from the installation envelope
with an alternate door/route through an allowed neighboring zone. The former
reduces the installation; the latter is usable only if that zone can remain open
and the new doorway is allowed. If neither condition can change, report the
incompatibility. Do not call the 24 m² passage simultaneous installation capacity
or move a fixed door to hide the failure.
