# Timber members, joints and weathering

Use the affected question for a timber frame, exposed member, secondary roof
support or timber-to-masonry interface. Develop the spatial and material
consequences of the joint; connection capacity, stiffness, fire resistance,
durability class and member sizing require actual project evidence. A historical
atlas detail supplies a mechanism to question, not an approved connection.

## Choose support topology and visible expression together

Locate the actual members, bearings and load recipients before choosing a
connector's appearance. Name what each interface does: direct compression
bearing, gravity transfer through a hanger, uplift tie, lateral restraint,
rotation or an intentionally permitted translation. One symbol does not establish
all these roles. Check the [complete restraint path](roof-and-span.md#keep-the-support-system-connected-in-three-dimensions)
when the joint changes the frame's mechanism.

| Arrangement | Spatial and architectural consequence | Failure to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Secondary member seated above a primary beam | The overlapping depth can express a clear hierarchy and leave visible bearing. It raises the roof or lowers the primary soffit for a fixed height cap. Blocking/cleats or straps occupy additional faces. | A bearing alone does not establish resistance to uplift, roll or longitudinal movement. Do not remove a restraint to simplify the visible joint. |
| Above-beam support with metal overstraps | Can retain the same stacked geometry while changing the visible connection language and tie arrangement; timber stops/cleats may remain. | Substituting an overstrap arrangement while retaining the stack does not remove the secondary depth above the primary. Top and side fixings can become inaccessible below the finished roof. |
| Secondary member framed into a primary's side | Can align member tops and reduce the structural stack, with an explicitly visible shoe or another engineered connection. | Gravity must reach the primary through the real seat/fastener arrangement. Plates, bolt ends and tool paths occupy space; an apparently flush joint is not automatically concealed, moment-resisting or buildable. |
| Continuous beam over posts versus separate beam ends on a cap | Continuous timber can give an uninterrupted line; separate members change splices, assembly length and the visible rhythm. | Preserve actual bearing and end support. A decorative cover over an unsupported splice does not restore continuity; a source's generic splice location is not a project force analysis. |
| Beam end in masonry pocket versus accessible bracket/seat | A pocket can conceal the interface; an external seat can expose it for inspection and make the timber/masonry distinction legible. | Check wetting/drying, installation and replacement as well as bearing. A sealed wet pocket or seat fixed only to cladding defeats the concept. |

Make a section stack with the same fixed finished floor, required clear envelope,
roof cap and weathering package for every alternative. Include the deepest cap,
shoe, bolt or crossing service where it actually occurs; compare local minima,
not just the attractive middle of the bay. Recalculate the roof and soffit when
the support topology changes. A favorable dimensional stack establishes a
reservation only, not the proposed member's capacity.

Decide whether the visible timber is primary structure, secondary framing,
cladding or applied ornament. Each can have a legitimate architectural purpose;
the label and detail should agree. A timber triangle drawn on a masonry gable
does not prove a roof truss or justify removing support behind it. Glued-laminated
members, solid sawn timber and plywood components also need distinct fabrication,
exposure and connection evidence; do not transfer a glulam atlas scheme to any
wood product merely because the surface looks similar.

## Reserve assembly and replacement access

Test the joint as a sequence with the proposed enclosure in place:

1. Identify what arrives as a fabricated member/assembly, its handling path and
   how it is supported before the final restraint system exists. The finished
   building's stability is not evidence for a safe erection stage.
2. Place the seat/shoe, introduce the timber and locate each fastener's insertion
   direction, opposite nut/washer face and required tool or driving space. Check
   these in plan and section against walls, adjacent members and finishes.
3. State when roof/deck, insulation, glazing and closure pieces are fitted. A
   connection installed before a wall is closed may still be impossible to
   inspect or remove afterward. Label a cover removable only if its fixings,
   removal direction and access are actually available.
4. Reverse the intended repair sequence: reach fixings, safely unload/support the
   affected member, remove closures and extract the component without relying on
   glass, finish or an unverified adjacent beam as temporary support. Design
   temporary works separately; a reversible fixing does not permit replacement
   under load.

A top-seated secondary calls for testing placement from above or demonstrating
another actual assembly path, such as an appropriate preassembled unit or lateral
insertion. An overstrap needs top and side fixing access, potentially requiring
roof removal later. A through-bolted
face shoe needs its opposite primary face as well as the secondary fixing face.
An acute-angle junction can leave a dowel's driving direction physically blocked
even when the final drawing fits. Moving to a prefabricated/alternative connection
or changing the member angle may solve that problem, with different fabrication,
lifting and visible-joint costs. Do not prescribe an improvised fastener or
extra field-cut hole as the repair.

Compare exposed hardware with a removable lining and a permanently concealed
connection. Exposure aids some inspection but also changes weather/fire exposure
and appearance; concealment may need larger sections, protection or access
closures. Establish actual protection requirements rather than assigning a
generic fire rating to metal or timber. A maintenance panel must not casually
interrupt a required protective enclosure. Finish selection follows substrate,
exposure and repair access; paint cannot make an inaccessible joint replaceable.

## Coordinate grain and differential movement

Draw the grain directions and identify the actual material/product and assembly
moisture condition. Timber movement is directional: changes across the grain
and accumulated cross-grain depths can matter differently from the length of a
post. Drying can also distort a member. Do not treat hardwood/softwood names as
strength grades, apply one shrinkage percentage to every axis, or assume a
coating stops moisture movement. Glulam manufacturing does not eliminate the
need for product-specific moisture and connection information.

At a masonry, glazing or lining interface, distinguish timber moisture movement,
elastic deflection/creep, joint slip, adjacent-material movement and construction
tolerance. Establish their relevant directions and combinations with the project
team; do not add unrelated extremes or invent a predicted value. Place the
resulting movement space and support it as a physical joint: rigid packing,
cladding screws, a fixed service or a sill return must not bridge the intended
release. Moving the joint may change visible shadow lines, light aperture and
the weather/air-control transition.

For example, with an **expressly supplied trial** downward relative displacement
of 20 mm and installation allowance of 5 mm, a proposed 15 mm head space has
`15−20−5=−10 mm` residual room. A 35 mm space gives 10 mm under that assumption,
but fails by 5 mm if displacement becomes 35 mm. Those are geometric sensitivity
checks, not timber movement predictions. Develop the real frame attachment,
closure and seal movement range; maintain required clear door/light dimensions
when increasing the closure zone.

An engineered sliding/rotating support addresses defined degrees of freedom.
It is not a reason to slot every base or loosen ties. Keep gravity support,
uplift/lateral retention and travel limits consistent with the structural
mechanism. Conversely, a base drawn with anchors is not proof of adequate
rotational fixity. If actual base stiffness is unknown, retain the coordinated
braced-system proposal or obtain a designed moment system before removing its
braces. Do not notch or cut a loaded timber member to invent clearance for an
anchor, service or tie; change the reservation and obtain the connection design.

## Keep exposed ends and bases able to shed water and dry

At an exposed timber end, cap, pocket or base, trace how water arrives by roof
runoff, wind-driven rain, splash, capillary contact and condensation; then show
how it leaves and how wetted timber can dry. A waterproof-looking coat does not
resolve trapped water. Select actual exposure, timber/product suitability,
protective treatment, compatible metals and maintenance strategy; no generic
moisture threshold or species name guarantees freedom from decay.

For a post on concrete/masonry, separate the timber end from persistent wet
contact through an actual designed support. Develop the concrete/plinth surface,
shoe/anchor, end-grain exposure, drainage and accessible inspection together.
A thin membrane in a structural sketch does not establish a drained, weather-safe
base, and a metal shoe can itself collect water. If a decorative skirt conceals
the gap and traps splash/debris, revise its geometry or omit it; keep the timber
end and anchor inspection accessible. A raised base changes the visible plinth,
nearby seating/route and potential trip/impact condition; show it in plan.

At a wall seat, keep the roof support independent of finish-only layers and
retain the [envelope control functions](envelope-and-assemblies.md#develop-four-connected-junctions).
Compare a designed ledger attachment to the actual backing with an independent
post line. The ledger can clear the near-wall floor but introduces anchors,
thermal/weather interfaces and dependence on existing support evidence. An
independent frame adds posts and its own stability/foundations; close proximity
to the wall still needs a moving weather junction. Neither option is resolved
by sealing timber into a recess.

Protect exposed horizontal ledges and end grain without creating a sealed trough;
coordinate caps/flashings, ends and drips with the real connection and drying
paths. Send roof discharge to a maintainable outlet and suitable ground route,
not onto a base or doorway. Use the [roof water method](roof-and-span.md#resolve-roof-water-in-plan-and-at-the-junction)
for collection/overflow. Finishes may intentionally reveal grain or make a more
opaque surface; their compatible preparation and renewal access are part of the
design. Do not import historical chemical-treatment recipes or count a finish
as structural, fire or moisture verification.

## Worked terrace revision

This original **2D architectural study** uses hypothetical supplied reservations,
not a source plan or a sized timber frame. Coordinates are metres. Finished
veranda floor is z=0; the existing finished masonry facade lies at y=0. The
covered rectangle is x=0…6,y=0…3, with a fixed inward-opening house door at
x=2.40…3.60. Keep a clear route x=2.25…3.75 across the full depth, required trial
clear height 2.60 and maximum roof plane +3.25. These are this study's inputs,
not code minima. Actual ground, loads, member/connection capacity and roof
specification remain unknown.

**T0 — diagnose the attractive but incomplete proposal.** A timber frame has
four 0.20-square trial posts centered at x=0.25/5.75,y=0.25/2.75. Primary beams
run along x, top+3.00,depth0.30. Secondary members span between the two primary
lines, depth0.20, seated above them; trial roof/weathering depth at the high
north edge is0.10. The roof high plane is `3.00+0.20+0.10=3.30`, exceeding the
cap by0.05. Under-primary clearance is2.70, so the problem is not solved by
checking headroom alone. A diagonal across the central door, boxed timber bases
against wet paving and a roof outlet at the door add independent failures.

**T1 — compare two real section changes.** Retain the door and plan:

| Hypothetical alternative | Roof high plane | Lowest structural soffit | Consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lower the primary top to2.95; keep the secondary above | 2.95+0.20+0.10=3.25 | 2.95−0.30=2.65 | Fits both trial boundaries with no roof-cap margin and0.05 headroom margin. Keeps the stacked timber expression; tolerance/deflection/protection can invalidate it. |
| Keep primary top3.00; frame the secondary into its side with tops aligned | 3.00+0.10=3.10 | min(3.00−0.30,3.00−0.20)=2.70 | Gives0.15 roof-cap and0.10 headroom margins before actual connection/protection projections. Changes the visible joint and requires real shoe, opposite-face and insertion access. |

Prefer the second **conditionally** for a lower roof edge and visible primary
beam, retaining the first as the simpler stacked alternative if connection or
repair access defeats the flush option. Substituting the overstrap arrangement
while retaining the stacked geometry would leave T0's3.30 high plane unchanged.
Neither trial proves a5.50 m primary or2.50 m secondary span, their cantilevers,
joint capacity or final roof package.

**T2 — repair plan, erection and wall interface.** Reserve four corner zones:
x=0…1.25 or4.75…6, combined with y=0…0.65 or2.35…3. Locate posts, prospective
end-bay bracing, shoes and access inside these zones where feasible; develop
the connected roof-plane/end-frame restraint separately. The central route is
disjoint from them. The area ledger is `18=3.25 corner reservations+4.50 route+
10.25 remaining field`. The posts'0.16 m² actual footprint is contained in3.25,
not an extra subtraction. This ledger does not establish furniture capacity,
that all braces fit or that the remainder is legal/net usable area.

Use the independent four-post concept while existing masonry anchorage is
unverified; compare a ledger only with actual backing/support evidence. A
face-shoe installed on the rear primary can have its opposite bolt face blocked
by the existing wall. With an additional **trial** primary width0.20 centered at
y=0.25, its rear face is y=0.15. A supplied0.20 tool-approach requirement fails
in that0.15 wall gap. Move the rear post/beam line to y=0.40: the rear face at0.30
gives0.10 excess over the trial approach. The post outline y=0.30…0.50 still
fits the rear corner reservation, without entering the central route. The
secondary center span changes from2.50 to2.35; its rear roof cantilever grows
from0.25 to0.40. Reopen member/support checks for that change rather than claiming
it is structurally harmless.

Keep the corresponding below-joint closure independently removable and test tool
reach, bolt extraction and member removal with that wall gap; the0.30 gap proves
only the supplied approach reservation, not a complete maintenance operation.
Compare a different engineered connection if actual tool/fastener needs exceed
it. Preassembly alone does not prove future replaceability. Keep primary-to-post
caps separate from secondary shoes. Do not
declare the corner bases moment-fixed to erase the bracing reservation. Temporary
restraint is needed before the roof's final connected system is established.

**T3 — repair water and base in section.** For the selected trial, roof plane
z=3.10 at y=0 falls to3.04 at y=3: a **study fall**0.06/3=2%, not a prescribed
slope. A level secondary top3.00 leaves0.10…0.04 of conceptual weathering depth;
if the actual assembly cannot work in that space, revise the roof stack. Put
collection on the garden edge and route discharge away from the central route
and four bases; resolve actual gutter fall, capacity, overflow and ground levels.
At the wall, a compatible moving flashing/control transition replaces the idea
of sealing beam ends into masonry. It is not a final watertight detail.

The base trial has outside paving z=−0.10, concrete-plinth top+0.20 and timber
end+0.35, giving0.15 between timber and plinth. These elevations illustrate
separation and inspection, **not a minimum weather clearance** or a sized shoe.
Develop a support that carries the actual actions while water sheds from the
plinth/shoe and the end can dry. Remove T0's sealed skirt; if a cover is retained,
make it independently removable with a real drainage/inspection path. Do not
count the apparent gap as effective if debris, planting or later paving bridges
it. Recheck route/impact conditions when the shoe/plinth exceeds the trial post
footprint or corner reservation.

**Counterexamples and revision limits.** A shoe that projects0.18 below the
primary soffit reduces the selected local clearance to2.52, failing2.60 despite
the aligned tops. A0.20 required weathering package raises the flush roof high
plane to3.20 and leaves0.05 cap margin; a0.30 package reaches3.30 and fails.
A permanent wall lining over the opposite bolt face defeats the claimed repair
access. Coating the boxed base leaves T0's wet trap. Each failure changes a
specific geometry, sequence or system decision; a new timber finish alone cannot
repair it. Choose again with actual capacity, movement, exposure/protection,
erection, access and roof evidence before accepting the terrace.
