# Performance and coordination

Use this reference for conceptual architectural trade-offs, not engineering
sign-off. Connect decisions to [program and space](program-and-space.md),
[requirements](requirements.md), and [calculators](calculators.md).

## Envelope and materials

Describe roof, walls, openings and ground interface as coordinated assemblies.
Consider exposure, water shedding and drainage, continuity of air and thermal
control, moisture risk, movement, fire strategy, maintainability and replacement.
The right layer order and details depend on local conditions and the assembly;
reserve them for appropriate technical analysis and specialist review.

For each candidate material record source and proposed application, durability,
availability, finish and weathering, maintenance, relative cost and any
substantiated environmental data. Record product, system boundary, date and
units before comparing embodied impacts; unsupported values are unknown.
Compatibility at junctions matters as much as individual products.

## Daylight, solar and thermal comfort

- Record location and climate when known, north, neighboring obstructions,
  orientation, occupied hours, room depth and intended activities.
- Explore opening location and depth, room shape, light distribution, shading,
  views, glare, privacy and electric-light needs together. More glazing is not
  automatically better; direct sun, glare and heat loss or gain also matter.
- Compare exposure and shading by facade and season. Document assumed sun and
  obstruction conditions; a sketch is not a daylight or solar simulation.
- Consider insulation continuity, glazing properties, air leakage, ventilation,
  thermal mass and occupancy together. A conceptual strategy is not a modeled
  energy result or a guarantee of comfort.
- Identify spaces likely to need detailed daylight, overheating, moisture or
  energy analysis. Refer to [calculators](calculators.md) for eligible estimates
  and their limits; do not invent inputs or implied precision.

## Acoustics

Identify noise sources, quiet receivers and occupancy patterns. Use separation,
room placement, buffer spaces, door positions and surface character as early
levers. Distinguish sound isolation between rooms from reverberation within a
room and from mechanical noise. No assumed wall rating or room finish proves
performance; request an acoustic specialist and project-specific targets when
relevant.

## Coordinate structure and services

- Mark tentative structural grid, spans, load paths and vertical supports on the
  plan. Test whether they interfere with program, openings or future change.
  Engineering determines loads, member sizes, stability and foundations.
- Reserve indicative plant, riser and distribution space; coordinate routes,
  ceiling zones, shaft continuity, maintenance access and external equipment.
  Confirm plant and shaft sizes with services designers, not generic allowances.
- Identify conflicts at facade, roof, structure, openings and penetrations;
  assign an owner, decision date and required information to each issue.
- Track vertical stacking of wet spaces and shafts, daylight versus privacy,
  structural depth versus clear height, and access versus envelope continuity.
- Agree references, levels, units and revision identifiers across disciplines.
  Revisit the plan when a specialist changes a critical assumption.

## Communicate uncertainty

Label supplied requirements, assumed conditions, calculated estimates and design
judgments separately. For each risk say what is unknown, its design implication,
the evidence or specialist required, and when it must be resolved. Do not claim
structural, environmental or regulatory compliance from a concept sketch.
