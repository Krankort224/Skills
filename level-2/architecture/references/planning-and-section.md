# Planning and section

Use this knowledge to generate a spatial solution from the
[program and relationships](program-and-space.md), or to repair a selected layout.
The schemes are conditional design tools, not prescribed building types.
[Sources](sources.md) records the upstream basis and exclusions. All worked
dimensions, furniture and heights below are hypothetical design assumptions,
not normative minima, engineering sizes or evidence of local compliance.

## Construct a room that can be used

Area is a consequence of width, depth, access and the activity arrangement.
Generate at least one furniture/action setting before fixing the room area. A
long narrow room and a compact room of equal area offer different useful space:
the narrow room may support a linear bench or exhibition but fail face-to-face
teaching; the compact room may support a group but waste a linear process route.

Start with the activity's demanding dimension: a bed plus access on its used
sides, a meeting table plus occupied chairs and passage, an equipment line plus
operating/maintenance access, or a display wall plus viewing distance. Place the
door where the approach can reach the settings without crossing the main task.
Keep the door swing, handles/approach and adjacent furniture visible. In a room
with several doors, draw the connecting route before allocating the remaining
floor to the principal activity. A route through the middle can divide a large
room into two unusable fragments.

Treat the exterior edge as a limited resource. Usable frontage is the facade
length that can serve occupied rooms after cores, blank walls, fixed openings,
structure, privacy constraints and required separation have been considered.
If three occupied rooms all need facade openings, their widths and intervening
partitions must fit that actual frontage, not just the unit's total area. A deep
remainder can accommodate compatible storage, bathrooms or approaches but cannot
substitute for an occupied room's requested outlook. A corner offers different
orientations and additional frontage, yet a deep corner room may still have a
poorly lit rear. Dual aspect means openings on different sides; an internal
partition, external obstruction or unopenable window may defeat the intended
light or ventilation benefit.

| Geometry or activity | Generate the room this way | Watch for this failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Teaching or group discussion | Choose the table/seat pattern and display wall together; keep an instructor route and alternate seating positions. Test the most oblique and furthest seats. | Equal area with a shallow wide shape can give poor end-seat views; excessive depth can put rear seats behind heads or columns. Rotating the display may work better than adding area. |
| Quiet work or reading | Put sustained tasks near suitable exterior exposure, locate entry and shared movement at an edge, and distribute local storage away from the entrance sightline. | A central aisle can turn a quiet room into the route to every other destination; full-height storage across the window wall destroys the intended frontage. |
| Living/sleeping | Establish usable furnishing walls and privacy at the door before dividing the remaining area. Place service rooms so they do not leave a narrow tunnel to the main room. | A door at the center of each wall can leave no continuous wall for a bed, sofa or storage; excess corridor consumes usable width without improving privacy. |
| Workshop or processing | Arrange receiving, working, intermediate holding and dispatch in process order; provide a path for the actual object and its handler. Reserve maintenance/replacement approaches. | A machine fits its footprint but its operating envelope crosses a door or a load cannot turn through the approach. Shift the equipment or transfer point, rather than reducing its operating envelope. |
| Future split or combination | Test two furnished rooms with independent doors, environmental exposure and support, then the combined setting with the partition removed. | A removable partition may terminate at a fixed window, leave one room without a door, or create a room wholly behind another. Flexibility belongs to the entire interface. |

For equipment and large objects distinguish three spatial claims: the **solid
footprint of a parked object**, its **active working envelope**, and the
**continuous transit/turning envelope** of an incoming object plus its handler.
Inactive work does not make a parked machine, bicycle or stored trolley
disappear. A transit envelope may share an inactive working area only if it
avoids the solid object, and its operating sequence is credible. Test its
intersection with every neighboring position, not just the receiving one.
If moving a second object is required, reserve an actual temporary holding place
and trace that move too; otherwise relocate/orient the positions, change the
approach or state the restricted operating condition. A clear square drawn
over two furnished bays is not evidence that a large object can turn there.

A useful early output is a room envelope with named edges: occupied frontage,
service/back edge, entry edge and potential joining edge. It gives the subsequent
plan an intelligible assembly rule. Keep interior clear dimensions separate from
reservation dimensions containing walls; revise the schedule when walls, columns
and doors are resolved.

### Construct a clear-area fit with explicit wall reservations

When the brief gives finished room areas, build the dimensions outward from
those clear areas instead of filling the whole plate with target-area blocks.
Choose the access family first. For a single room band in an inside-face shell
of width W and depth D, a trial clear common passage c and enclosing partition
t leave room clear depth d = D−c−t. A rectangular room of clear target A needs
at least width A/d, provided its activity can use that proportion. Round the
trial width upward where useful, then add intervening partitions once. Check
the sum against actual frontage and place each door on the common passage.
All assumed construction/route dimensions remain study inputs to verify, never
code minima or selected systems by implication.

For a hypothetical 14 × 9 m finished-face shell, try c=2.00 m and t=0.15 m:
the rooms are 6.85 m clear deep. Clear targets 36 and 24 m² can be trialled as
5.40 × 6.85 = 36.99 m² and 3.60 × 6.85 = 24.66 m². Place the passage at y=0…2,
cross-wall reservation at y=2…2.15, first room at x=0…5.4,y=2.15…9, divider at
x=5.4…5.55, and second room at x=5.55…9.15. Close its east edge with a partition
at x=9.15…9.30,y=2.15…9, reserving 1.0275 m² outside that clear room. The
remaining x=9.30…14 band is a separate 32.195 m² reservation, not secretly
additional room area. The ledger closes: 28 passage + 2.10 cross-wall +
36.99 room + 1.0275 divider + 24.66 room + 1.0275 closing wall +
32.195 remainder = 126 m². Door openings belong to the cross-wall reservation
in this allocation. Furniture, openings, fixed columns and approach still need
their actual checks, but wall thickness no longer prevents proposing a concrete
clear-area arrangement.

If the width sum fails, change the family, proportions or permitted neighbor
boundary and repeat; an L-shaped room may use otherwise stranded floor but must
still fit the activity. If one layout meets target areas only before walls, it
is not a completed response to a clear-area brief. Develop one trial with
declared build-ups or demonstrate the fixed incompatibility. Keep an alternative
on the same clear/reservation basis so its apparent advantage is not omitted
construction.

## Choose a plan family from the relationships

Generate families by changing how movement meets occupied rooms, not merely by
changing the outline. Sketch a section alongside each family: the same floor
diagram can imply an open gallery, an enclosed corridor or an atrium, with very
different privacy, weather and vertical-space consequences.

| Family | Constructive rule and good use | Benefits and characteristic failures |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single-loaded spine or gallery | Place one band of rooms along one side of a route; let rooms extend toward the opposite facade where through aspect is wanted. Put the route on the less private or less favorable exposure only after testing the site. | The route can be legible and externally exposed, and through rooms can gain more than one aspect. It consumes more route per served room; a gallery can overlook bedrooms, shade openings or be uncomfortable in weather. Use screened thresholds and place less private spaces at the access edge; test whether screening sacrifices the desired light. |
| Double-loaded spine | Put two room bands around one shared route. Derive total depth as first room band + route + second room band, including relevant partitions; do not choose room depths from the leftover plate alone. | It concentrates doors and movement and works with repeated cells. Internal routes can be dull, single-aspect cells can become too deep, and opposite doors concentrate noise and views. Break the repetition with recognizable landings/cross-views or shared nodes, while retaining real route continuity. |
| Branching spine and clusters | Connect several small neighborhoods to a clear main route; give each cluster a shared resource or supervised center and avoid using its occupied center as through circulation. | Strong local identity and short internal links suit teams or teaching groups. Duplicated support, blind branch ends and bottlenecks at the main spine are common costs. Give cluster entries visible landmarks and test the peak where branches merge. |
| Court or ring | Wrap occupied wings and a route around an outdoor court or an explicitly enclosed atrium. Choose whether movement is on the inner or outer edge and place major connections at clear nodes. | More perimeter and a common orienting space can improve access to outdoors. Narrow courts can become overlooked or shaded; the ring adds route length and makes all corners look alike. Test opposing windows, court height/width, entry recognition and shortcuts. An enclosed atrium consumes height and needs specialist environmental/fire coordination. |
| Open hall with service edge or islands | Use a continuous activity field with defined arrival and through paths; concentrate fixed support at an edge or islands whose backs can hold services/storage. Establish quieter subsettings at edges or in enclosures. | It permits changing furniture and views across shared work, display or assembly. Speech, glare and movement spread; service islands can create blind pockets; later enclosures can cut off light or access. Test one furnished normal mode, one peak mode and an actual future subdivision. |
| Rooms in sequence or enfilade | Connect rooms directly in an intentional order, with a parallel bypass if independent occupation or servicing requires it. | It makes a strong process or visitor narrative and can reduce separate corridors. A closed intermediate room otherwise blocks everything beyond; passage compromises privacy and concentration. Use only where sequential access is compatible with the operation. |

For a shallow plate with precious occupied frontage, compare a single-loaded band
with a compact cluster before forcing two insufficient room bands into it. For a
deep plate, compare a double-loaded scheme with a court or split wings: gaining
frontage sacrifices floor area and adds envelope, while keeping the deep plate
requires a credible use for its interior. For repeated cells, vary width and mix
where the facade allows it; a regular external module does not require identical
rooms behind every bay. For public/quiet mixtures, place shared social nodes at
arrival or between branches so that going to them does not cross quiet settings.

Make the test geometrical: locate entrances, cores, fixed structure and external
openings; draw room envelopes at a common scale; connect the doors with a real
route; then identify occupied frontage and residual strips. Compare route length
along the proposed path, not a centroid distance through walls. A family that
fits every area but needs people to cross a locked room has failed its access
logic.

When two developed alternatives are requested, give each enough spatial evidence
to test the same decisive conditions: room/program ownership, actual access from
the core or entrance, the principal service route and the critical section.
Exchanging north/south labels without refitting the room and approach is an
idea to investigate, not a comparable developed option. A rejected alternative
can remain useful if its tested conflict and the architectural change needed
to repair it are explicit. Spend detail on the discriminating interface rather
than repeating every unchanged material or legal limitation.

## Organize levels, cores and service depth

Start section with the ground relation: street and service access, finished
entrance level, outdoor thresholds, and the level of each principal destination.
Use a common datum and explicit units. A site slope can produce split levels,
but the direct stair between them does not establish a step-free journey or
independent service access. Draw the alternative route and its floor footprint
before accepting the split. Show which public destinations remain usable with
other floors closed.

Stack activities by dependencies. Frequent public arrival and bulky servicing
often favor a direct ground connection; quiet/private rooms may benefit from
separation above or beside them. Neither is an automatic floor assignment. A
noisy event room below sleeping or quiet study can transmit sound through floors,
structure and services even when the entrances are separate. Prefer compatible
vertical neighbors, relocate a sensitive setting or reserve a real isolation
strategy for specialist coordination. A service floor or storage buffer also
costs height and area; label that cost.

Read the core as a set of vertical and horizontal interfaces: stair/lift arrival,
shared lobby, controlled doors, service risers, maintenance access and eventual
discharge to outdoors. Reserve these independently where their boundaries differ.
Align the core through levels; check that an upper landing does not arrive inside
a private room and that ground-floor commercial space does not block discharge.
The number, size, protection and separation of stairs/lifts remain project and
applicability questions, not deductions from these generic diagrams.

- A central core can give shorter routes in several directions and free much of
  the facade, but its surrounding ring may be awkward to subdivide without
  circulation through another tenancy.
- A side or end core can leave a continuous activity field and can serve a blank
  or constrained edge, but its approach may lengthen routes or consume useful
  frontage. Test the far side, including an independent future tenant.
- Distributed cores can give several addresses and local access on a long plate;
  they also duplicate area and interfaces. An external core frees interior area
  only if its connections, landings, weather exposure and base actually work.

Draw at least one section through the most demanding service crossing and one
through the principal occupied edge. Read floor-to-floor height as an allocation,
not a room height: finished floor build-up, clear occupied zone, ceiling/service
zone and structure must reconcile. If structural and service depths overlap,
show the coordinated overlapping zones rather than subtracting both full depths
or assuming services can pass through every beam. At roof level include slopes,
roof structure, rooflights and the route to roof plant where relevant.

Use **shallow occupied-edge zones** where the main rooms benefit from uninterrupted
height; concentrate **deeper service bands** over circulation, wet/support rooms
or another compatible band where distribution and maintenance are possible.
This can preserve height without changing a fixed floor level. It fails if the
route from riser to room crosses an unplanned beam, if low ceilings spread into
the activity field, or if maintenance requires dismantling a private setting.
An exposed-services ceiling is a design choice with acoustic, visual and access
consequences; it does not remove the physical service depth.

### Reconcile upper floors, tall rooms and roofs

Overlay the proposed floor footprints on the same plan coordinates, then test
each overlap in section. A tall lower room and an upper occupied floor cannot
both claim the same volume. Mark the actual floor edge or void; subtract that
void from the upper floor ledger and relocate its displaced program. Conversely,
an upper floor entirely above a room prevents calling that room an independently
roofed high volume unless the supplied levels and depths actually permit it.
Different elevations or attractive stepped roofs do not resolve the overlap.

In a hypothetical 18 × 12 m plate, a hall occupies x=0…6, y=0…8 and requests
5.8 m clear from its finished floor. An upper finished floor at +3.6 m cannot
remain over that 48 m² hall: its structure lies below that level. Removing that
upper polygon leaves 216−48=168 m² of upper floor before other reservations.
Compare moving the upper program to those remaining bands, moving the hall to
an uncovered end, reducing its height only if the brief permits, or changing
levels only if authorized. Test support at the new floor/void edge and routes
around it. An upper plan that merely has enough total area has not resolved this
section conflict.

At every roof step distinguish occupied upper floor, unoccupied lower roof,
open terrace and internal void. Give each its own footprint and level. Test the
upper facade/threshold, drainage and maintenance at the lower roof; a terrace
needs more than a floor-area label. State whether the result is a tested block
section or still a height hypothesis, using only the supplied or explicitly
assumed datums and build-up reservations.

For future division, start at a shared landing and trace an independent door,
route, occupied frontage and support for each proposed part. Locate wet/service
interfaces so a future boundary does not cut off one part's supply or require
access through the other for maintenance. Align new partitions with suitable
facade and structural interfaces. For mixed use, test separately the access hours,
waste/delivery and maintenance of each use. For phasing, each occupied phase
needs a functioning entrance, route, support and safe separation from the next
phase; an unfinished corridor is not future flexibility.

### Resolve a Russian mixed-use plan before fixing the core

For a confirmed Russian project, use the [entrance, route and mixed-use
selection](normative/ru/applicability.md#resolve-entrances-routes-and-mixed-uses)
with the actual host and embedded activities. Keep three inputs separate:
program use and operating hours; functional fire-hazard class for each relevant
building/part/premise; and, where production/storage technology triggers it,
explosion/fire-hazard category. A public showroom in a production host does not
acquire the workshop's category simply by sharing its address. Conversely,
calling a workshop a “studio” does not establish a low-hazard process. Obtain
materials, quantities, process and storage information before freezing its
neighbors or access. The [123-ФЗ](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#federal-law-123-fz)
and [SP 12](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#sp-12-13130-2009) records identify
the distinct source questions; unread classification provisions remain open.

| Plan decision | Construct and compare | Consequence that can change the plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Public use inside a residential host | Draw the resident entrance/core/discharge and the embedded use's visitor, staff, delivery and waste paths on the same base | A café queue or goods holding at the resident core consumes a shared interface. Compare a direct public entrance and a separate service approach; retain the host's actual route while separation and opening requirements are checked |
| Visitor room beside production/storage | Place the public waiting/display setting at arrival, with a controlled boundary to work and a delivery path to the process side | If process/category evidence changes the required separation or permitted connection, move the boundary, opening or public setting before adjusting finishes. A shared reception is a tested operational choice, not proof that the uses may share evacuation |
| Destination above or below arrival | Compare a common step-free approach and vertical connection with moving the heavily used public destination to arrival level | The lift landing, waiting and door approaches take floor area. Moving the destination may free upper circulation or make independent opening hours possible; it does not settle stair, exit or lift fire requirements |
| Future tenancy or phased operation | Trace each part from its own permissible entrance through occupied rooms, support and eventual exit discharge | A future boundary through the only lobby, lift landing or service approach defeats the split. Move the boundary or retain shared circulation with an actual access arrangement |

First reserve entrances and level connections for ordinary use; then trace the
actual evacuation proposal, its doors, stairs, lobbies and outdoor discharge.
Keep these overlays distinct even where they share geometry. Use
[SP 59](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#sp-59-13330-2020) and
[181-ФЗ](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#federal-law-181-fz) for the selected
accessibility question, and [SP 1](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#sp-1-13130-2020)
with [SP 4](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#sp-4-13130-2013) for affected
escape/spread questions. A door usable in ordinary operation still needs its
own evacuation classification; an ordinary lift is not established as an escape
route by drawing a step-free journey through it. Keep any waiting/rescue
reservation beside, rather than silently within, the clear passage until its
actual strategy is established. This sequence generates the core footprint
before room areas make it impossible to change.

## Worked design progressions

These are explanatory block studies, not complete proposals. The arithmetic
checks floor ownership; furniture checks selected settings; plan/section checks
expose consequential revisions. Each example retains explicit unresolved
interfaces instead of converting a plausible block arrangement into approval.

### A public learning house with a quiet upper floor

**Brief and fixed geometry.** Assume two rectangular floors, each 24 × 16 m
measured inside the exterior walls, with x increasing east and y north. The
public entrance is on the southeast edge and a goods entrance on the northeast.
The west 6 × 16 m band is reserved on both floors for vertical circulation,
services, toilets and support. Its internal layout is unresolved. Floor-to-floor
height is provisionally 4.0 m. The activities are a 24-person class/event setting,
light making, public arrival and 24 quiet reading seats above. The activity
capacity does not establish a statutory occupancy.

**First scheme.** Use a double-loaded spine in the remaining 18 × 16 m block.
The spine occupies x=6…24, y=7…9. Each room band is 7 m deep. Divide each band at
x=18, leaving a 12 × 7 m west room and a 6 × 7 m east room.

| Ground-floor reservation | Coordinates | Area |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| Core/service/support band | x=0…6, y=0…16 | 96 m² |
| Shared spine, including its boundary allocation | x=6…24, y=7…9 | 36 m² |
| Event/teaching room | x=6…18, y=0…7 | 84 m² |
| Arrival/reception/waiting | x=18…24, y=0…7 | 42 m² |
| Light making | x=6…18, y=9…16 | 84 m² |
| Materials preparation/holding | x=18…24, y=9…16 | 42 m² |
| **Total** | **24 × 16** | **384 m²** |

The reservations own their boundary construction within the listed area; they
are not room net areas. Actual partitions/columns must reduce the interiors and
the 2 m spine reservation must yield a separately verified clear route. Place
the desk near the arrival/spine junction to see entry and the onward choice;
put waiting off the spine. Goods enter the preparation block and transfer directly
west to making, avoiding the public arrival. Keep wet support at the west band
where compatible. Door positions must preserve these intended connections.

**Fit discovery.** A 12 × 7 m teaching reservation is provisionally tested as an
11.6 × 6.6 m clear rectangle after assumed enclosing construction. The six-table
setting in [program demand](program-and-space.md#derive-a-provisional-capacity-and-room-envelope)
needs 10.5 × 6.5 m. It fits that assumed empty rectangle with little depth margin;
an inward door, a column, a wider required route or different furniture could
invalidate it. Place the entrance into the side circulation strip rather than
the presentation strip, and test the end seats' display view. The remainder is
not a growth allowance. Making capacity stays equipment-dependent until its
actual action envelopes and replacement route are known.

**Section discovery and revision.** The first upper-floor idea placed one quiet
reading room across the full south band (126 m²), directly over the event room.
That exposes most quiet seats to event sound. Preserve footprint, west core,
spine, facade and ground-floor activity positions; relocate quiet reading to the
two east 6 × 7 m blocks, over arrival and preparation. Allocate twelve seats per
room, using three four-person tables with a hypothetical 3 × 2 m occupied
envelope arranged end to end: the 3 × 6 m field leaves a side approach within
the assumed 5.6 × 6.6 m clear room. That remains a provisional furniture fit;
door approaches and suitable varied seating need a real layout. Use the two
west 12 × 7 m blocks upstairs for collaborative/group work. The upper ledger is
96 core/support + 36 spine + 84 quiet + 168 group work = 384 m².

Quiet area has decreased from 126 to 84 m² while the requested 24 seats remain
plausible under the stated table assumption. The change improves compatible
stacking; it does not calculate acoustic isolation. Preparation below quiet
must be quiet handling rather than machines, and the adjacent group rooms still
need appropriate boundaries. If making includes vibration or hazardous processes,
revisit the program and specialist separation rather than accepting the block.

**Level check.** Suppose the study allocates 0.4 m to floor/structure and 0.6 m
to an additional nonoverlapping ceiling/service zone: 4.0 − 0.4 − 0.6 = 3.0 m
clear. This reconciles a hypothetical 3.0 m room-height objective only until
structure, floor finishes and service crossings are coordinated. Trace the west
core landing into the spine on both floors and its ground exit independently of
event bookings. After-hours use needs a defined path from entrance to event room,
support and exit without opening upstairs; this may require a controlled spine
boundary or revised ground support doors. Unknown core fit and access/fire
requirements remain material limits despite the closed area ledger.

### A fixed shell: housing above quiet work, revised by frontage and section

**Brief and fixed geometry.** Assume a two-floor shell with 30 × 18 m internal
plates and north/south facades available for occupied rooms. Fixed end bands
x=0…5 and x=25…30 each reserve 90 m² for cores/support, with riser interfaces at
their inner edges. Ground uses are quiet design studios and public consultation;
upper use initially requests four identical two-bedroom homes. A 20 × 2 m central
route reservation runs x=5…25, y=8…10. The two occupied bands are each 20 × 8 m.
Keep the shell, floor levels, end cores and structural supports fixed. Facade
partition landing positions must be verified; the following widths assume that
appropriate openings and blank interfaces can be provided.

**First fit and failure.** Dividing both occupied bands into two 10 × 8 m blocks
gives four 80 m² reservations. The upper ledger closes: 180 core/support + 40
route + 320 dwelling reservations = 540 m². But each dwelling requests a 4.2 m
clear living frontage and two 3.2 m clear bedroom frontages. Even a hypothetical
0.4 m total for the separating interfaces gives 4.2 + 3.2 + 3.2 + 0.4 = 11.0 m,
more than its 10 m frontage. Putting a bedroom in the deep rear would violate
the stated exterior-exposure objective. The 80 m² total therefore conceals a
specific frontage failure; drawing smaller beds does not repair that requirement.

**Bounded mix revision.** If the operator allows the mix to change, place an
11.2 × 8 m two-bedroom reservation and an 8.8 × 8 m one-bedroom reservation in
each band. This yields two 89.6 m² and two 70.4 m² reservations: still 320 m²,
still four independent entrances, but six bedrooms rather than eight. The
family frontage now accommodates the 11.0 m trial lineup; the smaller home tests
a living room and one bedroom across its frontage. The capacity loss is an
explicit brief change, requiring confirmation; it is not an equivalent solution
to the original four two-bedroom requirement. If the mix is fixed, the shell and
frontage requirements conflict and that option remains rejected.

Place the occupied rooms in a trial 4.4 m facade band and entrances, storage,
bathrooms and kitchen/service interfaces in the remaining 3.6 m rear band. Keep
kitchens connected to living rather than a route through a bedroom. Test a
3.2 × 4.0 m clear bedroom with the actual intended bed, storage and approaches;
a hypothetical 1.6 × 2.0 m bed and 0.6 m deep wardrobe occupy only their own
footprints, not their use envelopes. Locate wardrobe doors and the room door so
the remaining approach is continuous. Check each room against window positions
and fixed columns before accepting the lineup. Single-aspect accommodation
remains a design/performance question; no daylight threshold follows from the
4.4 m depth.

**Section failure and repair.** Assume fixed floor-to-floor height of 3.3 m,
0.35 m floor/structural allocation and initially 0.45 m additional ceiling/service
depth. Clear height would be 2.50 m, below the operator's hypothetical 2.70 m
occupied-room objective. Trial a shallow 0.20 m service zone at the occupied edge
and a deeper 0.65 m band over compatible rear support: 2.75 m clear at the front,
2.30 m at the rear. This is a possible distribution concept, not engineered
sizing or a claim that 2.30 m suits every support use. The operator and applicable
requirements must permit those rear heights, and engineering must establish
distribution through the fixed supports. If a required duct must cross the
occupied edge at full depth, the proposed repair fails and the fixed height
conflict remains. Do not silently borrow height from the floor above.

**Independent use and later division.** Give the ground consultation setting a
public entry and waiting pocket at the east, with a controlled threshold to the
studios. Retain a resident entry at the west and trace each upper doorway from
the shared route; do not treat different addresses as proof that escape, lift or
support provision is adequate. Keep rear service access and maintenance outside
private bedrooms. Show actual connections from each dwelling's wet zone to the
fixed riser interfaces; distant rooms may need an additional reservation or a
different assignment. Stack quiet desk work below bedrooms and put public
meetings below living/support where possible. Machinery or evening amplified
events would change the vertical compatibility and cannot be accepted as the
same ground-floor program.

Test a future north-band tenant split from the shared route with independent
doors, support and facade exposure, rather than assuming an open studio is
divisible. The selected revision changes only permitted partitions and use mix;
it leaves facade feasibility, clear route/core geometry, detailed furniture,
services, acoustic separation and jurisdiction requirements to be resolved.

### A Russian production host with a public consultation room

**Hypothetical starting plan.** Assume a confirmed Russian new low-rise
production building with an 18 × 12 m ground plate, no basement and a public
showroom in the west 6 × 12 m band. A 6 × 6 m consultation room is initially
placed on a mezzanine at +3.60 m. Its daily approach uses a lift reached through
the workshop; the proposed emergency diagram repeats that lift arrow. Goods
arrive at the east edge. These dimensions and levels are project assumptions,
not requirements, and no process/category, lift or escape provision is established.

**Conflict and bounded choice.** The public journey crosses the working process,
and its vertical alternative has no established fire strategy. Retaining the
mezzanine would require a resolved public approach, vertical connection and
emergency arrangement, each taking real floor/section space. If the operator
permits reduced display area, compare moving consultation to grade: divide the
west band into two 6 × 6 m reservations, showroom to the south and consultation
to the north, each with a direct door onto a common west arrival path. Keep the
east delivery approach and a controlled staff/process connection at the public
boundary. The public allocation becomes 36 + 36 = 72 m² on grade; display loses
36 m² and public use leaves the mezzanine. Walls, furniture and door approaches
must be fitted within those reservations before they become clear room areas.

**Russian source selection and result.** The
[SP 59 record's checked scope](normative/ru/fire-and-accessibility.md#sp-59-13330-2020)
identifies public buildings open to the population; its private single-family
house exclusion cannot be used to omit this public-use question. This verifies
a source-selection trigger, not an entrance dimension or a mezzanine prohibition.
The two grade doors and common arrival path are a design response that removes
the workshop crossing and the visitors' dependence on the lift. Their actual
accessibility and evacuation status remain unresolved until selected primary
provisions, the project-date basis and the actual use/fire classification are
established under the [Russian procedure](normative/ru/applicability.md#resolve-entrances-routes-and-mixed-uses).
Do not assign the public rooms the process hazard category or declare the
workshop safe from its remaining area. If the technology requires a different
boundary, opening or relationship under the selected fire/spread provisions,
refit that west edge; if display capacity is fixed, this reduced-display option

## Repair a bounded proposal without losing its logic

When a new room or increased capacity is requested, make a small before/after
ledger and plan/section overlay of the affected zone. Preserve fixed coordinates
and name any interface whose consequences cross that zone. A larger meeting room
may take frontage from a focus room, displace storage into a route or require a
deeper service crossing; its area increment is only one consequence.

Choose repairs by cause. Insufficient room width calls for a different furniture
setting, different proportion, reduced capacity or a changed neighbor boundary.
Insufficient frontage calls for mix changes, corner use, a different access
family or an explicitly enlarged boundary. A route crossing an activity calls
for a bypass, edge route or changed access sequence. A height failure calls for
compatible service zoning, changed activity or changed levels where authorized.
A fixed column through the required setting calls for another setting or room
location, not an assumed column removal. Show which repair resolves which
failure and which accepted quality it costs.

Complete the permitted geometric repair before handing it off as solved. If
two required clear rooms exactly fill their zoning blocks while new partition
thickness is unknown, choose an explicitly labeled study build-up and take its
reservation from a permitted residual zone; recalculate finished faces, doors
and remaining passage. Test another plausible build-up if its uncertainty could
reverse the result. Do not call the original block areas finished room areas.
If no allowed boundary can supply the missing space, show that incompatibility
instead. Construction/performance confirmation can remain open while a concrete
architectural trial is developed.

Recheck the changed room, its immediate neighbors, the affected route to arrival
and outdoors, the floor ledger and the vertical neighbors/service interfaces.
Use the same normal/peak scenario and area definition in each option. State
whether the result is a furnished fit, a block fit with reservations, or only a
relationship concept. Preserve unresolved hard constraints visibly; a preferred
composition or efficiency score cannot make them disappear.
