# Sloping library

Start from the brief, [actual demand](../../references/program/demand.md) and accepted decisions. Choose and compare architectural consequences rather than inferring a style or regulation from a name; select [requirements](../../references/requirements.md) where applicability matters.

The following are hypothetical design studies. Their dimensions are selected to
make the geometric reasoning inspectable; they are **not legal minima, standard
room sizes, engineering sizes or performance predictions**. The area boundary is
geometric plan area; usable and legal areas would need separately defined
measurement. No project should inherit these numbers as defaults.

## A sloping library: shared reading, quiet work and an ordered facade

**Problem.** Suppose a narrow site falls from an upper street to a lower garden.
The brief wants a shared reading room, small quiet rooms, a legible principal
entrance and delivery access from below. A stepped sequence may follow the
landform, but frequent trips between shared and quiet spaces make a staircase-only
sequence unsuitable. Surveyed levels and program areas are not yet known. For
the section test only, take the upper-street arrival as level 0, the lower garden as −3 m and
local ground against the uphill boundary as +1.2 m; these are invented inputs.

**Hypothesis.** Keep entrance, shared reading and the main quiet-room route on
one common public level. Use a taller reading volume beside a lower band of
small rooms; let a lower service level meet the garden-side service approach.
Compare this with several half-level public rooms: the latter may follow ground
more closely but adds a level transition to every cross-room journey.

**Arrangement and test.** In a provisional transverse section reserve, from
street to garden, a quiet-room band, a continuous route and the taller reading
room. Trace upper-street threshold → orientation point → quiet room or reading
room without a public level change. Then draw ground against that section. A
provisional quiet-room sill at +0.9 m is 0.3 m below the assumed adjacent ground at +1.2 m: that portion of
the opening faces soil, and the plan's claim to unobstructed direct outlook
fails unless the ground/enclosure relationship changes. Moving the whole plate
down would also break the upper-street entry relationship. Service delivery below also needs a real
connection to book/storage demand above; note that vertical movement as an
operating interface rather than assume it is effortless.

**Revision.** Put storage, enclosed support and vertical service connection on
the uphill side; move quiet rooms to a lateral exposed edge and retain the shared
reading room toward the garden. The roof above the reading space rises beyond
the support band, allowing a potential high opening; its orientation and glare
consequences need assessment. If the lateral frontage cannot accommodate the
quiet-room demand, compare a court cut into the uphill mass with an additional
public level. The court removes floor area and adds enclosure/ground interfaces;
the extra level adds vertical journeys. State which brief priority governs that
trade-off before choosing.

For the lateral elevation, suppose four equal 4 m room bays occupy a 16 m
frontage and a candidate panel module is 1 m. Four panels per bay fit that
nominal order. Centering a nominal 1.6 m opening in each bay, however, puts jambs
1.2 m and 2.8 m from its start, off that panel grid. This is a geometric mismatch,
not a defect proved by a style rule. Compare special jamb panels, a smaller
subdivision or a revised opening. If a 2 m centered opening is explored, nominal
jambs fall on the 1 m lines, but the view, desk layout, glazing performance and
support zones must still work. Actual joints, tolerances and wall thicknesses
are unresolved; this arithmetic is not a fabrication schedule.

Let the taller reading room have a larger grouped opening and the entry a deep
recess within the same datum order. This differentiates the shared destination
from quiet bays while maintaining a related base, opening heads and joint
vocabulary. At the corner choose a solid end pier or a separate recessed joint
instead of forcing the module into a narrow sliver. Describe a unit or panel
cladding expression separately from the actual enclosing layers; visible
solidity does not imply a bearing masonry wall.

**Remaining inputs.** Survey and ground/water evidence, actual frontage and
room demand, inclusive route levels, delivery method and operating capacity,
reading light/glare, quiet-room acoustic needs, support/roof/service reservations,
product modules and maintenance access. If any invalidates the common public
level or lateral room band, return to the section hypothesis and recount area;
do not repair the drawing with cosmetic facade offsets.
