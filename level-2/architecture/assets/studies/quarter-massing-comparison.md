# Quarter massing at equal floor area and footprint

Compare three housing groups on a hypothetical 240 × 180 m site. Both layouts
must provide 40,896 m² of housing floor area on the same study basis, alongside
a separate 3,600 m² education building. The comparison isolates what changes
when the same built area is arranged around different outdoor spaces.

## Shared geometry and area basis

Coordinates are in metres on a level study plane. The southwest corner is
(0, 0), x increases east and y increases north. In the coordinate tables,
a pair of intervals defines one rectangle. Assume streets beyond the west,
south and east boundaries and a public park beyond the north boundary.
Locations of actual crossings, entrances and access rights remain open.

The public cross consists of the east–west strip x 0–240, y 80–100 and the
north–south strip x 112–128, y 0–180. A 32 × 32 m forecourt at x 104–136,
y 74–106 widens their intersection. The strips cover
`240 × 20 + 16 × 180 − 16 × 20 = 7,360 m²`. The forecourt adds four
6 × 8 m corner pieces outside the cross, bringing the public reservation to
7,552 m². It reserves space for public movement and staying.

The four study plots combine the x ranges 12–104 and 136–228 with the y
ranges 12–74 and 106–168. Each plot measures 92 × 62 m, or 5,704 m². Housing
occupies the southwest (SW), southeast (SE) and northwest (NW) plots.
Education occupies the northeast plot. Its 60 × 30 m footprint at x 144–204,
y 138–168 repeats on two storeys and leaves 3,904 m² of unbuilt plot. This is
a reservation to fit outdoor activity, access and support. A western public
approach from the spine and an eastern service approach are hypotheses to
trace through the intermediate ground and their separate arrival spaces.

For this study, floor area is the sum of geometric plan plates to the outside
of the proposed mass on each storey. Each storey repeats its stated footprint
without voids or setbacks. An actual ground or upper-floor exception requires
counting its own plate. Dwelling yield, educational capacity and formal area
definitions require separate inputs.

## A. Three corner buildings

Each L consists of two rectangles that share an edge. Their total is
`92 × 12 + 50 × 12 = 1,704 m²`. If drawn as 92 × 12 m and 62 × 12 m
rectangles instead, subtract their shared 12 × 12 m corner once per floor.

| Plot | Long wing (x, y), m | Return wing (x, y), m | Storeys | Housing floor area, m² |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| SW | 12–104, 12–24 | 12–24, 24–74 | 9 | 15,336 |
| SE | 136–228, 12–24 | 216–228, 24–74 | 9 | 15,336 |
| NW | 12–104, 156–168 | 12–24, 106–156 | 6 | 10,224 |
| **Total** | | | | **40,896** |

The 5,112 m² combined housing footprint leaves an 80 × 50 m outdoor field
in each housing plot. Each L frames two sides. Resident control depends on
how the open sides are bounded and where doors and service approaches land.
Trace a resident journey from the public side to the entrance and the court,
and test which approaches remain open when the resident boundary closes.

Assume 3 m per storey. The two southern groups reach a nominal 27 m above the
study plane and the NW group reaches 18 m, before roof or base allowances.
The northern building presents a lower edge toward the park. A section normal
to a southern long wing pairs a 27 m mass with a 50 m outdoor depth. This
describes enclosure geometry. Assess direct sun at actual receivers with
[the multi-building sun method](../../references/performance/comfort/multi-building-sun.md)
if use of the space depends on it.

## B. Six straight buildings

Each housing plot now has two 71 × 12 m bars with eight storeys each.
The housing footprint is again `6 × 71 × 12 = 5,112 m²` and the floor
area is `5,112 × 8 = 40,896 m²`. Nominal height is 24 m above the study plane.

| Plot | x for both bars, m | y for first bar, m | y for second bar, m |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| SW | 24–95 | 12–24 | 62–74 |
| SE | 145–216 | 12–24 | 62–74 |
| NW | 24–95 | 106–118 | 156–168 |

Each plot still has 4,000 m² of unbuilt ground. Its main outdoor field between
the bars is now `71 × 38 = 2,698 m²`, leaving 1,302 m² beside or beyond
the bars. A offers more continuity along the outer street edges and two
occupied sides to each court. B opens those edges, offers separate building
addresses and creates more building ends. The preferred layout depends on
whether the brief gives priority to enclosure or transverse openness. Both
need actual entrances, servicing, privacy boundaries and outdoor activity fit
before the outdoor reservations can be credited as usable gardens.

## Reconcile the ground and test two changes

The two baseline layouts have the same ground ledger. The school footprint is
included in building footprints. Unbuilt plot areas and the public reservation
are each counted once. Service, planting and surfaces may subdivide these
reservations once their geometry is established.

| Nonoverlapping ground allocation | A or B, m² | A with SW shifted north, m² |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Housing and education footprints | 6,912 | 6,912 |
| Unbuilt ground within the three original housing plots | 12,000 | 12,144 |
| Unbuilt ground within the education plot | 3,904 | 3,904 |
| Unbuilt public cross and forecourt | 7,552 | 7,480 |
| Unallocated perimeter and intermediate ground | 12,832 | 12,760 |
| **Total study ground** | **43,200** | **43,200** |

Shift the SW building in A by 12 m north while keeping the original plot and
public boundaries fixed. Its return wing now occupies x 12–24, y 36–86. Of
the 144 m² that moves outside the housing plot, 72 m² occupies the intermediate
strip at y 74–80 and 72 m² occupies the public cross at y 80–86. The footprint
and floor area remain equal to the baseline. The unbuilt part of the public
strip narrows from 20 m to 14 m at the affected wing. This fails the stipulated
continuous 20 m public reservation even though passage may remain possible.
The additional 144 m² of unbuilt ground inside the original housing plot also
needs a revised boundary and access test before any use is credited. Restore
the original position or revise the public space and its connections explicitly.

As a separate change to baseline A, raise NW from six to nine storeys.
This adds `3 × 1,704 = 5,112 m²` of housing floor area. Lowering either
southern building from nine to six storeys removes exactly that amount.
The nominal park-side edge rises from 18 m to 27 m and one street-side
edge drops from 27 m to 18 m. Recheck the affected sections, approach views,
receivers and housing arrangements before preferring that exchange.
