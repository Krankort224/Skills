## Identity and evidence

**Reported facts.** Villa Savoye and its gardener's lodge are at Poissy,
France, dated 1928–1931 by the
[Fondation Le Corbusier](https://www.fondationlecorbusier.fr/oeuvre-architecture/realisations-villa-savoye-et-loge-du-jardinier-poissy-france-1928-1931/).
Attribute the villa to **Le Corbusier and Pierre Jeanneret**: the
[CMN history](https://www.villa-savoye.fr/decouvrir/histoire-de-la-villa-savoye)
identifies Jeanneret's assistance. The foundation identifies a weekend-house
commission, its purist/five-point character, the automobile-related ground-floor
curve, a ramp alongside a stair, later restoration and 2016 World Heritage
inscription. CMN records early leakage and heating difficulties and its present
visitor use. These facts identify an experimental domestic work with a later
conservation history, not a proven contemporary construction system.

**Actually inspected.** P15, Ching's Russian *Архитектура: форма, пространство,
композиция* (2005), building index file page 400/printed 385 points to printed
373; the complete rendered leaf **file 388/printed 373** was viewed, including
its footer. Its small plan/diagram is used only for the central inclined route
within an outer order. The **April 2008 CMN visitor leaflet**, full file pages
1–2, was also visually inspected through its
[CUNY-hosted copy](https://openlab.citytech.cuny.edu/bt2resources/files/2020/01/docvisite_fichier_12B.villa_.savoye.EN_.pdf).
Page 2 labels ground, first and second-floor plans; first-floor rooms lie beside
the terrace, with ramp and stair occupying distinct positions. The FLC page's
Marc Tulane exterior photograph was inspected: a raised horizontal volume,
recessed lower enclosure, slender columns, strip openings and curved roof
screen are visible. No measured section or full construction set was inspected.

The leaflet's statement that listing occurred during the designer's lifetime
conflicts with the foundation's December 1965 date after his death. Use the
foundation for that chronology; do not silently reproduce the older leaflet's
claim. Its plans are visitor diagrams, not current surveyed/as-built evidence.

## Reading the mechanism

**Interpretation.** The important distinction is between the enclosing outer
figure and the route/rooms arranged within it. A frame can give these different
orders, so a room division need not coincide with each external support or
opening. “Free plan” therefore raises a coordination question: which boundaries
can move while support and the main route remain? It does not mean every room
can be combined without affecting privacy, services or access.

Read the ascent together with its intermediate occupied destination. A terrace
beside rooms lets a route meet an outdoor room before reaching the roof; the
route is more than a connection between stacked floors. At each level distinguish
arrival, through-movement and places to pause. The observed ramp/stair pairing
suggests comparing a prolonged sequence with a more direct connection; their
capacities and present inclusive use cannot be inferred from the small plans.

The exterior creates two scales of order: the long wall/opening datum and the
discrete column rhythm. Recessed lower enclosure gives the upper volume an
apparent separation from the ground. An opening at roof level can frame a
landscape without enclosing another whole room. These are perceptual readings
of the inspected material; no exact proportion, support spacing or enclosure
thickness is extracted.

The original weekend use and car arrival explain why a staged domestic journey
was a meaningful design subject. A weekday public service might value short,
repeatable movement differently. Likewise, the reported technical difficulties
require separating formal coherence from tested weather and occupied comfort.
A visually continuous terrace edge still contains parapet, outlet, threshold
and enclosure junctions that another project must resolve afresh.

## Original bounded adaptation: a neighborhood workshop

**Hypothetical brief.** A single-level workshop needs a common making room,
two independently bookable quiet rooms, supervised arrival and a garden on a
level site. A candidate puts all three rooms beyond a ceremonial ramped route
that enters through the making room. Closing the making room then cuts off the
quiet rooms. The failure is an access relationship, regardless of stylistic
quality.

**Revision.** Put arrival, making and quiet-room doors on a common circulation
level. Keep a route beside the making room, with a pause/view bay facing the
garden. Locate the quiet-room branch before the making-room access control.
Use a terrace as an optional destination and a recessed entrance as the first
spatial transition. A later upper room would require its own complete vertical
journey and support/service reservations; it cannot borrow an ornamental ramp
without counting its footprint.

Compare two facade choices on that revised plan. A uniform low strip window
may suit the long making room yet expose quiet-room tables to the public path.
Keep a common head datum but raise or screen the quiet-room lower view zone;
compare that with turning the quiet rooms toward a less public edge. Check
actual seated views, cleaning access and usable wall/storage frontage before
choosing. The resulting difference in openings is a response to use within an
ordered facade, not a loss automatically judged by the historic label.

**Counterexample and check.** Moving a quiet-room door back through the making
room would restore the closure failure even with the revised elevation. Trace
entrance → each quiet room with the making room locked, then deliveries to
storage during making-room use. If either fails, change the route or support
spaces and recount area. Coordinate an actually nonbearing partition change
through [planning and section](../../../../references/planning-and-section.md)
and [frame and core](../../../../references/frame-and-core.md); use the actual
new brief for structure, access, fire and enclosure decisions. No historical
geometry, room dimensions or accessibility claim is transferred.
