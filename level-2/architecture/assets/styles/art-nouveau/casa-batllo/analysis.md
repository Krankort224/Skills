# Casa Batlló: adapting a house through space and craft

**Object:** Casa Batlló, Passeig de Gràcia 43, Barcelona, Catalonia, Spain.
**Architect:** Antoni Gaudí, remodelling **1904–1906** of an **1877** building
by Emili Sala Cortés. This is an extensive transformation of inherited fabric,
not a new freestanding house.
[Operator history](https://www.casabatllo.es/en/antoni-gaudi/casa-batllo/history/),
[Gaudí Council](https://consellantonigaudi.cat/en/buildings/casa-batllo/).

## Classification and historical context

Use **Catalan Modernisme** as the specific context, following the Gaudí Council.
Spain's tourism authority also calls Casa Batlló Art Nouveau. These are compatible
levels of description; the international umbrella does not make Gaudí's work a
French example, nor does “Modernisme” here mean later architectural Modernism.
[Council](https://consellantonigaudi.cat/en/buildings/casa-batllo/),
[Spain tourism authority](https://www.spain.info/en/places-of-interest/casa-batllo/).

The operator records Josep Batlló's purchase in 1903 and the 1904–1906 renovation,
which changed the facade, partitions and lightwell. It places the house among
neighboring, separately designed Modernista renovations on the “Block of
Discord.” The useful historical relationship is therefore an existing urban
building competing for identity along a shared street, not an isolated organic
object with an unconstrained new plan.
[Operator history](https://www.casabatllo.es/en/antoni-gaudi/casa-batllo/history/).

The facade's dragon and marine readings are interpretive associations, not
verified generative rules. The operator itself presents multiple interpretations.
They cannot demonstrate the shape of a hidden room, a structural load path or
an environmental benefit.
[Operator facade](https://www.casabatllo.es/en/antoni-gaudi/casa-batllo/facade/).

**Source descriptions — operator interior account:** A communal entrance leads
to the family's private lobby; the principal salon faces the street and the
private dining room leads to the rear courtyard. The lightwell was enlarged;
its upper tiles are darker and upper windows smaller than those below, with
adjustable wooden ventilation slats. The attic served building residents and
has repeated catenary arches. These statements are textual evidence; no complete
plan, section, lightwell still or opening-operation inspection was obtained.

## Read the mechanisms and their limits

The following interpretations combine those bounded views and descriptions.
They are original design readings, not measured findings or assertions about
Gaudí's undocumented intentions.

| Architectural relationship | Causal reading | Alternative and check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Arrival and territories | A shared entrance and a differentiated private threshold can establish degrees of access within one urban building. Door, rail and wall form can make a transition tangible. | A single open lobby may be more usable for a public program. Trace who controls each door, where visitors wait and how a step-free arrival joins the same destination; the sculptural stair is not a complete access strategy. |
| Inherited depth and light access | An internal void can give a deep building a second internal edge. Altering openings and finishes by level treats the section as differentiated rather than merely repeating one facade indoors. | Shallower rooms or access to a second external edge may avoid a void. Check remaining occupied depth, privacy across the well and cleaning access. Tile color cannot establish daylight sufficiency or air change. |
| Proportion and rhythm | Repeated families can remain recognizable while changing to suit a particular position or use. Repetition can occur in the relationship among frame, opening and infill rather than in identical rectangles. | Standard repeated openings may be enough. Compare clear opening, furniture position and the number of special parts. No universal curve or proportional ratio is measured here. |
| Facade depth and occupation | A gallery changes where a person stands, looks and relates to the street. Its enclosure has spatial depth; the outer pattern and interior outlook need to be evaluated together. | A flat window and shading element may provide the needed view with fewer interfaces. Compare privacy, solar exposure, operability and maintenance from actual occupied positions. |
| Structural expression | The repeated attic arches give a legible sectional rhythm. Their low side zones also remind the designer that an expressive roof can reduce useful headroom near the perimeter. | A regular ceiling over another support system can keep more usable edge space. The photograph does not establish masonry thickness, load paths or capacity; do not derive an arch specification from its silhouette. |
| Finish, fitting and enclosure | Related outlines at touched and viewed elements can connect bodily use with the larger room. A family of details can reinforce spatial identity without requiring a single material everywhere. | A few robust shared details may be preferable to unique fittings throughout. Test grip, cleaning, repair, closing and replacement independently of appearance. |

**Material distinctions:** The operator identifies stone, glass and ceramic on
the front, and cast-iron balcony railings. Those are different construction and
maintenance roles even when they form one visual composition. A modeled surface
cannot establish whether it is a support, lining or finish without additional
evidence. The house's symbolism also does not establish a literal skeletal
structural system.
[Operator facade](https://www.casabatllo.es/en/antoni-gaudi/casa-batllo/facade/).

**Sequence limit:** The textual progression from shared lobby to private lobby,
street salon and rear court suggests a useful contrast between public display,
controlled domestic territory and an inward outdoor destination. It does not
establish exact adjacencies, lengths or that every historical journey followed
today's visit sequence. A route diagram made from these sources would have to
remain relational and explicitly incomplete.

**Counterexample within the case:** The attic's repeated, relatively restrained
arches differ from the elaborate street-facing glazing. A coherent building
does not require identical decoration or the same level of elaboration in every
space. Repeating the front gallery's treatment through service and private rooms
could destroy the very hierarchy the example makes useful.

## A small changed brief

**Hypothetical commission:** Adapt a small two-level urban shell for a daytime
craft workshop, a shared display/teaching room and a quiet consultation room.
Assume a street frontage, rear yard and retained internal support line. There
is no surveyed geometry, confirmed jurisdiction or measured daylight data.
The budget permits one special shared-room assembly, with standard components
elsewhere. This is an original proposal, not a copied house plan.

**Proposal A — a usable family around a shared threshold:** Put the shared
display/teaching room at the street where its activity can address passersby.
Give it one coordinated window, seat and door family, retaining ordinary opening
and replacement parts where possible. Put the noisy workshop toward the yard
with its own goods route; keep the quiet room accessible without crossing it.
Connect the two levels through a legible shared arrival, while reserving a
continuous inclusive journey appropriate to the actual brief. Retain the
support line unless a survey justifies intervention. Do not introduce a curved
plan or internal shaft merely to resemble the precedent.

**Proposal B — inward shared room:** Put the teaching room toward the yard and
use the street room for display/reception. This may protect teaching from
street noise, but weakens direct public visibility and may make guests cross
the workshop's operating territory. Compare the actual access relationships
before choosing; the same craft family can serve either organization.

| Changed condition | Adaptation and discriminating test |
| --- | --- |
| A third independent tenant is added | Give each controlled territory a usable route. Compare a shared entrance with separate entrances; reject A if every visit must pass through teaching or noisy work. A single visual family need not mean one access regime. |
| The shell is deeper than expected | Compare shortening occupied depth, opening toward the yard, and a deliberately sized light space. Test each room and the area sacrificed before choosing a void; do not claim a blue tile gradient solves the problem. |
| A cold climate or exposed location replaces Barcelona | Compare an enclosed shared threshold and a simpler glazing assembly with the proposed viewing bay. Check enclosure continuity, cleaning and condensation questions through the local envelope methods; historic appearance supplies no climatic proof. |
| Funding decreases or the program grows | Retain the shared-room family and simplify repeated private-room components. For growth, repeat identifiable groups with distinct thresholds rather than scaling one intricate opening across a large facade. Quantify special parts and their replacement journeys before claiming affordability. |

The transferable mechanism is **coordinating an existing building's spatial
relationships and a limited family of useful craft elements**. It fails if
custom form consumes room fit, interrupts independent routes, confuses support
with finish or cannot be repaired. A modest standard opening with a well-placed
seat can be a better adaptation than a visually closer but unusable gallery.
