## Identity and evidence

**Object:** Villa Almerico Capra, called La Rotonda, outside Vicenza, Italy;
Andrea Palladio's Renaissance suburban house, continued by Vincenzo Scamozzi.
The [Palladio Museum's Veneto inventory](https://www.palladiomuseum.org/en/veneto),
entry “villa Almerico Capra detta la Rotonda,” separates **design 1566–1567**
from **construction 1567–1605**. The Met's [1570 plate record,
41.100.126-(2.19)](https://www.metmuseum.org/art/collection/search/698054)
attributes the design to Palladio, identifies Scamozzi's completion and
classifies the work as Renaissance. Its **1566–69** shorthand does not describe
every later completion event. Use the museum inventory's phase distinction
rather than a single supposedly exact completion year.

**Actually inspected:** the Met record's introductory account and object
metadata; its [1570 book II, printed page 19 plate](https://collectionapi.metmuseum.org/api/collection/v1/iiif/698054/1504311/main-image),
showing plan and combined elevation/section; the monument site's
[“The project,” “Structure” and “Floors” text](https://www.villalarotonda.it/en/villa/);
its column-base detail and
[oblique aerial photograph](https://www.villalarotonda.it/wp-content/uploads/2024/09/DJI_0029-scaled-e1765375662630-uai-1440x1440.jpg);
and library P15 file **210 / printed 195** and **315 / 300**, La Rotonda plans
and selected accompanying text. Printed numbers were visually checked.

The 1570 plate is an architect's published representation, not an as-built
survey. The modern photographs do not verify internal dimensions, hidden
support or environmental performance. No measured survey, full construction
history, present interior section, monitoring record or on-site visit was
inspected. The book's broad historical captions are checked against the
specialist inventory above.

## Arrangement observed and reported

In the published plate, four projecting porticoes address a square body around
a circular central hall. Axial passages connect fronts to center; room groups
occupy the quadrants, with small stair zones beside the circular boundary.
The combined elevation/section represents a raised principal level above a
lower level, a taller central space and domed termination. These are statements
about that plate; its numbers are not adopted as dimensions for reuse.

P15's selected plans repeat the center-and-quadrants reading but simplify the
representation. Its Renaissance discussion treats relationships between whole
and parts as a compositional ideal. This supports testing related sizes and
alignments, not treating a pleasing diagram as proof of room usability.

The monument text identifies service accommodation below the principal rooms
and describes the projecting fronts as places relating the building to its
surroundings. The inspected aerial view supplies a present exterior comparison;
the close base photograph supplies only surface and profile detail. Neither
establishes that every facade has identical use or exposure.

## Architectural interpretation

The center can be read as a shared reference point; the fronts mediate between
that inner order and several outward directions. Repetition of a front makes
the house legible from changing positions, while its depth gives a place
between exterior ground and interior room. The main room, passage and portico
have different roles despite their alignment. This distinction is more useful
than declaring the whole building simply “symmetrical.”

The raised floor also makes arrival a sectional question: a visible stair and
an elevated outlook are not the same as an inclusive route. This is an issue
for a new design, not a retrospective compliance judgment. Material articulation
can reinforce hierarchy without requiring every surface to be costly cut
stone. The inspected detail alone cannot identify the substrate or assembly
behind all the villa's surfaces.

## Original transfer, failed version and correction

**Hypothetical inputs:** an arts retreat on a slope, one public approach, a
valley view, private bedrooms, deliveries and a constrained budget. The brief
requires access to bedrooms during an event in the shared room. These inputs
are newly authored, not historical evidence.

Version A reproduces four equally prominent approaches around a circular
common room. Two fronts face constrained land, four stair systems consume the
budget, and bedroom journeys cross an event. A neat center has become an
operational bottleneck. The error comes from transferring the diagram before
resolving the new site's edges and the center's occupation.

Version B retains a common reference room, related room groups and deep
thresholds, but gives each edge a role. One principal loggia receives public
arrival; a second faces the valley; a quieter edge serves bedrooms; a separate
service approach follows the available ground. A distribution route beside the
shared room preserves bedroom access during events. The elevations share a
limited set of heads, support rhythms and material transitions while their
openings and sheltered depths differ.

Compare B with a linear terrace following the contour. The central scheme
wins only if shorter internal journeys and a shared identity justify its
groundwork, privacy and roof complexity. Check actual bedroom furniture and
doors, a closed common room, deliveries, an accessible arrival section, privacy
from the public loggia and exposure at each outdoor room. In a hot climate,
test the shaded inhabited edge and ventilation paths; in a cold climate, make
external loggias seasonal and keep an efficient enclosed route. At smaller
scale/budget, retain one useful portico and simplify the center; at institutional
scale, compare several smaller centers before enlarging a single hub.

## Corrections and reuse limits

P15's **1552–1567** caption is not retained as the villa's project chronology.
The monument site's English “no foundations” assertion is also excluded:
its description is not a verified account of ground support and cannot
authorize a foundation-free contemporary building. Similarly, the plate's
proportional organization cannot prove that four equivalent facades suit
unequal solar exposure, topography, access or privacy.

The lesson is to coordinate center, room groups, thresholds and the actual
ground. These new alternatives are not replicas of the source plan. No source
plate, photo, dimensions or extensive text is installed; the Met's plate-specific
public-domain label does not license unrelated book scans or monument photos.
