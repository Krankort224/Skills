# Documentation

## Inputs

Use accepted design decisions, intended design stage, audience, project conventions and required deliverables. Identify which statements are proposed, accepted or unresolved.

## Procedure

1. Select the requested document/drawing content: design explanation, program/area schedule, adjacency, option rationale, material/assembly intent, coordination issues or a deliverable index. Use [design development](../references/design-development.md) to choose the plan/section/elevation information that demonstrates the actual decision; do not prescribe a full drawing suite for a small explanation.
2. Read the relevant [program](../references/program-and-space.md), [performance/coordination](../references/performance-and-coordination.md) and [requirements](../references/requirements.md) sections.
3. Use [design issue coordination](#coordinate-a-design-issue) to reconcile changed identifiers, dimension definitions, option/revision and material intent across the supplied outputs. Identify contradictions before issuing a coordinated explanation.
4. Record applicable requirement sources and separate assumptions, calculated results and design judgments. Select jurisdiction through [requirements](../references/requirements.md#select-a-jurisdiction-module) and its implemented [module](../references/normative/index.md). Distinguish applicable project-documentation scope, working-documentation rules and the task's concept deliverable. Keep project-specific selections with their existing owner; a concept-stage package does not establish construction completeness.
5. State missing specialist inputs and the concrete architectural interfaces to resolve. Use existing project owners and formats rather than introducing a separate process system.
6. If a later explicit modeling task is requested, prepare the accepted design handoff: program, dimensions/coordinates, orientation, material intent, constraints and open issues. Do not invoke a modeling tool or another Level-2 skill automatically.

For Russian project-documentation scope, select the [documentation route](../references/normative/ru/routes.md#documentation) for actual works, stage and dates. A concept narrative, project-documentation composition and working-drawing conventions are distinct decisions; verify the relevant primary provision before claiming a required section set. Carry each selected condition into the changed plan/section/schedule item and identify any unresolved basis.

## Result

Deliver the requested coordinated explanation/schedules or drawing/content requirements, with revision, stage and unresolved interfaces. Use actual requested artifacts; the skill does not mandate a universal folder layout or document suite.

## Coordinate a design issue

Use when an accepted decision is being communicated across text, schedules or
requested drawings. Inputs are the selected design revision, the intended use
of the issue, supplied dimensions and identifiers, and the existing project
sources. A concept issue needs evidence appropriate to its claims; it does not
inherit a complete construction drawing set.

1. Name the decision the recipient must make or implement. Select only the
   views, explanation or schedule needed to convey it in the requested format.
   Separate agreed facts, proposed choices and unresolved specialist inputs.
2. Reuse room, opening, assembly and option identifiers from the project. For
   the changed items, identify where location, dimensions, appearance and
   performance intent are owned. Do not create a second project registry.
3. Choose what is being specified: a supported product, an appearance/material
   intent, or a performance requirement with an identified basis. A performance
   statement needs a defined property, units, conditions and verification
   method; an unknown value remains open. Do not convert an attractive finish
   or a generic material name into a tested assembly claim. Naming a product
   does not establish availability, installation feasibility or compliance.
4. Trace each critical changed item across the supplied representations:
   plan/location → relevant section or explanation → schedule/material note.
   Check identifier, dimension definition, level and revision agreement. A
   door-leaf width and a clear passage are different facts; establish which the
   project actually provides before comparing them.
5. Check the affected interfaces with the relevant design owner: opening and
   access control with escape/access; facade material with junction, drainage
   and maintenance; room changes with ceiling/service reservations. An absent
   detail or specialist confirmation is a named open interface, not a pass.
6. Resolve contradictions against the accepted project sources. Do not assume
   that prose, a specification or the newest-looking drawing automatically
   overrides another owner. Identify the decision needed, update the affected
   representations together and preserve unrelated accepted content.
7. Issue with its stated purpose, revision, precision and open decisions. A
   checked cross-reference is evidence of coordination, not construction
   completeness, product approval or regulatory approval.

**Worked change.** A brief fixes opening D4's width but relocates it from the
public lobby to an adjoining room. The revised plan changes location while the
schedule still names the lobby and the material note describes the old finish.
Reconcile D4's location and finish across those supplied outputs; retain the
fixed width. Check the changed approach and swing against the actual access and
evacuation question before claiming the new arrangement works. Do not resize
the opening or regenerate the entire drawing set to conceal the mismatch.


Use [design development](../references/design-development.md) for the spatial content of the issue and [sources](../references/sources.md#documentation-coordination) only for provenance.
