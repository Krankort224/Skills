# Concept

## Inputs

Use the available brief, site/context, accepted decisions, requested result and the specific alternatives the user needs to explore. Preserve any output format already chosen by the user. Do not require every brief field before independent exploration.

## Select the concept scope

- For a local spatial relationship, compare only that occupied field, boundary,
  access/closure and immediate interface. Use the selected spatial/room methods;
  do not design a whole building, site or drawing set to answer the fragment.
- For an explicitly requested larger concept, connect its program, plan, section,
  outward expression and relevant systems at the declared stage. Operations
  remain selectable; the following procedure is not a compulsory stage chain.

## Procedure

1. State a small set of architectural organizing ideas tied to function and context: arrangement, public/service relationship, arrival, circulation, enclosure, light and outward expression.
2. Use [context and arrival](../references/composition-and-style.md#how-should-the-design-respond-to-site-and-arrival) and [parti comparison](../references/composition-and-style.md#how-can-different-partis-be-generated-and-compared), with the relevant program relationship. When the question is how spaces meet or how movement occupies them, select [spatial relationships](../references/spatial-composition.md#choose-how-spaces-meet) or [path relationship](../references/spatial-composition.md#place-movement-beside-through-or-into-a-space), including its closure/use test. Change organizing relationships between options; test each against the same brief instead of presenting cosmetic variants.
3. Construct the affected part of each option using the selected [room](../references/planning-and-section.md#construct-a-room-that-can-be-used), [plan-family](../references/planning-and-section.md#choose-a-plan-family-from-the-relationships) or [level/section](../references/planning-and-section.md#organize-levels-cores-and-service-depth) method, not only a bubble label. The following whole-building checks apply only to that requested scope; a local relationship uses its immediate boundary and connections. Distribute the program by level, show plausible room bands and independent paths, locate entrance/core/service zones, and reconcile their dimension chains and area allowances. Overlay floor footprints and voids at common coordinates; test upper-floor overlaps with tall lower rooms before describing their roofs. Explain at least one section through occupied space, support and roof/floor depth when the whole-building concept needs it. A textual concept is sufficient when no artifact is requested; identify provisional dimensions.
4. Develop massing, facade openings/solid fields and material expression from those arrangements using the selected composition sections. Choose a plausible [envelope family](../references/envelope-and-assemblies.md) and [structure/services strategy](../references/performance-and-coordination.md#structure-and-services-reservations) where needed to test the concept. Compare function, floor/footprint fit, context and operating modes. Distinguish verified constraints from aspirations; revise a weak arrangement rather than merely listing its risks.
5. Explain stylistic direction through the [character/reference translation method](../references/composition-and-style.md#how-can-a-requested-style-become-design-decisions) and the shared design contract. Verified named cases/history may support a mechanism; do not invent case evidence or replace the spatial decision with a biography or label.
6. Recommend a direction with explicit trade-offs. Preserve accepted decisions; label an unselected idea as an option, not a new baseline.
7. When a specific presentation or artifact is requested, use [presentation](presentation.md) to complete that deliverable and inspect any produced artifact appropriately. Do not add a compulsory presentation step, image or file to an ordinary concept discussion.

## Result

Deliver a compact concept comparison, the reason for the preferred direction, a visual where requested, assumptions and the next design decisions. Do not claim feasibility or compliance that has not been checked.
