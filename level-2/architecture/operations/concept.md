# Concept

## Inputs

Use the available brief, site/context, accepted decisions, requested visual format and the specific alternatives the user needs to explore. Do not require every brief field before independent exploration.

## Procedure

1. State a small set of architectural organizing ideas tied to function and context: arrangement, public/service relationship, arrival, circulation, enclosure, light and outward expression.
2. Read [composition and style](../references/composition-and-style.md) and relevant [program](../references/program-and-space.md) sections. Give alternatives meaningful differences; avoid cosmetic variations presented as different concepts.
3. Express zoning, adjacency, massing intent and facade rhythm through 2D diagrams/elevations or a clearly illustrative raster. Work within known geometry and identify any dimensions still provisional.
4. Compare functional consequences, area/footprint implications, contextual fit, orientation/light opportunities, structure/services reservations and material expression. Distinguish verified constraints from aspirations.
5. Explain stylistic direction through proportion, composition, material/facade decisions and spatial character. Do not route by architects, biographies or historical narratives.
6. Recommend a direction with explicit trade-offs. Preserve accepted decisions; label an unselected idea as an option, not a new baseline.
7. When a visual concept is requested, continue through [presentation](presentation.md) to deliver and inspect the actual selected-format artifact.

## Result

Deliver a compact concept comparison, the reason for the preferred direction, a visual where requested, assumptions and the next design decisions. Do not claim feasibility or compliance that has not been checked.
