# Framing a research question

## Identify the unknown

Formulate one main question and state what knowledge or decision its answer would change. Distinguish investigating unknown behavior from ordinary execution of already defined requirements.

Establish the system, applicability scope, constraints, and available observations. Separate a user requirement from an assumption about how to meet it. Do not require a mechanistic hypothesis when checking operation against specified criteria is sufficient.

## Choose the framing

| Type | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Exploratory | Which regimes, relationships, or explanations merit further investigation |
| Confirmatory | Whether a prespecified prediction agrees with a discriminating observation |
| Engineering | Under what conditions an option satisfies criteria and what limitations arise |
| Diagnostic | Which cause explains observed behavior and which alternatives remain |

The type describes the current operation; it does not assign the entire project to one category.

## Formulate a testable claim

For a hypothesis, state:

- the claim and conditions under which it should hold;
- observable quantities and how they are defined;
- a prediction derived from the hypothesis;
- a result incompatible with that prediction;
- limitations under which the check would be inconclusive.

If a hypothesis has no observable implication yet, keep it untested and find a narrower accessible question. Do not turn a metahypothesis into a mandatory check in every local experiment.

For an engineering question, define operational criteria and permissible trade-offs. Do not introduce a statistical null hypothesis merely to satisfy a form.

## Consider alternatives

Identify material rival explanations, including measurement error, implementation error, numerical artifacts, and omitted factors when applicable. Do not create alternatives to meet a quota.

Prefer a check whose outcomes distinguish explanations. If the expected result occurs under every considered explanation, it cannot select among them.

## Bound the nearest cycle

State the smallest sufficient answer, available resources, stopping grounds, and questions outside the current task. If necessary observations cannot yet be obtained, prepare a definition or data requirements rather than a fictitious experiment.

Preserve the material definition in the adopted project document. Use the corresponding references in the root skill for further literature work, formalization, and testing.
