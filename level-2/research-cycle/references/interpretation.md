# Interpretation and the next iteration

## Recover the claim being tested

Compare the result with the definition, model, prediction, and criteria under which the check was performed. Do not assess an earlier run against a later configuration or rewritten criterion.

Establish evidence quality before drawing a substantive conclusion. If it is insufficient, explain which conclusion is limited and which observation is needed; do not turn an execution failure into falsification of a hypothesis.

## Determine the state of the question

| State | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Supported under tested conditions | Evidence agrees with the prediction and permits a bounded conclusion |
| Partially supported | The result depends on conditions or part of the tested claim remains unsupported |
| Contradicts the prediction | A credible result is incompatible with the claimed implication under the specified conditions |
| Inconclusive | Evidence is insufficient or the check does not distinguish material explanations |

Name the supported claim precisely. Do not extend the state of one run to the entire theory or every regime. If a counterexample to a universal claim is found, state its scope and verify that the assumptions hold.

## Separate observation from explanation

Connect the conclusion to specific data, a table, plot, log, or proof. Separate the observed effect from a hypothesis about its mechanism; list only material remaining alternatives.

For a statistical check, distinguish effect size, uncertainty, and practical significance. For a numerical model, state tested ranges and dependence on computational parameters. For a runtime investigation, identify the version and environment actually tested.

Do not present agreement with one metric, absence of discovered analogues, or an agent's expert-like assessment as proof of a mechanism or novelty. Do not assign a numerical confidence probability without a justified model for obtaining it.

## Choose the next action

Propose the next step based on the remaining unknown:

- close the local question when the answer is sufficient;
- obtain missing data or reproduce a doubtful result;
- test a rival explanation or a regime boundary;
- refine the model or hypothesis with an explicit reason for the change;
- move to a more rigorous model or physical check;
- stop/defer the direction if the nearest check is not justified by resources.

Explain which uncertainty the next step would resolve. Execute new series within the assigned investigation and available budget; do not treat a negative result as grounds to delete history or necessarily change direction.

## Preserve the conclusion within its scope

Distinguish an experimental result, interpretation, project decision, and next configuration. A conclusion from one check does not automatically become a new global model or an accepted engineering decision.

When assigned to update knowledge, use the project's established authority and owners. Preserve the original definition, evidence, and reason for revision; incorporate a durable conclusion with applicability limits into the current document. Keep decision provenance in history.

At completion, state the question's status, material limitations, and the next step or stopping grounds. Evaluate the result and formally accept the task within applicable project rules without conflating them.
