# Related work and external evidence

## Define the search purpose

Define source purpose: mechanism, method, control, claim verification, limitations, or novelty. Translate project terms into established phenomenon/method names.

Choose targeted, landscape, or systematic depth; a full review is not required for every iteration.

## Search by question and mechanism

Query phenomenon, mechanism, geometry, assumptions, and effect; consider synonyms, neighboring disciplines, and citations, beyond project-name matches.

Choose field-appropriate sources; record material search queries, date, coverage, and limits. Counts do not establish completeness.

Select using search results/summaries; verify material claims in original work, documentation, or implementation. Publication existence and bibliographic accuracy differ from claim support.

## Compare rather than merely summarize

For each analogue, record problem, mechanism, conditions, evaluation, result, and limits. Classify its relationship:

- supports the claim under comparable conditions;
- contradicts it under comparable conditions;
- tests a related idea with material differences;
- offers a method or control without testing our hypothesis;
- does not allow a conclusion with the available access.

Distinguish mathematical proof, numerical demonstration, physical experiment, and an author's assertion. Assess which transitions among them require further testing.

## Preserve uncertainty about novelty

Say “not found within the stated coverage,” not “nobody has studied this.” Separate novelty of a combination, problem formulation, mechanism, and implementation. Missing code, publication, or matching terminology does not imply the absence of prior work.

Do not determine patentability or legal status from a general search for analogues; a separate request for that requires the corresponding verification.

## Record the outcome

Record material claims, contradictions, transfer conditions, methods, and gaps with claim/check IDs. Optional root evidence maps suit several claims; one check may need only a source link and precise location.

State implications for the nearest definition/check. Source or agent agreement is not independent proof of the effect.
