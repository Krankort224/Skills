# Framing a research question

## Identify the unknown

State the main question and knowledge/decision affected. Distinguish unknown-behavior investigation from execution of defined requirements.

Define system, applicability, constraints, and observations. Separate requirements from assumed means; checking specified criteria need not require a mechanistic hypothesis.

## Choose the framing

| Type | What to establish |
| --- | --- |
| Exploratory | Which regimes, relationships, or explanations merit further investigation |
| Confirmatory | Whether a prespecified prediction agrees with a discriminating observation |
| Engineering | Under what conditions an option satisfies criteria and what limitations arise |
| Diagnostic | Which cause explains observed behavior and which alternatives remain |

Classify the current operation, not the whole project.

## Formulate a testable claim

For a hypothesis, state:

- the claim and conditions under which it should hold;
- observable quantities and how they are defined;
- a prediction derived from the hypothesis;
- a result incompatible with that prediction;
- limitations under which the check would be inconclusive.

Without observable implications, retain `claim_status: untested` and seek a narrower question. A metahypothesis need not be checked in every local experiment.

For engineering, define criteria and permissible trade-offs; no statistical null hypothesis is required merely for a form.

## Consider alternatives

Identify material rivals: applicable measurement/implementation errors, numerical artifacts, and omitted factors; no quota.

Prefer discriminating outcomes; a result predicted by every rival cannot select among them.

## Bound the nearest cycle

Define sufficient answer, resources, stopping grounds, and excluded questions. If observations are unavailable, prepare a definition or data requirements.

Assign claim/check IDs and preserve the definition in the adopted document. Route further work through root operation links.
